Ever since I was young, I have always been passionate about soccer. Soccer has been a huge part of my life for as long as I can remember. I started playing when I was five years old and I have been playing ever since.

In middle school, I joined the travel team and we practiced every day after school. My coach always told us that hard work pays off and I think that is really true. We worked really hard that season and ended up winning the regional championship. It was a great feeling to know that all of our hard work had paid off.

Then in sophomore year, I tore my ACL during a game. It was really hard for me because soccer was my whole life. I had to sit on the sidelines and watch my team play without me. I felt really sad and frustrated. The doctor told me I would need surgery and six months of physical therapy.

During my recovery, I learned a lot about myself. I learned that I am stronger than I thought I was. Physical therapy was really hard but I pushed through it every day. My physical therapist told me I was one of the hardest workers she had ever seen. I also learned the value of patience because I couldn't rush the healing process.

When I finally came back to the field, everything felt different. I was so happy to be playing again. My teammates were really supportive and helped me get back into the groove. We ended up having a great season and I scored the winning goal in our final game.

This experience taught me that you can overcome anything if you put your mind to it. I learned that hard work, perseverance, and dedication are the keys to success. Soccer has taught me so many life lessons that I will carry with me to college and beyond. I believe that the skills I learned on the soccer field will help me succeed in whatever I do in life.

I am really excited to continue playing soccer in college and I hope to join the club team. I also want to study business because I think the teamwork skills I learned from soccer will help me in the business world. I can't wait to bring my passion and dedication to your campus community.
