# L6 Coaching Quality Rubric

> Gold standard for multi-turn coaching quality. Used to evaluate whether the
> coaching system evolves across turns or degrades into repetition.

---

## Turn-by-Turn Expectations

### Turn 1: "What do you think of my essay overall?"

**Gold standard**: Lead with the single most important architectural observation. For the piano essay, this is the **identity gap** — the essay talks about piano and coding but never reveals who the *person* is beyond their hobbies.

**Must include**:
- At least one direct quote from the essay text
- An honest assessment (this essay is mediocre — it should say so kindly)
- Connection to admissions reality (AO at 4pm won't remember this)
- Phase-appropriate zoom (Foundation → structural, not word-level)

**Must NOT**:
- List 5+ generic suggestions
- Say "Great question!" or "There's a lot to like here"
- Give word-level or sentence-level craft advice (Foundation phase)

**Scoring**:
- 5: Names the core problem (identity gap), quotes text, honest about mediocrity, admissions-grounded
- 4: Names a real structural problem, quotes text, honest
- 3: Identifies issues but stays surface-level ("show don't tell")
- 2: Generic feedback, no text references
- 1: Sycophantic or list-based response

---

### Turn 2: "Tell me more about the opening paragraph"

**Gold standard**: Focus on P1 specifically. The opening claims "my fingers first danced across the piano keys" — a cliche. The coach should identify that P1 fails to earn reader attention in the AO's 3-second window.

**Must include**:
- Specific analysis of P1's structural role (hook/bid for attention)
- Reference to specific sentences or phrases in P1
- Why P1 fails in admissions context (cliche, tells rather than shows)

**Must build on Turn 1**: If Turn 1 discussed the identity gap, Turn 2 should connect P1's weakness TO that gap — "The opening promises a music essay, but you need it to promise a *you* essay."

**Scoring**:
- 5: Deep P1 analysis, connects to Turn 1's observations, specific text references
- 4: Good P1 analysis, some connection to prior turn
- 3: Adequate but could be a standalone response (no conversation evolution)
- 2: Generic "openings should hook" advice
- 1: Repeats Turn 1 observations about the whole essay

---

### Turn 3: "What about the opening paragraph though — is it good enough?"

**CRITICAL ANTI-REPETITION TEST**. The student is returning to a topic already discussed in Turn 2.

**Gold standard**: Acknowledge this is a revisit. Go DEEPER into P1 — cover sentences or dimensions NOT discussed in Turn 2. Example: If Turn 2 covered the cliche issue, Turn 3 might examine the tonal register of P1 vs the rest of the essay, or compare P1's specificity to what P3 achieves with "Chopin's Nocturnes."

**Must include**:
- Something NOT said in Turn 2 about P1
- A new angle, new sentences, or new dimension
- Honest "is it good enough" assessment (no — here's why)

**Must NOT**:
- Rephrase Turn 2's analysis in different words
- Give the same 2-3 points with slightly different phrasing
- Start with "As I mentioned..."

**Scoring**:
- 5: Demonstrably new analysis of P1 (different sentences, different dimension, deeper layer)
- 4: Mostly new, with one overlap point handled well
- 3: Some new material but largely rephrases Turn 2
- 2: Same analysis in different words (anti-repetition failure)
- 1: Copy-paste quality — reads like Turn 2 was never given

---

### Turn 4: "Actually I meant the music-coding parallel to show my versatility"

**REINTERPRETATION TEST**. The student is offering an alternative reading of their own essay.

**Gold standard**: Evaluate whether the essay TEXT actually communicates "versatility." Quote the specific passages (P4: "parallels with another interest: coding", P5: "Practicing scales mirrors debugging code") and assess: does the reader experience versatility, or just a surface analogy? The text says these things are similar — it doesn't show the student being versatile.

**Must include**:
- Honest evaluation: does the text deliver on the student's stated intent?
- Specific text evidence for the gap between intent and execution
- If the intent is valid but execution fails, say WHAT would make it work

**Must NOT**:
- Simply accept "oh that's interesting, let me reconsider"
- Reject the reinterpretation without evidence
- Ignore what the student said and continue previous thread

**Scoring**:
- 5: Quotes specific text, identifies intent-execution gap, suggests what would actually show versatility
- 4: Good evaluation with some text evidence
- 3: Acknowledges reinterpretation but doesn't deeply evaluate against text
- 2: Accepts or rejects without evidence
- 1: Ignores the reinterpretation

---

### Turn 5: "How should I improve paragraph 3?"

**PARAGRAPH-SPECIFIC COACHING TEST**. The coach should use profile-level understanding of P3.

**Gold standard**: P3 is about "reimagining classical pieces" and Chopin. The coach should know P3's structural role (from L3/L4 analysis), what it contributes to the essay's architecture, and give specific improvement advice grounded in that analysis.

**Must include**:
- Specific reference to P3 text ("Reimagining classical pieces by infusing modern elements...")
- P3's structural role in the essay architecture
- Phase-appropriate improvement advice
- Connection to the essay's North Star / through-line

**Must NOT**:
- Give generic paragraph improvement advice
- Ignore the conversation context (4 prior turns of discussion)

**Scoring**:
- 5: Uses deep profile understanding of P3, connects to architecture, specific text + specific alternatives
- 4: Good P3 analysis, some architectural grounding
- 3: Adequate but could be a first-turn analysis (no evolution)
- 2: Generic paragraph advice
- 1: Wrong paragraph or no specific content

---

## Overall Conversation Evolution Score

Evaluate the 5-turn sequence as a whole:

| Criterion | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Progressive depth** | 25% | Does each turn go deeper than the last? |
| **Anti-repetition** | 25% | Turn 3 vs Turn 2: demonstrably different content? |
| **Reinterpretation handling** | 20% | Turn 4: proper evaluation of student's alternative reading? |
| **Conversation memory** | 15% | Do later turns reference/build on earlier discussions? |
| **Specificity** | 15% | Text quotes, specific sentences, not generic advice? |

**Overall score**: weighted average of all criteria.

- 4.5-5.0: Exceptional — feels like talking to a human expert who knows this essay deeply
- 3.5-4.4: Good — clear evolution, occasional repetition or generic moments
- 2.5-3.4: Adequate — some evolution but often feels like independent responses
- 1.5-2.4: Poor — repetitive, generic, or ignoring student input
- 1.0-1.4: Failing — each turn could be a standalone query; no conversation evolution

---

## What Makes Coaching BAD (automatic deductions)

- **Repeating the same observation in different words** (-2 from Turn score)
- **Generic advice ("add more detail", "show don't tell")** without specific text reference (-1)
- **Ignoring what the student actually said** (-2)
- **Sycophantic opening** ("Great question!", "That's really interesting") (-1)
- **Not referencing specific text from the essay** (-1)
- **Giving craft/word-level advice during Foundation phase** (-1)
- **Bullet list of 5+ suggestions** without prioritization (-1)
