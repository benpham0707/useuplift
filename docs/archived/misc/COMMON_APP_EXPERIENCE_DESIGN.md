# 🎓 Common App Workshop - Experience Design
## The Brain Behind the System

**Core Question**: How do we help students write 10-15 college-specific essays that are both authentic AND strategically tailored?

**The Challenge**: Students face cognitive overload:
- 8-12 colleges, each with unique values
- 14+ different essay types
- 200+ words per essay, 2000+ words total
- Need to differentiate while staying authentic
- Deadline pressure

**Our Solution**: Progressive disclosure of intelligence, organized by mental model

---

## 🧠 The Core Mental Models

### Mental Model 1: "Colleges Are Different People"
**Student Thinking**: "Stanford is NOT the same as Harvard. They want different things from me."

**What They Need to Understand**:
- Stanford = The curious explorer (40% Intellectual Vitality)
- Harvard = The community builder (30% Community Contribution)
- MIT = The maker (35% Hands-On Creativity)

**How We Show It**:
```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Think of each college as a person      │
│  with unique priorities                 │
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                         │
│  STANFORD                               │
│  "Show me you learn for love of it,     │
│   not for grades"                       │
│                                         │
│  Cares Most About:                      │
│  🔬 Intellectual Vitality ..... 40%     │
│  💡 Impact & Leadership ....... 25%     │
│  💪 Context & Resilience ...... 20%     │
│  ✨ Authentic Voice ........... 15%     │
│                                         │
│  [See How This Differs From MIT →]     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Key Insight**: Make the weights VISIBLE and VISCERAL. 40% vs 15% should feel different.

- Adds sentence about why genetics fascinates her

- Teaching card appears: "Good! But Impact is low (40/100) - Stanford weighs this at 25%"
- Adds sentence about research outcomes
- Impact score: 40 → 68

**Step 6**: Checks Portfolio Context
- Right panel shows: "You've mentioned debate club in 3 essays"
- "💡 For MIT, consider showcasing your coding project instead"
- Mental note: diversify across colleges

**Step 7**: Compares with Harvard
- Clicks "Compare with Harvard"
- Modal shows Harvard's "Why Us" essay weights Community at 30%
- "Aha! I can't just copy my Stanford essay for Harvard"
- Sees recommendation: "Harvard version should emphasize how you'll contribute to COMMUNITY"

**Step 8**: Finishes Draft
- Word count: 248/250 ✓
- Overall alignment: 76/100
- Status changes to "In Workshop"
- Returns to College View to see progress: 1/3 complete

**Mental State**: Sarah now understands:
- Stanford's unique priorities (40% Intellectual Vitality)
- Essay type requirements (Why Us = research + fit + contribution)
- How Stanford differs from Harvard
- How her draft aligns with Stanford's values
- What specific changes would improve her score
- How this essay fits into her broader portfolio

## 📊 Key Design Principles

### 1. **Progressive Disclosure**
Don't show everything at once. Reveal intelligence when needed:
- **Level 1** (Dashboard): High-level progress
- **Level 2** (College): Deep dive on one school
- **Level 3** (Workshop): Real-time writing feedback
- **Level 4** (Comparison): Strategic differentiation

### 2. **Context-Aware Guidance**
Every piece of feedback must be:
- **College-specific**: "Stanford weighs this at 40%"
- **Type-specific**: "Why Us essays need X"
- **Draft-specific**: "You mentioned Y but missing Z"

### 3. **Quantified Progress**
Make improvement tangible:
- ✗ "This needs work"
- ✓ "Add one curiosity moment to gain 13 points in Intellectual Vitality"

### 4. **Teaching, Not Just Telling**
Every critique includes:
- **Problem**: What's wrong
- **Teaching**: Why it matters + how to fix it
- **Example**: Concrete demonstration
- **Connection**: Why THIS college cares

### 5. **Strategic Intelligence**
Help students see:
- **Individual essays**: How does THIS essay align?
- **College portfolios**: How do my 3 Stanford essays work together?
- **Full portfolio**: Do my 14 essays tell a coherent story?
- **Differentiation**: How do I show different sides to different colleges?

### 6. **Real-Time, Not Batch**
Feedback happens AS they type, not after submission:
- Word count updates live
- Value alignment updates live
- Suggestions appear when relevant
- Portfolio coherence updates when they finish an essay

### 7. **Transparency of Intelligence**
Always show the "why" behind recommendations:
- Source citations (Dean interviews, admission websites)
- Research depth indicators
- Explicit weights (40% vs 15%)
- Clear cause-and-effect (Add X → Gain Y points)

This is the **brain and experience** of the Common App Workshop. It's a teaching system, a strategy tool, and a writing coach - all organized around the student's natural mental models and questions.

The UI should feel like having a wise college counselor sitting next to you, pointing out:
- "See how Stanford is different here?"
- "Notice you're relying on this theme a lot?"
- "Let me show you what 90+ scorers do differently"
- "Here's exactly what would improve your alignment"

All delivered progressively, contextually, and with transparent reasoning. 🎯
