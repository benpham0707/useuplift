# Stage 0: Voice Excavation System

## The Problem We're Solving

**Current State**: Our 3-stage coaching system (Foundation → Development → Refinement) assumes the student's raw draft already has authentic voice and genuine energy. It can polish and refine, but it cannot INJECT spark where none exists.

**The Result**: When students submit bland, safe, metrics-focused drafts, even excellent coaching produces essays that "hit all the points" but lack that magnetic quality that makes admissions readers CARE.

**Evidence**:
- Our Stanford essay scored NQI 92/100 with Authenticity 96%
- Yet it reads like "what the writer thinks Stanford wants to hear"
- Meanwhile, the CMU example essay has grammatical issues, weird specifics, and imperfect structure—but it GRABS you

---

## What Creates "The Spark" - A Spectrum of Authentic Voice

### The Core Insight

"Spark" is not one thing. It's **authentic presence on the page** - the feeling that a real person wrote this, not an essay-writing machine. This presence manifests DIFFERENTLY depending on the emotional register of the essay.

The CMU essay demonstrates ONE type of spark (energetic enthusiasm). But a melancholy essay about loss, a quiet essay about identity, or a contemplative essay about failure each require DIFFERENT spark strategies.

---

## The Spark Spectrum: 6 Emotional Registers

### 1. ENERGETIC ENTHUSIASM (CMU Essay Type)
**When to use**: Intellectual vitality, "why this major," passion projects, activities you love

**Spark Strategies**:
- Unexpected/absurd openings ("The sharks fight over the newest electrical flying rug!")
- Unfiltered naive excitement ("surely one of them will work right?")
- Random specificity that feels real ("SAUCED", "compression trash cans")
- Embarrassing enthusiasm moments ("I screamed 'I got the offer!'")
- Cheesy-but-real callbacks
- Run-on energy, exclamation without apology

**Voice Markers**: Fast rhythm, incomplete thoughts, jumping between ideas, genuine wonder

**Example Opening**: "I have 47 tabs open about medieval siege weapons and honestly? I don't know how I got here."

---

### 2. QUIET INTENSITY (Contemplative/Identity Type)
**When to use**: Personal identity, cultural essays, introspective "who am I" pieces

**Spark Strategies**:
- Still, precise observations that accumulate weight
- Silence and white space (what ISN'T said)
- Single sensory details that carry symbolic weight
- Restraint that makes small moments enormous
- Repetition that builds meaning
- Ending mid-thought or with a question

**Voice Markers**: Shorter sentences, careful word choice, breath between ideas, understatement

**Example Opening**: "My grandmother's hands. That's where I start, always."

---

### 3. MELANCHOLY/LOSS (Grief, Hardship, Failure Type)
**When to use**: Overcoming challenges, personal loss, difficult family situations, failure essays

**Spark Strategies**:
- Haunting specificity (the small detail that breaks your heart)
- Dry humor as defense mechanism
- Unexpected pivot from grief to something mundane
- The moment BEFORE the loss (more powerful than describing the loss itself)
- What you still carry (object, habit, phrase)
- Refusing to tie it up neatly - grief doesn't resolve

**Voice Markers**: Fragments, long pauses (paragraph breaks), present tense for memories, emotional restraint that makes breaks more powerful

**Example Opening**: "The last thing my father taught me was how to check tire pressure. I think about this more than I should."

---

### 4. DEFIANT/IRREVERENT (Against-the-Grain Type)
**When to use**: Challenging assumptions, unconventional paths, disagreeing with expectations

**Spark Strategies**:
- Direct address that challenges the reader
- Uncomfortable truths stated plainly
- Rejecting the "lesson learned" framework
- Humor that has an edge
- Short, punchy sentences that land like punches
- Ending by doubling down, not softening

**Voice Markers**: Blunt vocabulary, rhetorical questions, "you" address, confident fragments

**Example Opening**: "Everyone says I should be grateful. I'm not."

---

### 5. WONDER/CURIOSITY (Discovery Type)
**When to use**: Research interests, intellectual journeys, discovering new perspectives

**Spark Strategies**:
- Questions that lead to more questions
- The moment of confusion before clarity
- Showing the mess of thinking (not just the conclusion)
- Tangents that reveal how the mind works
- Admitting what you still don't understand
- Child-like wonder without irony

**Voice Markers**: Parenthetical asides, "I wondered," conditional language ("what if"), building complexity

**Example Opening**: "The question that ruined my sleep for three weeks: Why do we have two nostrils?"

---

### 6. WARMTH/CONNECTION (Relationship/Community Type)
**When to use**: Community essays, relationship-focused pieces, "who influenced you"

**Spark Strategies**:
- Specific dialogue (actual words someone said)
- Physical details of the person (hands, voice, habits)
- A small ritual or repeated moment
- Inside jokes or private language
- What you learned vs. what they intended to teach
- Showing love through specificity, not declaration

**Voice Markers**: Dialogue, sensory details, present-tense memory, conversational intimacy

**Example Opening**: "'You're holding it wrong.' My grandmother said this about everything - the broom, the knife, my entire life."

---

## Universal Spark Elements (Across All Registers)

While strategies differ, these elements create spark in ANY emotional register:

### 1. SPECIFICITY OVER ABSTRACTION
- ❌ "I learned to appreciate my heritage"
- ✅ "I learned to make lumpia on a Tuesday when my mom was too tired to argue about the wrapper thickness"

### 2. EARNED MOMENTS (NOT PERFORMED)
- ❌ Vulnerability placed strategically for effect
- ✅ Vulnerability that slips out because the story required it

### 3. THE UNEXPECTED TURN
- Something the reader doesn't see coming
- A juxtaposition that creates meaning
- An ending that goes somewhere new (not circular)

### 4. PRESENCE OF A REAL MIND
- Contradictions and complications
- Unresolved questions
- Admission of what you don't know
- Thinking on the page, not conclusions

### 5. SENSORY GROUNDING
- At least one moment we can SEE/HEAR/FEEL
- Physical world anchors abstract ideas
- Body in space (not just thoughts floating)

### 6. VOICE CONSISTENCY
- The word choices match across the essay
- The rhythm feels like ONE person
- The level of formality stays consistent
- No code-switching into "essay mode"

### 7. SOMETHING AT STAKE
- We understand why this matters NOW
- There's tension, even if quiet
- Something could be lost or gained

## The Spark Gap: What's Missing in "Essay Mode"

Regardless of register, "essay mode" writing lacks:

| Missing Element | Essay Mode Version | Authentic Version |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Specificity | "I learned a lot" | "I learned that MSG isn't actually bad for you and felt betrayed by my entire childhood" |
| Stakes | "This was meaningful" | "I still check if she's breathing" |
| Imperfection | "This experience taught me" | "I'm not sure what this taught me, honestly" |
| Surprise | Predictable arc | Unexpected detail or turn |
| Voice | Generic formal | Distinct rhythm and vocabulary |
| Embodiment | Abstract reflection | "My hands were shaking" |

## The Voice Excavation Process

### Philosophy: Mining, Not Manufacturing

We cannot CREATE authentic voice—it must come from the student. Stage 0 is an **excavation** process that helps students dig out what's already there but buried under "college essay" expectations.

## Step 0A: Raw Input Collection

### Three Input Channels

#### 1. The Draft (Required)
Whatever the student has written—could be:
- A polished "essay voice" draft
- Bullet points
- Stream of consciousness
- Even "I don't know what to write"

#### 2. Voice Sample (Highly Recommended)
Ask the student: "Text your best friend about this topic. Don't think, just type."

**Why This Works**:
- Texting voice is authentic
- Removes "essay mode" entirely
- Reveals real personality and rhythm

**Example Prompt**:
> "Pretend you're texting your closest friend about the thing you want to write about. Don't worry about grammar or complete sentences. Just... tell them about it like you would in real life. What would you say?"

#### 3. Quick Interview (If Needed)
5-8 rapid-fire questions designed to bypass the "essay brain":
- "What's the weirdest detail about this that you've never told anyone?"
- "If you had to explain this to a 5-year-old, what would you say?"
- "What's the part that makes you cringe or feel embarrassed?"
- "What would your best friend say about why you love this?"
- "Finish this: 'The moment I knew I was weird about this was when...'"

## Step 0B: Spark Gap Analysis

### The Essay Mode Detector

Analyze the draft for signs of "performed" vs "genuine" voice:

**Essay Mode Red Flags** (Indicates Spark Gap):
- Generic transitions ("Furthermore", "In conclusion")
- Abstract claims without specific moments
- Vocabulary that sounds "impressive" not natural
- Perfect 5-paragraph structure
- Everything ties together too neatly
- Name-drops the college in a calculated way
- Uses the prompt's language back at the prompt

**Genuine Voice Green Flags** (Spark Present):
- Unexpected word choices
- Sentence fragments or non-standard grammar that feels intentional
- Self-deprecating humor
- Specific details that seem randomly included
- Moments of slight awkwardness or cringe
- Enthusiasm without ironic distance
- Internal contradictions (wanting conflicting things)

### Spark Score: 0-100

| Score Range | Meaning | Action Needed |
|-------------|---------|---------------|
| 0-30 | **Full Essay Mode**: Draft is entirely performed voice | Full excavation needed |
| 31-50 | **Mostly Essay Mode**: Some buried authentic moments | Targeted excavation on weak areas |
| 51-70 | **Mixed Voice**: Authentic sections mixed with essay mode | Amplify authentic, strip performed |
| 71-85 | **Mostly Authentic**: Voice is there, needs slight tuning | Light excavation, proceed to Stage 1 |
| 86-100 | **Fully Authentic**: Genuine spark throughout | Skip Stage 0, proceed directly |

### Buried Spark Detection

Even in "essay mode" drafts, look for:
- One sentence that sounds different from the rest
- A detail that's oddly specific (why did they include THAT?)
- A moment where structure breaks down (they forgot to be formal)
- A parenthetical aside that reveals real thought

**These are excavation starting points.**

## Step 0C: Deep Interview (The Mining Process)

### Interview Types Based on Spark Gap

#### For Spark Score 0-30: Full Excavation

**The "Before the Essay" Questions**:
1. "Before you started writing, what was the first thing that popped into your head about this topic? Not the 'right' answer—the actual first thing."
2. "What did you cut from your draft because it felt too weird or personal?"
3. "If I read your essay out loud at a party, what would your friends think? 'That sounds like you' or 'That sounds like a college essay'?"

**The "Specific Moment" Questions**:
4. "Tell me about a specific moment—like a snapshot—that captures why this matters to you. What time of day was it? What were you wearing?"
5. "What's the version of this story you'd tell at 2 AM to someone you trust?"
6. "What part of this makes you smile in a weird, private way?"

**The "Voice Recovery" Questions**:
7. "How would you explain this to someone who thinks it's stupid?"
8. "What's the part that sounds impressive but honestly isn't the real reason you care?"
9. "Complete this: 'I know it's weird but...'"

#### For Spark Score 31-50: Targeted Excavation

Focus on the buried authentic moments:
- "This line here sounds different from the rest: [quote]. Tell me more about that."
- "You mentioned [specific detail]. Why that specifically? There must be a story."
- "The rest of your essay sounds very polished, but this part doesn't. That's actually good. What were you thinking when you wrote it?"

#### For Spark Score 51-70: Amplification

- "These parts sound like YOU [point to authentic sections]. Why did you switch to a different voice in these other parts?"
- "What would happen if the whole essay sounded like this section?"
- "What are you holding back because you think it's 'too much'?"

## Step 0D: Voice-First Draft Generation

### The Anti-Essay Draft

After excavation, generate a draft that PRIORITIZES voice over:
- Perfect structure
- Complete sentences
- Seamless transitions
- Tied-together endings

### Generation Principles

1. **Start with the most interesting moment**, not the "logical beginning"
2. **Include 2-3 "risky" details** that feel slightly too specific or personal
3. **Leave at least one thing imperfect**—a sentence fragment, an unclear reference, an unresolved tension
4. **Use the student's actual vocabulary** from the interview, not synonyms
5. **Preserve awkward but authentic phrasing** from the voice sample
6. **Don't name-drop the college** unless it comes up naturally
7. **End somewhere interesting**, not with a conclusion

### The "Would They Say This?" Test

For every sentence, ask: "Would this specific student actually say this in conversation?"
- If no → Remove or rewrite using their actual words
- If yes → Keep, even if imperfect

## Type Definitions

### New Types for Stage 0

```typescript
// ============================================================================
// EMOTIONAL REGISTER TYPES

/**
 * The 6 emotional registers that determine spark strategy
 */
type EmotionalRegister =
  | 'energetic_enthusiasm'   // Intellectual vitality, passion, "why this major"
  | 'quiet_intensity'        // Identity, cultural, introspective
  | 'melancholy_loss'        // Challenges, grief, failure
  | 'defiant_irreverent'     // Against-the-grain, unconventional
  | 'wonder_curiosity'       // Discovery, research, intellectual journeys
  | 'warmth_connection';     // Relationships, community, influence

/**
 * Register detection result
 */
interface RegisterDetection {
  primary: EmotionalRegister;
  secondary?: EmotionalRegister;
  confidence: number;                    // 0-100
  reasoning: string;
  avoid?: EmotionalRegister[];           // Registers that would be mismatched
}

/**
 * Register-specific voice characteristics
 */
interface RegisterVoiceProfile {
  register: EmotionalRegister;

  // Voice qualities to capture
  voiceQualities: string[];

  // Structural choices that match this register
  structuralChoices: string[];

  // Example rhythms (sentence patterns)
  exampleRhythms: string[];

  // Green flags for this register
  greenFlags: string[];

  // Red flags for this register
  redFlags: string[];
}

// STAGE 0 INPUT TYPES
// ============================================================================

/**
 * All inputs for voice excavation
 */
interface VoiceExcavationInput {
  rawDraft: string;                      // Whatever student has written

  interviewResponses?: InterviewResponse[]; // Answers to excavation questions
  essayPrompt: string;                   // The essay prompt

 * A student's response to an excavation question

interface InterviewResponse {
  questionId: string;
  question: string;
  response: string;
  voiceMarkers: string[];               // Phrases that sound authentic
  emotionalEnergy: 'high' | 'medium' | 'low';  // How engaged they seem

// SPARK GAP ANALYSIS TYPES
// ============================================================================

/**
 * Complete spark gap analysis result
 */
interface SparkGapAnalysis {
  // Overall spark assessment
  sparkScore: number;                   // 0-100
  sparkLevel: 'full_essay_mode' | 'mostly_essay' | 'mixed' | 'mostly_authentic' | 'authentic';

  // Register detection
  detectedRegister: RegisterDetection;
  registerFit: number;                   // 1-10: how well current voice matches needed register

  // Universal issues (apply to all registers)
  universalIssues: {
    essayModeIndicators: {
      indicator: string;
      quote: string;
      location: string;
      severity: 'minor' | 'moderate' | 'severe';
      howToFix: string;
    }[];

    missingElements: {
      element: 'specificity' | 'stakes' | 'surprise' | 'embodiment' | 'voice_consistency';
      description: string;
      howToExcavate: string;
    }[];
  };

  // Register-specific issues
  registerIssues: {
    register: EmotionalRegister;
    missingQualities: string[];          // What this register needs that's missing
    wrongTone: string[];                 // Elements that don't match this register
    recommendations: string[];
  };

  // Buried authentic moments found
  buriedSpark: {
    quote: string;
    location: string;
    whyAuthentic: string;
    register: EmotionalRegister;         // What register this moment belongs to
    amplificationPotential: 'high' | 'medium' | 'low';
  }[];

  // Section-by-section breakdown
  sectionAnalysis: {
    text: string;
    verdict: 'authentic' | 'essay_mode' | 'mixed';
    registerMatch: boolean;
    explanation: string;
  }[];

  // Recommendations
  recommendation: 'skip_to_stage_1' | 'light_excavation' | 'full_excavation';
  recommendedQuestions: ExcavationQuestion[];
}

// ============================================================================
// EXCAVATION QUESTION TYPES

/**
 * An excavation question designed to surface authentic voice
 */
interface ExcavationQuestion {
  id: string;
  question: string;

  // What this question is trying to do
  purpose:
    | 'recover_voice'        // Get them out of essay mode
    | 'find_specifics'       // Get concrete moments
    | 'uncover_stakes'       // Find what's at risk
    | 'amplify_spark'        // Expand on authentic moment
    | 'find_quirks'          // Surface unique voice patterns
    | 'access_emotion';      // Access genuine feeling

  // Which register this question serves
  targetRegister: EmotionalRegister;

  // What buried spark or issue this targets
  targetedAt?: string;

  // Excavation potential (how likely to produce gold)
  potential: 'high' | 'medium' | 'low';
}

    specificMomentCount: number;
    sensoryDetailCount: number;
```

```
## UNIVERSAL ESSAY MODE RED FLAGS (All Registers)
- Generic transitions ("Furthermore", "In conclusion", "This experience taught me")
- Abstract claims without specific moments or sensory grounding
- Vocabulary that sounds "impressive" not natural to this person
- Perfect structure with clear thesis and conclusion
- Name-drops the college in a calculated way
- Uses the prompt's language back at the prompt
- Every point ties together too neatly
- Missing embodiment (no physical/sensory details)
- No stakes (nothing at risk, nothing to lose)
- Performed vulnerability (placed strategically, not earned)

## UNIVERSAL AUTHENTIC VOICE GREEN FLAGS (All Registers)
- Unexpected word choices that feel specific to this person
- Sentence rhythms that feel human (fragments, long sentences, breath)
- Specific details that seem randomly included because they're REAL
- At least one moment of surprise (unexpected turn, detail, ending)
- Contradictions or complications (real people aren't resolved)
- Sensory grounding (we can see/hear/feel something)
- Something at stake (tension, even if quiet)

## REGISTER-SPECIFIC ANALYSIS

Depending on the detected register, also check for:

### If ENERGETIC ENTHUSIASM:
Green flags: Naive wonder, run-on energy, embarrassing excitement, absurd specificity
Red flags: Forced enthusiasm, claiming passion without evidence, too polished

### If QUIET INTENSITY:
Green flags: Precise observations, meaningful white space, restraint, symbolic details
Red flags: Over-explaining, too many words, missing the weight of silence

### If MELANCHOLY/LOSS:
Green flags: Haunting specificity, dry humor, unresolved grief, small carrying objects
Red flags: Melodrama, forced silver lining, "I learned from this" resolution

### If DEFIANT/IRREVERENT:
Green flags: Direct challenge, uncomfortable truths, edge, doubling down
Red flags: Trying too hard to be edgy, softening at the end, fake rebellion

### If WONDER/CURIOSITY:
Green flags: Questions leading to questions, visible thinking, admitted confusion
Red flags: Just reporting conclusions, no mess, too certain

### If WARMTH/CONNECTION:
Green flags: Specific dialogue, physical details of person, rituals, inside references
Red flags: Declaring love instead of showing it, generic relationship description
```

```
## UNIVERSAL EXCAVATION RULES (All Registers)

1. NEVER ask "Why is this important to you?" (too essay-like)
2. NEVER ask for the "lesson" or "what you learned"
3. ALWAYS ask for specific moments (who, what, where, when)
4. ALWAYS include at least one question about what they CUT or held back
5. ALWAYS ask how someone else (friend, parent, sibling) would describe this
6. Build on any buried spark found - make them expand those authentic moments

## REGISTER-SPECIFIC QUESTION BANKS

### For ENERGETIC ENTHUSIASM essays:
- "What's the most embarrassing thing about how much you care about this?"
- "If you had to explain this to someone who thinks it's stupid, what would you say?"
- "What's the weirdest rabbit hole you've gone down related to this?"
- "Tell me about a time you got SO excited about this that you annoyed someone."
- "What would your best friend say about your obsession with this?"
- "Finish this: 'I know it's nerdy but...'"
- "What's something about this that makes you laugh?"
- "What did you cut from your draft because it felt TOO enthusiastic?"

### For QUIET INTENSITY essays:
- "If you could only show me one image that captures this, what would I see?"
- "What's the smallest detail that carries the most weight for you?"
- "What do you notice that other people miss?"
- "Describe a moment of complete stillness related to this."
- "What's something you've never said out loud about this?"
- "What would be lost if you tried to explain this to someone who doesn't get it?"
- "What's a texture, smell, or sound connected to this?"
- "What do you keep coming back to, even when you try to move on?"

### For MELANCHOLY/LOSS essays:
- "What's a small, random detail you can't stop remembering?"
- "What's something mundane that now reminds you of this?"
- "What would you want to say to someone who says 'it's for the best'?"
- "What's something funny or absurd about this situation? (Grief is weird.)"
- "What's something you still haven't figured out how to feel?"
- "What's something you still carry with you—a phrase, an object, a habit?"
- "What's the thing people always say that doesn't help at all?"
- "What happened right BEFORE the hard part? What were you thinking about?"

### For DEFIANT/IRREVERENT essays:
- "What's the thing everyone assumes about you that's wrong?"
- "What's something you refuse to apologize for?"
- "What's the most uncomfortable truth you've realized?"
- "What would you say if you knew no one would judge you?"
- "What's the part of this that makes people uncomfortable?"
- "Who told you to be different and how did you respond?"
- "What's the thing you're NOT going to change, no matter what?"
- "What's the honest version of this that you thought you couldn't write?"

### For WONDER/CURIOSITY essays:
- "What's a question that keeps you up at night?"
- "Walk me through how your brain works when you're figuring something out."
- "What's something that seemed simple until you looked closer?"
- "What's a tangent you went down that seemed unrelated but actually mattered?"
- "What's something you still don't understand and it bothers you?"
- "What's the messiest part of how you think about this?"
- "When did you first realize this was more complicated than you thought?"
- "What question would you ask if you could ask anyone anything about this?"

### For WARMTH/CONNECTION essays:
- "Tell me exactly what they would say about this—their actual words, in their voice."
- "What's a weird little ritual or habit between you?"
- "What's something they do that used to annoy you but now you'd miss?"
- "What's an inside joke or private reference only you would understand?"
- "Describe their hands, or their laugh, or how they stand."
- "What's something they taught you that they didn't mean to teach you?"
- "What's a small moment that was actually a big moment?"
- "What would they say if they read your essay?"
```

```
## REGISTER-SPECIFIC INSTRUCTIONS

### For ENERGETIC ENTHUSIASM:
Voice qualities to capture:
- Fast rhythm, sentences that run into each other
- Incomplete thoughts, tangents, parenthetical asides
- Genuine wonder without ironic distance
- Embarrassing levels of excitement
- The feeling of someone who can't contain themselves

Structural choices:
- Start in the middle of action or thought
- Let one idea tumble into the next
- Include at least one "I know this is weird but..."
- End with energy, not reflection

Example rhythm: "And then—okay this is the part where it gets actually insane—I realized the whole thing was upside down. Like, LITERALLY upside down. Three weeks of work. I should have been mad but honestly? I just started laughing."

### For QUIET INTENSITY:
Voice qualities to capture:
- Precision over speed
- Weight in small observations
- Breath between thoughts (use paragraph breaks)
- Understatement that makes moments larger
- The feeling of someone choosing each word carefully

Structural choices:
- Shorter paragraphs, more white space
- One image that carries symbolic weight
- Restraint—leave things unsaid
- End with a question or incompleteness

Example rhythm: "My grandmother's hands. Flour under the nails, always. Even at the hospital."

### For MELANCHOLY/LOSS:
Voice qualities to capture:
- Haunting specificity (the small detail that breaks your heart)
- Dry humor as defense mechanism
- Present tense for memories (makes them alive)
- Emotional restraint—breaks hit harder
- The feeling of carrying something heavy

Structural choices:
- Unexpected pivot from grief to something mundane
- Focus on the moment BEFORE the loss
- Include what you still carry (object, phrase, habit)
- DO NOT resolve. Grief doesn't tie up neatly.

Example rhythm: "The last thing he said was 'don't forget the milk.' I think about this every time I open the refrigerator."

### For DEFIANT/IRREVERENT:
Voice qualities to capture:
- Blunt, direct address
- Short sentences that land like punches
- Comfortable with discomfort
- Edge without performing edge
- The feeling of someone who won't back down

Structural choices:
- Start with a challenge or uncomfortable truth
- Use "you" address when appropriate
- Don't soften at the end—double down
- Let some anger or frustration show

Example rhythm: "Everyone said I'd regret it. They were wrong. I'd do it again."

### For WONDER/CURIOSITY:
Voice qualities to capture:
- Questions that lead to more questions
- Visible thinking process (not just conclusions)
- Comfortable admitting confusion
- Tangents that reveal how the mind works
- The feeling of someone genuinely puzzled

Structural choices:
- Show the mess of thinking, not just the answer
- Include at least one "I still don't understand..."
- Let curiosity pull the narrative forward
- End with an open question, not a resolution

Example rhythm: "Here's what doesn't make sense to me. If the cells regenerate every seven years, then technically I'm not the same person who started this project. So who's finishing it?"

### For WARMTH/CONNECTION:
Voice qualities to capture:
- Specific dialogue in their actual voice
- Physical details of the person (hands, laugh, stance)
- Private moments and inside references
- Love shown through specificity, not declaration
- The feeling of intimacy with the reader

Structural choices:
- Ground in specific scenes with this person
- Include at least one thing they actually said
- Show a small ritual or repeated moment
- End with presence, not lesson

Example rhythm: "'You're doing it wrong.' She said this about everything—the dishes, the folding, my whole approach to problems. Now I catch myself saying it too."
```

## The Key Insight

**We're not writing the essay for the student. We're excavating the essay that's already inside them but buried under "college essay" expectations.**

The CMU essay works not because the student is a better writer—it works because they forgot to be "a college essay writer" and just... talked. Our job is to help students forget they're writing a college essay.
