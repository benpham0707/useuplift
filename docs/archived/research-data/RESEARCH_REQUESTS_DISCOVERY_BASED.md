# Discovery-Based Research Requests for College Essay Evaluation
## Evidence-First Methodology (No Confirmation Bias)

**Date Created**: December 3, 2025
**Methodology**: Discovery-first, not confirmation-based
**Purpose**: Let evidence determine what each college values, rather than confirming pre-existing assumptions

---

## CRITICAL INSTRUCTIONS FOR AI RESEARCH TOOLS

### What NOT to Do ❌
- Do NOT search for specific traits I've pre-specified (e.g., "Does Dartmouth emphasize tight-knit community?")
- Do NOT filter results to confirm assumptions
- Do NOT ignore evidence that contradicts expected patterns

### What TO Do ✅
- Search BROADLY for what each college values in applicants
- Include ALL themes that emerge from sources (even unexpected ones)
- Report ACTUAL frequency/emphasis of themes across sources
- Let the data speak for itself

---

## REQUIRED OUTPUT STRUCTURE

For each college, provide:

```markdown
## [College Name] - Discovery Research Results

### STEP 1: Source Collection (No Filtering)

**Source 1: Common Data Set Section C7**
- URL: [direct link]
- Year: [2023-2024 or most recent]
- Complete C7 Table: [All factors with ratings]
- **Analysis**: Which 3-5 factors are rated "Very Important"?

**Source 2: Institutional Mission/Philosophy**
- URL: [official college mission page]
- Full Mission Statement: "[EXACT QUOTE]"
- **Analysis**: What 3-5 themes appear most frequently? (Count mentions)

**Source 3: Admissions Officer Statements**
- Person: [Dean/Director name and title]
- Source: [Interview, blog, video, book]
- URL/Citation: [link or book details]
- Date: [when published]
- All Quotes: "[Include ALL substantive quotes, not just those matching assumptions]"
- **Analysis**: What qualities/traits are mentioned most often?

**Source 4: Expert Analysis**
- Author: [Name and credentials]
- Title: [Book or article]
- Publisher/Publication: [where published]
- Year: [publication date]
- ISBN/URL: [if available]
- Relevant Excerpts: "[ALL passages about this college's priorities]"
- **Analysis**: What does expert say distinguishes this college?

**Source 5: Comparative Data**
- Comparison: [This college vs. 2-3 named peers]
- Data Source: [CDS comparison, mission comparison, expert comparison]
- **Analysis**: How does this college differ from peers in measurable ways?

### STEP 2: Theme Analysis (Evidence-Based)

**Frequency Analysis**: Count how many sources mention each theme
- Theme 1: [Name] - Mentioned in [X/5] sources
- Theme 2: [Name] - Mentioned in [X/5] sources
- Theme 3: [Name] - Mentioned in [X/5] sources
- Theme 4: [Name] - Mentioned in [X/5] sources
- Theme 5: [Name] - Mentioned in [X/5] sources

**Intensity Analysis**: For each theme, rate emphasis level
- High Emphasis (explicit, repeated, central to mission): [list themes]
- Moderate Emphasis (mentioned, but not primary): [list themes]
- Low Emphasis (mentioned once or briefly): [list themes]

### STEP 3: Unexpected Findings
**Report anything that contradicts common assumptions about this college**
- Example: "Expected to find X, but sources actually emphasize Y"
- Contradictions between different source types
- Gaps (themes you expected but didn't find)

### STEP 4: Recommended Dimensional Weights
**Based ONLY on evidence gathered, suggest 3-6 evaluation dimensions with weights**
- Dimension 1: [Name] - [Weight %] - Evidence: [which sources, how strong]
- Dimension 2: [Name] - [Weight %] - Evidence: [which sources, how strong]
- [etc., totaling 100%]

**Justification**: Explain how you derived these dimensions from evidence, NOT assumptions
```

## SUCCESS CRITERIA

For each college, the discovery research should produce:

1. **Clear Theme Hierarchy**: 3-6 themes ranked by evidence strength (not assumption)
2. **Quantified Support**: Number of sources supporting each theme
3. **Unexpected Insights**: At least 1 finding that surprises or contradicts expectations
4. **Data-Driven Weights**: Recommended dimension weights justified by evidence frequency/intensity
5. **Honest Gaps**: Acknowledgment of themes that AREN'T strongly supported despite expectations

## EXAMPLE: What Discovery-Based Results Should Look Like

### Good Discovery Research (Evidence-First) ✅

```markdown
## Example College - Discovery Results

**Theme Analysis**:
1. "Intellectual curiosity" - 5/5 sources (very strong)
   - CDS: Essay "Very Important"
   - Mission: Mentioned 8 times
   - Dean: Used in 3 of 4 quotes
   - Expert: "Primary selection criterion"
   - Comparative: Rates higher than peers

2. "Community contribution" - 3/5 sources (moderate)
   - Mission: Mentioned 3 times
   - Dean: 1 quote references
   - Comparative: Similar to peers
   - NOT in CDS top priorities
   - Expert: Mentions but doesn't emphasize

3. "Leadership" - 2/5 sources (weak)
   - Mission: Mentioned once
   - Dean: Not mentioned
   - Not prominent in CDS
   - Expert: "Less important than other schools"

**Unexpected Finding**: Despite assumption that this college values leadership, evidence shows it's mentioned far less than intellectual curiosity and community contribution.

**Recommended Weights** (Based on Evidence):
- Intellectual curiosity: 35% (strongest evidence)
- Community contribution: 20% (moderate evidence)
- [other dimensions based on actual findings]
```

### Bad Confirmation-Biased Research (What to Avoid) ❌

```markdown
## Example College - Biased Results

**Theme Analysis**:
1. "Leadership" - Found sources confirming leadership is important ✅
   - [cherry-picked quotes supporting leadership]

**Problem**: Researcher ASSUMED leadership was important, searched only for confirmation, ignored that other themes appeared MORE frequently in sources.
```
