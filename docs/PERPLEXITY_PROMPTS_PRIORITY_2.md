## PROMPT 1: Show Don't Tell - The Master Technique

```
I need comprehensive research on the "show don't tell" writing technique specifically for college admissions essays. This is for building an AI essay coaching system.

Please find and provide:

1. EXPERT DEFINITIONS
- How do professional writing coaches define "showing" vs "telling" in personal narratives?
- What's the difference between showing emotion and naming emotion?
- What does "scene vs. summary" mean in essay context?

2. TRANSFORMATION TECHNIQUES
- Specific techniques for converting "telling" statements into "showing" scenes
- Before/after examples: "I learned resilience" → [concrete scene version]
- Before/after examples: "I'm passionate about science" → [concrete scene version]
- Before/after examples: "This experience changed me" → [concrete scene version]

3. SENSORY DETAIL GUIDANCE
- Which senses are most effective in college essays?
- How much sensory detail is too much vs. too little?
- Research on why sensory details create reader empathy

4. SHORT-FORM SHOWING (Critical for 150-300 word essays)
- How do you "show" in only 150 words?
- What's the minimum viable scene?
- Techniques for implying rather than stating

5. ADMISSIONS OFFICER PERSPECTIVES
- Direct quotes from admissions officers about what "showing" looks like
- What do AOs say about essays that "tell" too much?
- How do readers recognize authentic scenes vs. manufactured ones?

6. COMMON MISTAKES
- Most common "telling" phrases in college essays
- Why students default to telling (psychology)
- How to catch yourself telling vs. showing

7. ADVANCED TECHNIQUES
- How to show internal change through external action
- Showing character through dialogue
- Showing growth without stating "I grew"
- The "iceberg theory" applied to college essays

Please cite specific sources: admissions officers, writing coaches, published guides, and research studies where available.
```
