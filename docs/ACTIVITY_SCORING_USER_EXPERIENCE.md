**Formula:** Total = (Tier × 0.30) + (Recognition × 0.25) + (Leadership × 0.125) + (Community × 0.15) + (Commitment × 0.175)

*For solo activities (individual research, personal projects, etc.), leadership weight redistributes to other components.*

### Craft Teaching: Description Excellence

Based on patterns in your portfolio, here are the key craft elements to master:

---

#### Craft Lesson 1: Active Voice Command

**The Principle:**
Active voice ("I created") signals ownership. Passive voice ("was created") signals observation. Admissions officers read thousands of passive descriptions—active voice stands out.

**Why It Matters:**
Passive language subconsciously signals "I was there but didn't drive this." Even if you led the project, passive voice makes you sound like a bystander.

**From Your Portfolio:**

| Weak | Strong | Explanation |
|------|--------|-------------|
| "Research was conducted on..." | "Built neural network that..." | You did it—say so |
| "Events were organized" | "Organized events that..." | Own the action |
| "Program was started" | "Started program serving..." | You started it |

**Student-Specific Fixes:**

| Activity | Current | Improved |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Research | "Developed neural network models" | "Built neural network that improved..." |
| Environmental | "Recycling program achieving..." | "Built recycling program that cut..." |
| NHS | "Participated in tutoring program" | "Created tutoring curriculum serving..." |

**Tips:**
- Start descriptions with action verbs: Built, Created, Founded, Launched, Designed
- If "was" appears in your description, rewrite
- Your subject should be "I" (implied) not "it" or "the program"

---

#### Craft Lesson 2: The Quantification Hierarchy

**The Principle:**
Not all numbers are equally powerful. The hierarchy: Outcomes > Scale > Time

**Why It Matters:**
"Twice per month" quantifies your time (least impressive). "15 students served" quantifies scale (better). "3 students qualified for AMC" quantifies outcome (best).

**From Your Portfolio:**

| Level | Example | Impact |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **Outcome (Best)** | "5,000+ students using platform" | Proves real-world validation |
| **Scale (Good)** | "Led team of 12 volunteers" | Shows scope of operation |
| **Time (Weak)** | "Tutoring twice per month" | Quantifies minimal commitment |

**Student-Specific Fixes:**

| Activity | Current | Improved |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Research | No quantification | "Improved detection 23%, found 12 objects" |
| Environmental | "30% waste reduction" | "8,000 lbs annually, cut 30% (2,400 lbs)" |
| NHS | "Twice per month" | Remove or replace with "15 students weekly" |

**Tips:**
- Lead with outcome numbers when possible
- Convert percentages to concrete units
- If you only have time numbers, add scale or outcome numbers
- Bad quantification (like "twice per month") is worse than no quantification

---

#### Craft Lesson 3: The "So What" Sentence

**The Principle:**
Every description needs one sentence that answers: "Why should I care?" This is usually your impact or discovery.

**Why It Matters:**
Admissions officers read descriptions fast. They remember activities that have a clear "so what." They forget activities that only describe tasks.

**From Your Portfolio:**

| Activity | Has "So What"? | The Hook |
|----------|---------------|----------|
| USAMO | Yes | "5,000+ students using platform" |
| Debate | Yes | "8 novice-to-varsity conversions" |
| Research | No | Missing discovery/finding |
| Environmental | Partial | "30%" but missing context |
| NHS | No | Nothing distinctive |

**How to Add "So What":**

- Research: "...identifying 12 previously missed objects" ← discovery
- Environmental: "...dashboard now used by administration" ← lasting system
- NHS: "...3 qualifying for AMC this year" ← measurable outcome

**Tips:**
- Write the "so what" sentence first, then build description around it
- If you struggle to articulate the "so what," dig deeper into what you actually did — there's always an angle
- Test: Can someone repeat your activity's impact after reading once?
