## Overview and Key Findings

### Executive Summary

Elite universities employ **proprietary rating methodologies** that differ dramatically from each other. Understanding these systems is critical because **personal rating is MORE predictive of admission than academic rating** at most schools. The rating system is where holistic review becomes quantified—and where the threshold effect (3→4 drops from 20-40% to 2%) operates.

### Critical Findings

| Finding | Source | Implication |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| Personal rating > Academic rating in predictive power | SFFA v. Harvard data | Character and fit matter most post-threshold |
| MIT uses REVERSED scale (5=best) | Solomon Admissions | Cross-school analysis requires careful translation |
| Stanford separates IV from academics | Stanford Admissions | 4.0 students can get low IV ratings |
| Rating 3→4 = 20-40% to 2% probability | SFFA litigation | One rating level is life-changing |
| Fewer than 50 students got personal 1 over 6 years | Harvard data | "Outstanding" is nearly impossible |

## Harvard's Six-Category 1-6 System

### The Six Rating Categories

| Category | Scale | What It Measures | Rating 1 Definition |
|----------|-------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Academic** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Grades, test scores, rigor, rank | Top 1-2% nationally, 99th percentile SAT |
| **Extracurricular** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Quality and depth of activities | Extensive achievement in specialized domain |
| **Personal** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Character, leadership, interpersonal | **Most predictive of admission** |
| **Recommendations** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Teacher/counselor letter strength | Exceptional third-party validation |
| **Athletic** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Competition level | Division I potential (recruited athletes) |
| **Interview** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Alumni interview report | Outstanding connection |

**Source:** [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2018/6/16/harvard-admissions-behind-the-scenes/), SFFA v. Harvard litigation

### Rating Scale Definitions (Personal Rating)

| Rating | Definition | Frequency |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| **1** | "Outstanding" | Fewer than 50 over 6 years |
| **2** | "Very strong" | Rare but achievable |
| **3** | "Generally positive" | Common among competitive applicants |
| **4** | "Bland or somewhat negative or immature" | **Effectively denial territory** |
| **5** | "Questionable personal qualities" | Serious concern |
| **6** | "Worrisome personal qualities" | Near-certain rejection |

**Source:** [SFFA v. Harvard findings](https://www.harvard.edu/admissionscase/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2021/06/2019-10-30_dkt_672_findings_of_fact_and_conclusions_of_law.pdf)

### Academic and Extracurricular Rating Statistics

> "The academic rating is strongly correlated with admission to Harvard; **more than 82% of admitted students had a 1 or a 2**."

> "The extracurricular rating is strongly correlated with admission to Harvard; **more than 69% of admitted students had a 1 or a 2**."

> "Asian Americans are stronger than white applicants on the extracurricular rating. More than 28% of Asian Americans received a 1 or a 2, as compared to 24% of white applicants."

**Source:** [Harvard Crimson Court Filings Analysis](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2018/6/19/asian-american-academics/)

### What Harvard Evaluates for Personal Rating

The personal rating captures:
- Integrity
- Helpfulness
- Courage
- Kindness
- Fortitude
- Empathy
- Self-confidence
- Leadership ability
- Maturity
- Grit
- Humor
- Sensitivity

**Source:** [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2018/6/16/harvard-admissions-behind-the-scenes/)

### Plus/Minus Modifiers

Harvard uses +/- modifiers (e.g., "2+" or "3-") to indicate:
- **Plus (+)**: Slightly stronger than numerical score
- **Minus (-)**: Slightly weaker than numerical score

These modifiers flag borderline cases for closer discussion.

## Yale's Two-Dimensional Grid System

### The Two Dimensions

| Dimension | Scale | What It Measures |
|-----------|-------|------------------|
| **Academic Rating** | 1-4 (1=highest) | Grades, scores, rigor, "intense love of learning" |
| **Personal Rating** | 1-4 (1=highest) | Specialized achievements, leadership, personal qualities |

### How the Grid Works

1. **Plot applicants**: Each gets Academic (1-4) and Personal (1-4) ratings
2. **Draw cutoff line**: "Zigzag line" position varies by pool strength and capacity
3. **Sort by line**: Above = presumed admit, Below = presumed deny
4. **Discussion zone**: Area around the line where committee deliberation matters

### The Zigzag Cutoff Line

> "Students in the upper left corner [high Academic, high Personal] are seen as the most prepared and likely to succeed."

**CRITICAL:** The line is NOT fixed year-to-year—it shifts based on:
- Applicant pool strength
- Available capacity
- Institutional priorities that year

### Yale Voting Threshold

> "Any applicant with more than a total of two reject and/or wait-list votes was automatically denied."

**Source:** Ivy College Prep research on Yale admissions

## Stanford's Intellectual Vitality Separation

### Stanford's Three Rating Dimensions

| Dimension | Scale | What It Measures |
|-----------|-------|------------------|
| **Academic** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Class rank, rigor, test scores, recommendations |
| **Extracurricular** | 1-6 (1=highest) | Depth of achievement in specialized domains |
| **Intellectual Vitality (IV)** | 1-6 (1=highest) | "Commitment, dedication, and genuine interest in expanding intellectual horizons" |

### Why IV is Separate

> "Unlike the Ivies, Stanford uses a separate rating specifically for intellectual vitality. **Many Stanford applicants who earn the highest academic ratings (a coveted '1' on the Academic scale) rate low on the Intellectual Vitality scale** because they love competing for grades more than learning for learning's sake."
> — [NovaScholar](https://www.novascholar.org/blog-posts/insights-on-intellectual-vitality-from-a-former-stanford-admissions-officer)

### The IV Weeding Function

> "The Stanford intellectual vitality essay is used **to weed out countless 4.0 students who lack a true love of learning**."
> — [Solomon Admissions](https://www.solomonadmissions.com/stanford-university-admissions)

> "This essay can be seen as 'the never-ending dinner conversation essay'—if you can't talk endlessly about an intellectual idea that excites you, then you will struggle with this essay."

### How IV is Assessed

**Dr. Irena Smith, former Stanford admissions officer:**

> "Intellectual vitality must **ooze from the file**."

IV can manifest through:
- Teacher recommendations showing students asking creative questions
- Personal statements revealing genuine intellectual passions
- Lists of self-directed readings and projects pursued independently
- Evidence of learning for learning's sake (not just grade achievement)

**Source:** [NovaScholar](https://www.novascholar.org/blog-posts/insights-on-intellectual-vitality-from-a-former-stanford-admissions-officer)

### What Stanford Looks For (Richard Shaw)

> "We want to hear a 'voice'—that's a critical component."
> — **Richard Shaw**, Stanford Dean of Admission (2005-2023)

> "Shaw estimates that **80 percent of the applicants are capable of handling Stanford's academic load**. This reveals why academic excellence alone isn't enough—Stanford must distinguish among thousands of brilliant, capable students."
> — [Stanford GSE News](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/we-re-looking-good-match-stanford-s-dean-admissions-getting-college)

## MIT's Reversed 1-5 Scale

### MIT's Three Rating Categories

| Dimension | Scale | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| **Academic** | 1-5 (**5=best**) | STEM-specific GPA weighted |
| **Activity** | 1-5 (**5=best**) | EC vs co-curricular balance |
| **Interpersonal** | 1-5 (**5=best**) | "Hardest category to get 4-5 in" |

**CRITICAL WARNING:** MIT's reversed scale (5=best, 1=worst) is a trap for cross-school analysis!

### Rating Distribution and Competitiveness

| Profile | Competitiveness | Admission Likelihood |
|---------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **5-5-5** | Rare (<1% of pool) | Almost always admitted with good essays |
| **5-4-4** | Highly competitive | Strong admit chances |
| **4-5-5** | Highly competitive | Often admitted over 5-4-4s |
| **4-4-4** | Borderline | Rare admits, must be exceptional in one area |

**Source:** [Solomon Admissions MIT Guide](https://solomonadmissions.com/mit-admissions)

### MIT's Interpersonal Rating Focus

> "Unlike the Ivies, MIT has a separate rating for interpersonal, which measures an applicant's potential contribution to the community."

> "MIT is a difficult place where you are going to encounter a lot of setbacks, and the admissions committee needs to know that you have already faced a setback in some area of your life and gotten past it."

> "Do not say things you think the admissions reader wants to hear. That is the easiest way to get a 2 rating under interpersonal."
> — [Solomon Admissions](https://solomonadmissions.com/mit-admissions)

### MIT Specialization Preference

> "Activity-wise, MIT looks for specialists who have high-level achievement in one or two things over students who are well-rounded."

> "Candidates should show their intellectual aptitude in pursuit of something STEM-related yet also pursuing something completely outside of STEM at a high-achievement level will result in a high rating."

## Personal Rating Deep Dive

### Why Personal Rating Matters Most

From the SFFA v. Harvard statistical analysis:

> "**Personal ratings prove MORE PREDICTIVE of admission than academic ratings** when controlling for other factors."

> "An applicant with top personal rating and moderate academic rating (1 personal, 3 academic) achieves HIGHER admission rates than the inverse profile."

**This contradicts the public narrative that academics are paramount.**

**Source:** [SFFA litigation documents](https://studentsforfairadmissions.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Doc-620-SFFA-Proposed-FF-and-CL-redacted.pdf)

### Historical Significance of Personal Rating

From 1969 Harvard Crimson research:

> "An outsider, given only the computer data and asked to guess which applicants the committee will accept, will come close if he **chooses those with the highest personal ratings**, tossing in about 100 students who are extraordinarily superior academically or athletically."

> "If this outsider relies instead primarily on the 'academic' rating, he is likely to make a lot more wrong guesses than right ones."

**Source:** [Harvard Crimson 1969](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1969/9/18/admissions-personal-rating-is-crucial-pin/)

### Personal Rating Component Traits (Harvard)

| Trait | Definition | Evidence Sources |
|-------|------------|------------------|
| **Likability** | "Very attractive person to be with" in residential community | Recommendations, interview |
| **Courage** | Risk-taking, resilience, willingness to challenge | Essays, activities |
| **Kindness** | Empathy, leadership that elevates peers | Recommendations |
| **Being Widely Respected** | Peer recognition, mentor respect | Recommendations |
| **Positive Personality** | Demeanor, humor, authenticity | Interview, essays |

### Achieving a Personal Rating of 1

> "Over the course of six years of admissions at Harvard, **fewer than 50 students achieved a 1 personal rating** (with no other 1s)."

Requirements for Personal Rating 1:
- "Student may display enormous courage in the face of seemingly insurmountable obstacles in life"
- "Student receives unqualified and unwavering support from their recommenders"

**Source:** [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2018/6/16/harvard-admissions-behind-the-scenes/)

### Red Flags That DAMAGE Personal Ratings

| Red Flag | Impact | Evidence Source |
|----------|--------|-----------------|
| Academic dishonesty | Near-certain rejection | School report |
| Ghostwritten/AI essays | Entire application authenticity questioned | Writing analysis |
| Inconsistent information | Credibility concern | Cross-document check |
| Disciplinary history | Poor judgment signal | School report |
| Problematic social media | Hate speech, illegal activity | Digital footprint |
| Negative essay tone | Blame, complaints, entitlement | Essay review |
| Unprofessional communication | Pushy emails, demanding treatment | AO interactions |
| Rec letter contradictions | Honesty/self-awareness concern | Recommendation comparison |

## Rating-to-Outcome Probability

### Harvard SFFA Data: Quantified Admission Probability

| Overall Rating | Admission Probability | Interpretation |
|----------------|----------------------|----------------|
| **1** | 90-98% | "Exceptional—clear admit with very strong support" |
| **2** | 50-90% | "Strong credentials but not quite tops" |
| **3** | 20-40% | "Solid contenders" |
| **4** | **~2%** | "Neutral—respectable credentials" (effectively denial) |
| **5-6** | <1% | Functionally rejection |

**Source:** SFFA v. Harvard litigation (2014-2019 actual admissions data)

### The Primacy of Personal Ratings (Statistical)

From the trial:

> "The battle over the significance of the personal rating in explaining Harvard's admissions outcomes was the locus of the trial's thorniest statistical wrangling."

Statistical measures:
- Pseudo R-Squared of personal ratings regression: **0.28**
- Pseudo R-Squared of academic rating regression: **0.56** (nearly double)

However, this doesn't mean academics are more important—it means academic ratings are more predictable from objective data, while personal ratings add unique information.

## The Threshold Effect

### The Critical 3→4 Drop

> "The difference between a '3' and a '4' represents a drop from **20-40% admission to 2% admission**—a catastrophic shift based on a single rating level."

### The Cutoff Model

This suggests admissions operates on a **CUTOFF MODEL**:
- **Above threshold** → Discussed, potentially admitted
- **Below threshold** → Proceeds almost automatically to rejection

### Implications for Applicants

| Rating Zone | Strategic Implication |
|-------------|----------------------|
| 1-2 range | Focus on fit and narrative coherence |
| 3 range | Every element must push toward 2 |
| 3-4 boundary | **CRITICAL ZONE** - small improvements have massive impact |
| 4+ range | Major weakness likely needs to be addressed |

```typescript
// School-Specific Rating System
interface SchoolRatingSystem {

    modifiers: boolean;
  };
  dimensions: RatingDimension[];

  gridConfig?: GridDecisionConfig;
}

interface RatingDimension {
  name: DimensionType;
  scale: string;
  weight: 'threshold' | 'differentiator' | 'high' | 'medium' | 'lower';

  | 'personal'
  | 'intellectual_vitality'
  | 'interpersonal'
  | 'recommendations'

  | 'interview'
  | 'overall';

interface GridDecisionConfig {
  xAxis: DimensionType;
  yAxis: DimensionType;

    type: 'zigzag' | 'straight';
    variableBy: ('pool_strength' | 'capacity' | 'institutional_priorities')[];
  };
  discussionZone: {
    widthFromLine: number;
  };
}

// Admission Probability Model
interface AdmissionProbabilityModel {
  ratingLevel: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6;

    dropFrom: number;
    dropTo: number;
    description: string;
  };
}

// Personal Rating Assessment
interface PersonalRatingAssessment {

    grit?: TraitScore;
    maturity?: TraitScore;
  };

  predictedRating: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6;
  confidence: number;
}

interface TraitScore {
  score: number; // 1-6
  evidence: string[];
  source: ('essay' | 'recommendation' | 'interview' | 'activities')[];
}

interface PersonalRatingRedFlag {
  type: 'dishonesty' | 'ghostwritten' | 'inconsistency' | 'discipline' |
        'social_media' | 'negative_tone' | 'unprofessional' | 'rec_contradiction';
```
