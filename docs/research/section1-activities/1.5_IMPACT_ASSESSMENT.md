## Overview

Impact assessment determines how achievements are weighted in admissions evaluation. The level of impact (local, regional, state, national, international) significantly affects application strength.

### Key Takeaways

- **Tier system:** National (Tier 1) down to Local (Tier 4) with point values
- **Key insight:** Outsized local impact can equal national achievement
- **Verification reality:** Selective verification focuses on most impressive OR suspicious claims
- **Credibility matters:** Quantified, verifiable claims weighted more than vague superlatives
- **Context modifier:** Under-resourced students' local impact may be viewed MORE favorably than elite school national impact

> "The greater impact the claim has on your potential as an applicant, the more likely schools will do some fact-checking"
> — Sara Harberson

## Sara Harberson's Impact Tier System

Former Penn/Franklin & Marshall admissions officer's point system:

| Tier | Points | Level | Examples | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|----------|-------|
| **Tier 1** | 4 | National | USAMO, Regeneron ISEF, National DECA 1st, Jack Kent Cooke | Gold standard |
| **Tier 2** | 3 | State | State orchestra, DECA/FBLA state leadership, State science fair | "Sweet spot for many applicants" |
| **Tier 3** | 2 | Regional | County science fair, District championships, Regional competitions | Solid achievement |
| **Tier 4** | 1 | Local/School | Club member, Local volunteer, Town sports league | Baseline participation |

### How This System Works

The tier system provides rough guidance for evaluating achievement level:
- Activities span multiple tiers based on specific achievement
- Same activity can be Tier 1 (national winner) or Tier 4 (participant)
- Context modifies tier assessment (resource constraints elevate tiers)

## Differential Evaluation by Level

### School-Level Impact (Tier 4 baseline)

**Generally baseline unless exceptionally transformative:**
- "President of club" alone signals little
- Strong example: "Founded new club that grew to 50+ members with lasting program"

**What elevates school-level:**
- Measurable growth or change
- Lasting infrastructure (program continues after you leave)
- Recognized by administration
- Impact on significant portion of student body

### Local Community Impact (Tier 3-2)

**KEY:** AOs value OUTSIZED LOCAL IMPACT similarly to national achievement

**What's "outsized":**
- Lasting infrastructure
- Real problems solved
- Measurable beneficiaries
- Sustained over time

**Strong example:** "Weekly tutoring serving 75 underserved students, sustained 3 years"

### State/Regional Impact (Tier 2)

"Sweet spot for many applicants"

**Why this matters:**
- Achievable without extraordinary resources
- Demonstrates genuine competition and merit
- Validates talent beyond school context

**Proven outcomes:** State DECA → Columbia, Cornell, UC Berkeley, Johns Hopkins

### National/International Impact (Tier 1)

Gold standard BUT "unhooked admissions still sub 10%"

**Important:**
- Not sufficient alone for admission
- Must be combined with strong overall application
- Highest verification scrutiny

### Contextual Modifier

> "A student creating regional impact from an under-resourced rural school may be viewed more favorably than similar impact from elite private school"

This is critical: achievement is evaluated RELATIVE to opportunity.

## Verification Methods

### How Schools Verify Claims

| Method | Description | Frequency |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Recommendation Cross-Reference** | Teachers/counselors confirm activities | Common |
| **Internal Consistency Check** | Feasibility of claimed involvement | Always |
| **Feasibility Analysis** | Time audits (4-8 hrs optimal, 25 max credible) | Always |
| **Direct Follow-Up** | Contact supervisors/coaches | Rare but happens |
| **Social Media** | 28% of AOs check (2023), 67% think it's "fair game" | Increasing |

### The Verification Reality

> "Contacted over 50 coaches/advisors. Not a single one was asked to verify student claims."
> — M-A Chronicle investigation

**Implication:** Verification is SELECTIVE - focuses on:
1. Most impressive claims (Tier 1 achievements)
2. Most suspicious claims (implausible timelines, scale)
3. Red-flagged applications

### When Verification Happens

- After admission offers (pre-enrollment)
- When claims seem implausible
- For recruited athletes (increased post-Varsity Blues)
- Random audits at some institutions

## Credibility Spectrum

### Highly Credible

- Third-party validation (competition results, publications, media)
- Specific quantification with plausible scale
- Sustained trajectory (200 hours over 3 years vs. 200 hours senior year)
- Corroboration in recommendations

### Moderate Credibility (Needs Strong Description)

- Self-initiated projects (need clear reach, outcomes, challenges)
- School leadership (need more than title)
- Community service (must specify beneficiaries, time, evolution)

### Low Credibility (Red Flags)

- Vague superlatives ("made huge impact")
- Implausible scale ("founded national organization" without online presence)
- Timing inconsistencies
- Honor/title inflation ("CEO" without evidence)

## Quantitative Claims Evaluation

### Effective Patterns

These patterns demonstrate credible impact:

| Claim | Why It Works |
|-------|--------------|
| "Raised $3,000 for animal shelter through bake sale and social media" | Specific amount, method, cause |
| "Tutored 15 students weekly for 2 years, 80% showed improvement" | Numbers, duration, outcome |
| "Grew membership from 12 to 47 over two years" | Before/after, timeline |
| "Organized conference with 120 students from 8 schools" | Scale, scope, specifics |

### Problematic Patterns

| Claim | Problem |
|-------|---------|
| "Raised $50,000" without explanation | Extraordinary without validation |
| "Served exactly 534 community members" | False precision |
| "30 hrs/week" while maintaining academic excellence | Hours inflation |
| "Changed 10,000 lives" | Unmeasurable, grandiose |

### Exaggeration Data

- 40-50% of applications have some exaggeration
- BUT: "Risk-reward usually against you" - unverified claims don't matter much anyway
- Fabrication (vs. embellishment) can result in rescission

## Counselor's Role in Verification

### Structural Limitation

**1:300+ ratio common at public schools**

This means counselors cannot verify everything and must prioritize.

### What Counselors CAN Verify

- School-sanctioned activities
- Official positions, athletics
- Context about school resources
- Flag major discrepancies

### What Counselors CANNOT Verify

- External activities
- Summer employment
- Non-school competitions
- Family circumstances (often taken at face value)

### Post-Varsity Blues Effect (2019+)

- Increased caution in admissions
- 2020 UC audit: 64 improperly admitted students found
- Focus areas: Athletic recruitment, major donor connections
- More verification of exceptional claims

## School-Specific Information

### Harvard

- Uses internal 1-6 rating system
- Rating 1-2 requires verifiable exceptional achievement
- Cross-references with recommendations
- High scrutiny for "perfect" applications

### Stanford

- Values "intellectual vitality" over pure achievement level
- Contextual evaluation of impact
- Less emphasis on national awards if local impact is exceptional

### MIT

- 4-activity limit means each activity scrutinized more
- Values Makers Portfolio with tangible evidence
- Research claims often verified through faculty

### Ivy League Generally

- Post-SFFA: More emphasis on demonstrated impact
- Contextual evaluation within school resources
- Geographic diversity consideration in impact assessment

```typescript
interface ImpactAssessment {
  activity: string;
  impactLevel: 'local' | 'school' | 'regional' | 'state' | 'national' | 'international';
  tierRating: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4;
  pointValue: number;

  quantification: {
    metric: string;
    value: number;
    unit: string;
    timeframe: string;
  };

  verification: {
    thirdPartyValidation: boolean;
    recommendationCorroboration: boolean;
    onlinePresence: boolean;
    documentationAvailable: boolean;
  };

  credibilityScore: number; // 0-100
  contextModifier: number; // Multiplier based on resource constraints
}

interface VerificationAssessment {
  claimType: 'achievement' | 'hours' | 'impact' | 'leadership';
  claimMagnitude: 'routine' | 'notable' | 'exceptional' | 'extraordinary';
  verificationLikelihood: 'unlikely' | 'possible' | 'likely' | 'certain';

  redFlags: {
    implausibleScale: boolean;
    timingInconsistency: boolean;
    noCorroboration: boolean;
    vagueClaims: boolean;
  };

  recommendation: 'proceed' | 'document_better' | 'reduce_claim' | 'remove';
}

interface ImpactTierSystem {
  tier1: {
    level: 'national/international';
    points: 4;
    examples: string[];
    verificationLevel: 'high';
  };
  tier2: {
    level: 'state';
    points: 3;
    examples: string[];
    verificationLevel: 'moderate';
  };
  tier3: {
    level: 'regional';
    points: 2;
    examples: string[];
    verificationLevel: 'low';
  };
  tier4: {
    level: 'local/school';
    points: 1;
    examples: string[];
    verificationLevel: 'minimal';
  };
}

interface QuantitativeClaimEvaluation {
  claim: string;

  assessment: {
    specificity: 'vague' | 'general' | 'specific' | 'precise';
    plausibility: 'implausible' | 'questionable' | 'credible' | 'highly_credible';
    documentation: 'none' | 'self_reported' | 'partial' | 'full';
  };

  recommendation: {
    include: boolean;
    modifications: string[];
    verificationNeeded: boolean;
  };
}
```
