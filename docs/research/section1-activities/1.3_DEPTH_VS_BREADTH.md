## Overview

Elite admissions has shifted from valuing "well-rounded students" to building "well-rounded classes" composed of individuals with demonstrated depth in focused areas.

### Key Takeaways

- **Depth beats breadth:** Progressive leadership trajectory is the strongest depth signal
- **Quantified impact matters:** Officers expect measurable outcomes, not vague participation
- **Thematic coherence:** Breadth is valuable when activities collectively tell a story
- **Local impact = national impact:** Sustained local service can equal or exceed prestigious national activities
- **2024-25 trend:** Universities prioritizing "depth, initiative, and impact over those who simply check boxes"

> "Universities are prioritizing applicants who show depth, initiative, and impact over those who simply check boxes."
> — 2024-25 Early Admissions Research

## Depth Signals Taxonomy

### 1. Progressive Leadership Trajectory

**The most compelling depth signal - cannot be replicated by resume padding.**

| Year | Example Progression | Signal Strength |
|------|---------------------|-----------------|
| 9th | Member | Baseline |
| 10th | Coordinator/Committee | Growth |
| 11th | Co-Captain/VP | Significant |
| 12th | President/Captain | Peak |

**Harvard Rating 2 exemplars:** "class president, newspaper editor" - roles requiring years to attain

**Implementation:** Track and reward leadership progression over time, not just final titles.

### 2. Quantifiable Impact Metrics

Admissions officers expect **measurable outcomes** demonstrating contribution beyond participation.

| Impact Type | Weak Example | Strong Example |
|-------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Fundraising** | "Volunteered for charity" | "Raised $15,000 for refugee relief through school fundraiser" |
| **Service** | "Tutored students" | "Tutored 25 students over 3 years, 18 showed grade improvements" |
| **Growth** | "Led a club" | "Grew membership from 12 to 47; established mentorship program for 15+ freshmen" |
| **Competition** | "On debate team" | "Ranked top 5 LD debaters in state; coached 6 novices to regional semifinals" |

**Implementation:** Reward quantification, flag vague claims.

### 3. Specialized Expertise Development

Long-term involvement enables skills single-year participants cannot achieve.

- MIT: "intellectual vitality and authentic engagement"
- Students develop "problem-solving skills through various challenges"
- "Witness the full cycle of organizational challenges"

**Implementation:** Extra weight for 3+ year involvement in single domain.

## Impact Metrics Quantification

### Fundraising Thresholds

| Amount | Assessment | Context |
|--------|------------|---------|
| <$500 | Entry level | Shows effort, minimal differentiation |
| $500-$2,000 | Solid | Good for school-level initiatives |
| $2,000-$5,000 | Strong | Meaningful community impact |
| $5,000-$10,000 | Very impressive | Demonstrates serious organization |
| $10,000-$25,000 | Exceptional | College-level achievement |
| $25,000-$50,000 | Rare | Top-tier spike level |
| $50,000+ | Extraordinary | "No college expects a student to accomplish" |

> "Raising $50,000 is a crazy amount that no college expects a student to accomplish."
> — CollegeVine

### Impact Metrics by Activity Type

| Activity | Moderate | Strong | Exceptional |
|----------|----------|--------|-------------|
| **Volunteer hours** | 50-100 | 100-250 | 250+ |
| **People served** | 50-100 | 100-500 | 500+ |
| **Students tutored** | 3-5 | 6-15 | 15+ |
| **Members managed** | 10-20 | 20-50 | 50+ |
| **Budget managed** | $100-500 | $500-2,000 | $2,000+ |
| **Events organized** | 2-5 | 5-10 | 10+ |
| **Followers (content)** | 100-500 | 500-5,000 | 5,000+ |

### Quality Over Quantity Principle

> "It's not really about hitting a magic number. Colleges notice impact more than dollars—how many people you've helped, the changes you've made, and your dedication over time."

### Impact Evidence Hierarchy

1. **Sustained change** (program running after you left)
2. **Measurable outcomes** (grades improved, policies changed)
3. **Scale** (people/resources affected)
4. **Recognition** (awards, media)
5. **Duration** (years committed)

## Breadth Signals Taxonomy

### 1. Thematic Coherence

Valuable breadth = activities that **collectively tell a story**

**Coherent Breadth Examples:**
- **STEM Focus:** Robotics captain + coding instructor + accessibility app developer
- **Social Justice:** Debate + Model UN + petition platform founder + legal aid volunteer

> "Students with multiple interests should seek to define a common theme between all their interests that will unite their application."
> — InGenius Prep

**Implementation:** Detect and reward thematic patterns across activities.

### 2. The 2-4-6 Approach (UC Admissions Experts)

| Category | Count | Description |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| **Primary** | 2 | Leadership/depth activities (3+ years, significant time) |
| **Secondary** | 4 | Consistent involvement (2+ years, moderate time) |
| **Supporting** | 6 | Meaningful pursuits that round out profile |

This structure permits demonstrating multiple competencies without appearing scattered.

### 3. Four-Domain Framework (Empowerly)

| Domain | Purpose | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Academic-oriented** | Demonstrates intellectual passion | Research, academic clubs, subject competitions |
| **Leadership** | Shows initiative and people skills | Student government, team captain, organization founder |
| **Community Service** | Indicates responsibility and empathy | Sustained volunteering, tutoring, community organizing |
| **Personality** | Humanizes the application | Arts, sports, hobbies that reveal character |

**Implementation:** Check domain coverage, flag missing domains as potential gaps.

## Scattered Involvement Red Flags

| Red Flag | Pattern | Detection Method |
|----------|---------|------------------|
| **Chronological Inconsistency** | 1-2 semesters across 8+ organizations | Count activities with <1 year involvement |
| **Absence of Leadership** | Many memberships, no progression | Check for any leadership roles |
| **Generic Descriptions** | Can't articulate specific contributions | Analyze description specificity |
| **Thematic Incoherence** | Random assortment | Detect lack of connecting narrative |

### The "Checklist Problem"

"Checklist-style" profiles trigger automatic skepticism:
- 10+ activities with minimal leadership depth
- Generic descriptions like "Helped organize events" without specifics
- Prestige name-dropping without substantive takeaways
- Implausible hours (20+ weekly across multiple activities)

> "Admissions officers assess whether a student has made a meaningful difference in their activities. Simply being a member of multiple clubs without taking on leadership roles or driving initiatives does little to strengthen an application."
> — Rise Global Education

## Local Impact Equivalence

### Core Principle

> "You don't need to go across the world. What's important is that you find what you are passionate about. Go with your passion whether it's local, regional, national or international."
> — Vincent Ilustre, Executive Director, Tulane University Center for Public Service

### Local vs. International Service Comparison

| Factor | Local Service | International "Voluntourism" |
|--------|---------------|------------------------------|
| **Authenticity** | Often more genuine | Can appear "bought" or resume-driven |
| **Sustained impact** | Easier to maintain over years | Usually one-time or short-term |
| **Verifiability** | Easily confirmed by local contacts | Harder to validate claims |
| **Depth** | Can develop deep relationships | Often surface-level interaction |
| **Cost** | Usually free or low-cost | Expensive (privilege signal) |
| **Context understanding** | Deep knowledge of issues | Limited understanding |

### When Local Impact Matches or Exceeds National

| Local Impact | Why Equivalent to National |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| Leading community food bank for 3 years | Sustained, measurable, verifiable impact |
| Tutoring 20+ students to college readiness | Direct, documented outcomes |
| Creating neighborhood mutual aid network | Genuine community leadership |
| Advocating for local policy change | Civic engagement with real results |
| Running family business while in school | Demonstrates responsibility and constraint |
| Caregiving for family members | Shows character over resume-building |

> "We view substantial family contributions as very important... It will only positively impact the review of their application."
> — 315 Deans Commitment (300+ colleges)

### Authenticity Markers for Local Service

**What AOs look for:**
1. **Duration:** Multi-year commitment (not just senior year)
2. **Depth:** Real relationships, not just hours
3. **Leadership growth:** Member → coordinator → leader
4. **Problem identification:** Student-identified need vs. assigned task
5. **Reflection:** Can articulate why it matters and what was learned
6. **Continuation:** Will the work continue? Did you train successors?

### Examples of Strong Local Impact

| Activity | Why It Works |
|----------|--------------|
| "Founded ESL program at local library; trained 8 volunteers; 45 families served over 2 years" | Initiative, scale, sustainability |
| "Weekly grocery delivery to 12 elderly neighbors; organized 6 volunteers; 3 years running" | Consistency, community building |
| "Advocated for bike lanes; attended 15 city council meetings; collected 500+ signatures; policy passed" | Civic engagement, measurable outcome |
| "Built website for local nonprofit; donated 200+ hours; enabled $15K in new donations" | Technical skill applied to community need |

### What NOT to Do (Voluntourism Red Flags)

- One-week trip to build houses abroad (often torn down and rebuilt)
- Expensive program with no follow-up engagement
- "Raised awareness" with no measurable outcome
- Service that could have been done better by locals
- Photography-focused trips for social media content

> "If it's not authentic, don't pursue it. If your passion is sports, writing, music, etc. – pursue your talent in those areas."
> — Tulane Admissions

### Making Caring Common's Message

> "What counts in college admissions are not long brag sheets, but the depth and quality of students' civic and intellectual engagement... Long-term pursuits develop many of the same skills as brief, high-profile community service projects."

## School-Specific Information

### Harvard

- Rating 2 requires demonstrated leadership progression
- Values "impact within communities" over prestige
- Contextual evaluation: achievements relative to opportunities

### Stanford

- "Have a spike, not be a spike" - depth with breadth
- Values "intellectual vitality" shown through sustained engagement
- Thematic coherence highly valued

### MIT

- 4-activity limit forces depth demonstration
- Makers Portfolio values tangible building/creation
- Research depth: substantive projects with outcomes

### Yale

- "Well-developed" over "well-rounded"
- Open to diverse patterns if authentic
- Values curation and intentionality

### Princeton

- "Intellectual curiosity" through sustained engagement
- Standard accolades are "a dime a dozen"
- Seeks "excitement of discovery"
