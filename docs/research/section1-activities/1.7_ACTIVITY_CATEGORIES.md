## Overview

Understanding how different activity categories are valued is essential for portfolio evaluation, but the key insight is that NO category is inherently superior.

### Key Takeaways

- **No activity type hierarchy:** Elite institutions EXPLICITLY REJECT category rankings
- **Three universal dimensions:** Impact, depth/trajectory, and institutional fit apply to ALL activities
- **Field-specific processes exist:** Arts/music have faculty review, athletics has recruitment track
- **"Typical activities" problem:** NHS, student government are ubiquitous - differentiation comes from WHAT you did
- **Endangered species:** Rare skills (bassoon, crew coxswain) offer supply/demand advantage

> "Maybe it's a sport. Maybe it's an instrument. Maybe it's research. Maybe it's being a leader in your community. Math. Baking. **Napping. Hopscotch.** Whatever it is, spend time on it."
> — Chris Peterson, MIT Director

## The Revolutionary Finding: No Activity Type Hierarchy

### MIT's Position

**Chris Peterson, MIT Director of Special Projects and Communications:**
> "Maybe it's a sport. Maybe it's an instrument. Maybe it's research. Maybe it's being a leader in your community. Math. Baking. **Napping. Hopscotch.** Whatever it is, spend time on it. Immerse yourself in it. Enjoy it."

### Stanford's Position

**Richard Shaw, Stanford Dean of Admissions:**
Emphasizes "intellectual vitality and passion" over specific domains - wants to hear "what it is that turns them on" regardless of whether interests manifest inside or outside the classroom.

### The Implication

**Our system must NEVER penalize students for activity category.**
- Research is NOT inherently better than athletics
- Arts is NOT inferior to academics
- Community service is NOT superior to personal hobbies

The only evaluation criteria are:
1. **Depth and authenticity** of engagement
2. **Achievement level** within the category
3. **Fit with institutional values**

## Three Universal Evaluation Dimensions

Instead of category hierarchies, AOs evaluate ALL activities across these dimensions:

| Dimension | Question | Detection Method |
|-----------|----------|------------------|
| **Impact & Achievement** | What did the student accomplish or change? | Quantified outcomes, recognition level |
| **Depth & Trajectory** | How sustained was engagement? Did they grow? | Years involved, leadership progression |
| **Institutional Fit** | How do activities signal alignment with university values? | Thematic coherence, spike matching |

### The Community Building Lens

> "Academic results 'are kind of a given'... It really tends to be everything else that makes their application more compelling. These admissions officers are building a community; they're not just building a classroom."
> — Former Harvard AO

## Field-Specific Portfolio Exceptions

**While no category hierarchy exists, specialized evaluation occurs for:**

| Domain | Special Process | Notes |
|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **Arts/Music** | MIT has separate portals reviewed by faculty | Professional-level achievement evaluated by experts |
| **Athletics** | Parallel recruitment track | 86% acceptance for recruited athletes at Harvard vs 3.41% overall |
| **Research** | Equity-aware evaluation | Stuart Schmill (MIT): "Research is one of these activities that we're very aware they're not offered equitably" |

**Important:** These are PROCESS exceptions, not VALUE hierarchies.

## Field-Specific Achievement Tiers

### MUSIC Achievement Levels

| Level | Achievement | Admissions Impact | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| **Tier 1 (National)** | All-National Band/Orchestra | Highest - equivalent to USAMO | <7% selection rate from 10,000+ applicants |
| **Tier 2 (State)** | All-State Orchestra/Band | Very strong - equivalent to state sports | ~2-3% of participants achieve |
| **Tier 3 (Regional)** | Regional Superior ratings, District honors | Moderately impressive | Starting point for meaningful distinction |

## The Typical Activities Ubiquity Problem

### Core Challenge

At 40,000+ application institutions, NHS/student government/standard clubs signal baseline engagement, NOT differentiation.

### The Data

- 75% of AOs view NHS as indicating leadership/dedication (NACAC)
- BUT Princeton guidance: "Standard accolades like Merit Scholar, NHS, AP Scholar, or club e-board member are **a dime a dozen**"

### Harvard Rating Classifications

- **Rating 3** (majority): "Active participation" but "little to distinguish them from other applicants"
- This encompasses: Standard clubs, varsity sports (non-recruited), typical NHS, general volunteer work

### The Issue

It's NOT the activity type - it's the lack of distinction WITHIN it.

> "A competitive swimmer may dedicate over 20 hours weekly to training, which limits their participation in other clubs. Conversely, another student might explore various activities... **both approaches are equally commendable.**"
> — Christy Pratt, Notre Dame

## What Makes Activities Stand Out

### Delta Institute's Four Markers of Memorable Activities

1. **Depth of Engagement** – Long-term commitment to a specific area
2. **Personal Ownership** – Self-directed initiative (NOT parent-guided)
3. **Measurable Impact** – Tangible results within communities or fields
4. **Unique Perspective** – Individual approach to problems or interests

### Concrete Examples from Accepted Applicants

| Activity | What Made It Stand Out |
|----------|----------------------|
| Bird Watching Club | Organized trips, documented species, shared findings |
| D&D Club | Created inclusive space, organized weekly campaigns |
| Fun Maths Society | Founded 40+ member group, guest speakers, problem-solving |
| Gardening Club | Donated produce to food banks |

**Common Thread:** Genuine interest + initiative + community value

## The Endangered Species Concept

### The Framework

**Lila Fowler's Framework:** Unusual skills valuable to campus communities gain advantage through INSTITUTIONAL NEED FULFILLMENT, not inherent superiority.

### Orchestra/Band "Endangered Species" List

| Instrument | Demand Level | Why |
|------------|--------------|-----|
| **Bassoon** | Very High | Few players, essential for orchestra balance |
| **Oboe** | Very High | Technical difficulty, low player numbers |
| **Tuba** | High | Large ensemble need, low player count |
| **French Horn** | High | Difficult instrument, always needed |
| **Viola** | High | Overshadowed by violin, orchestra needs many |
| **Double Bass** | High | Essential for orchestral sound |
| **Bass Trombone** | High | Specialized, fewer pursue |
| **Euphonium** | Moderate-High | Band essential, low numbers |
| **Harp** | Very High | Extremely rare, always in demand |
| **Bass Clarinet** | Moderate | Specialized role |

> "The rarer the instrument, the higher the chances are for a student musician to win a scholarship. Instruments like the harp, oboe, tuba, and bassoon have the best scholarships."
> — CollegeVine

### Non-Music Examples

- **Crew coxswains:** Specific athletic need with limited supply
- **Specialized technical skills:** Rare programming languages, hardware expertise
- **Uncommon language fluency:** Arabic, Mandarin, indigenous languages

### Important Caveats

- Talent matters more than rarity: "Colleges often prefer a skilled violinist over an average tuba player"
- Passion shows in auditions - don't switch instruments just for strategy
- All-State/All-National achievement matters regardless of instrument
- Institutional needs vary - research specific school ensemble composition

## Passion Projects and Self-Directed Initiatives

### The Gold Standard

**Maite Ballestero (RSI Executive VP):**
> "We want students to find out what they are passionate about and explore it deeply... exploit their surroundings in the most positive way."

### Authenticity Detection Markers

| Authentic Signal | Inauthentic Signal |
|------------------|-------------------|
| Self-motivated start | External pressure (parent-driven) |
| Persistence through obstacles | Smooth path with purchased access |
| Evolution based on learning | Static execution of bought template |
| Connection to personal values | Trending topic with no personal link |

### The Paid Program Problem

> "Admissions officers are **turned off** when experiences repeatedly appear to be bought—versus earned or self-directed."
> — Former Ivy League AO

### The Nuance (Lumiere Education)

> "If a student hustles by themselves to find a professor to work with that will show initiative. But, most students can't and don't do that - and research is seen as having value **regardless of its origin** (as long as the student really did the work)."

### The Reader Limitation

> "The first reader is very young and in almost all cases majored in humanities or social sciences. **They can't tell if a paper in the sciences means anything or is new at all.**"
> — Jon Reider, former Stanford AO

**Implementation:** Document authenticity markers, don't automatically discount paid programs if student did genuine work.

## Virtual/Online Activities

### Post-Pandemic Status: Fully Normalized

> "As long as you show a combination of dedication, skill, and leadership in an activity, it'll be legitimate to colleges, whether you did it remotely or not... Colleges won't hold pandemic- or health-related restrictions against you."
> — PrepScholar

### Credible Virtual Activities

| Activity Type | Examples | Credibility Level |
|---------------|----------|-------------------|
| **Online leadership** | Discord server admin, online community moderator | Moderate-High |
| **Content creation** | YouTube channel, blog, podcast | High (if metrics demonstrable) |
| **Remote volunteering** | Crisis text line, online tutoring | High |
| **Virtual competitions** | Online debate, coding competitions | High |
| **Online organizations** | Remote nonprofit work, virtual clubs | Moderate-High |
| **Self-directed learning** | Coursera certificates, independent research | Moderate |

### Credibility Markers

1. **Measurable outcomes:** Followers, views, impact metrics
2. **Duration:** Started before just applying to college
3. **Leadership/contribution:** Not just participant but contributor
4. **External validation:** Recognition, partnerships, growth
5. **Platform credibility:** GitHub, LinkedIn, established platforms > generic claims

### Red Flags for Virtual Activities

- Vague claims without verifiable outcomes
- No digital footprint to confirm
- Started right before applications
- Claims leadership of "organization" with no web presence
- Impressive titles with no substance

### Equity Benefit

Virtual activities democratize access for:
- Rural students without local options
- Students with family responsibilities
- Disabled students
- Low-income students who can't travel to activities

## School-Specific Information

### MIT

- Separate portals for arts/music (faculty review)
- Values "tinkerer" profile regardless of category
- 4-activity limit forces depth demonstration
- Research equity awareness

### Harvard

- Rating 3 encompasses most "typical" activities
- Rating 1-2 requires exceptional achievement in ANY category
- Building community, not just classroom

### Stanford

- "Intellectual vitality" in any domain
- Values passion over prestige
- Thematic coherence matters more than category

### Yale

- Liberal arts ethos welcomes diverse categories
- "Well-developed" over category-specific achievement
- Arts and humanities equally valued

### Princeton

- "Intellectual curiosity" applies to all categories
- Standard accolades are "a dime a dozen"
- What you DID matters more than category
