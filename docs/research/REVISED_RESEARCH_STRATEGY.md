# Revised Research Strategy for PASS System

## Key Insight from Perplexity Response

Perplexity correctly identified that:
1. Per-school granular data (ED/EA/RD rates) is often not publicly available
2. CDS publication timing varies - not all 2024-25 data is out
3. A 30-school deep-dive in one pass exceeds practical limits

## Our Revised Approach

### Philosophy Shift: Universal Principles + Selective Tailoring

Instead of 30 custom college profiles, we build:

1. **Universal Framework** - What ALL elite colleges look for (the 80% that's common)
2. **Tiered School Categories** - Group schools by shared characteristics
3. **High-Impact Tailoring** - Only customize the things that truly differ and matter

This is actually **better** for students because:
- Professional counselors think in patterns, not 30 separate playbooks
- The fundamentals of a strong application are universal
- Over-tailoring leads to inauthentic applications

## Revised Research Prompts

### PROMPT A: Universal Elite Admissions Criteria (The Core 80%)

```
I'm building a college advising system and need to understand what the top 30 US universities (Ivy League, Stanford, MIT, top privates, top publics) have IN COMMON when evaluating applicants. Rather than school-by-school data, I need the universal patterns.

**Focus on what elite admissions officers consistently value across institutions.**

### 1. Academic Expectations: The Universal Bar

What academic profile do elite schools universally expect?

**GPA and Course Rigor:**
- What is the effective GPA floor for serious consideration at elite schools?
- How do admissions officers universally weigh weighted vs. unweighted GPA?
- What course rigor signals "college ready" vs. "didn't challenge themselves"?
- How many AP/IB/honors courses is considered "rigorous" at a well-resourced school?
- How is rigor evaluated at schools without APs?
- When does a B+ student still have a chance, and what else must be true?

**Standardized Testing (in the test-required/recommended era):**
- What test scores make you "academically qualified" at most T20 schools?
- What scores make testing a strength vs. neutral vs. weakness?
- When should test-optional students submit vs. not submit?
- How do admissions officers view score choice and superscoring?

**Academic Trends:**
- How is an upward grade trend valued vs. consistent excellence?
- How severely is senior year "slump" viewed?
- How do admissions officers interpret a single bad semester?

### 2. Extracurricular Evaluation: Universal Principles

How do admissions officers at elite schools universally evaluate activities?

**Quality vs. Quantity:**
- Is there an ideal number of activities? What's too few? Too many?
- How do officers distinguish "padding" from genuine involvement?
- What signals depth vs. superficial participation?

**Leadership Evaluation:**
- What types of leadership actually impress (vs. just holding a title)?
- How is "founded an organization" evaluated vs. "led an existing one"?
- When does leadership matter less than individual excellence?

**The "Spike" Concept:**
- How universally do elite schools value a focused "spike" vs. well-rounded?
- What makes a spike compelling vs. just narrow?
- Can you have multiple spikes, or does that defeat the purpose?

**Impact and Achievement:**
- How do officers assess "impact" claims?
- What's the difference between school-level and state/national impact?
- How are activities verified or fact-checked?

**Time Commitment:**
- What hours/week signals serious commitment?
- How is summer activity involvement weighted vs. school year?
- How do officers view activities dropped after 1-2 years?

### 3. Essays: Universal Quality Standards

What makes an essay work at ANY elite school?

**Voice and Authenticity:**
- What does "authentic voice" actually mean in evaluation?
- How do readers detect genuine vs. performative essays?
- What are the universal red flags that hurt any application?

**Content and Insight:**
- What depth of self-reflection do elite schools expect?
- How do readers evaluate intellectual curiosity in essays?
- What balance of "showing" vs. "telling" works?

**Topic Selection:**
- Are there universally "risky" topics to avoid?
- Are there universally "overused" topics that require exceptional execution?
- What makes a common topic work vs. fail?

**Craft and Execution:**
- What writing quality signals "this student will succeed in our classes"?
- How is creativity valued vs. clarity?
- What structural approaches work best?

### 4. Recommendations and School Context

**Teacher Recommendations:**
- What do ALL elite schools want to learn from recommendations?
- What phrases/qualities carry weight universally?
- How important is the recommender's credentials vs. content?

**Counselor Recommendations:**
- What context do elite schools universally want from counselors?
- How is school context (under-resourced, limited offerings) communicated?

**School Profile:**
- How do officers universally calibrate for school difficulty?
- What metrics do they use to understand a school's context?

### 5. Character and Personal Qualities

What character traits are universally valued across elite admissions?

- Intellectual curiosity - how is it demonstrated and verified?
- Resilience/grit - what evidence is convincing?
- Community contribution - what counts?
- Leadership potential - beyond titles, what signals this?
- Maturity and self-awareness - how does this appear in applications?

### 6. Red Flags: Universal Negatives

What universally hurts applications at elite schools?

- Academic red flags (beyond low grades)
- Activity red flags
- Essay red flags
- Character/integrity concerns
- Application inconsistencies

### 7. The Holistic Review Process

How do elite schools universally conduct holistic review?

- How are applications typically read (first reader, committee, etc.)?
- How are close decisions made?
- What tips a "maybe" to "yes" or "no"?
- How is the full picture assembled from components?

**IMPORTANT: Cite specific admissions officers, published guidance, or documented practices. Include direct quotes where available. Focus on patterns across T20/T30 schools rather than any single institution.**
```

### PROMPT B: School Category Characteristics (The Meaningful Differences)

```
For college advising, I need to understand how top US universities meaningfully DIFFER in their admissions approach. Rather than 30 individual profiles, help me categorize schools by their distinctive characteristics.

**Focus on differences that actually affect application strategy.**

### 1. Testing Policy Categories (2025-2026 Cycle)

Group schools by current testing approach:

**Test Required Schools:**
- Which elite schools now require SAT/ACT?
- What drove their decision to require testing?
- How do they communicate the role of scores?

**Truly Test-Optional Schools:**
- Which schools are genuinely test-optional (scores don't help/hurt)?
- What evidence exists that they mean it?
- How should students decide whether to submit?

**Test-Optional But Scores Help:**
- Which schools are nominally optional but strong scores clearly help?
- What's the strategic implication?

**Test-Blind Schools:**
- Which elite schools won't consider scores even if submitted?

For each category, provide the list of schools and source for their policy.

### 2. Demonstrated Interest Categories

This significantly affects application strategy. Categorize schools:

**Interest Matters Significantly:**
- Which schools explicitly track and value demonstrated interest?
- How do they track it (visits, emails, events)?
- What actions have measurable impact?

**Interest is Noted but Minor:**
- Which schools consider interest as a tiebreaker?
- What's worth doing vs. overkill?

**Interest Not Tracked:**
- Which schools (often most selective) don't track interest?
- Why don't they track it?
- Does "Why Us" essay substitute for demonstrated interest?

### 3. Early Application Strategy Categories

Group schools by early round dynamics:

**ED Schools (Binding) with Clear Advantage:**
- Which schools have meaningfully higher ED admit rates?
- What's the typical ED advantage (2x? 3x? marginal?)?
- Financial aid implications of ED?

**EA Schools with Advantage:**
- Which non-binding early options provide meaningful advantage?
- REA schools and their restrictions

**Early = Same as RD:**
- Which schools' early rates are misleading (athlete/legacy heavy)?
- Where does early application NOT help?

### 4. What Makes Each School Type Distinctive

Rather than 30 profiles, what makes these CATEGORIES different?

**Research Universities (Harvard, Stanford, MIT, etc.):**
- What do these schools uniquely value?
- How should applications differ for research-intensive schools?
- What opportunities do they emphasize?

**Liberal Arts-Leaning (Yale, Princeton, Brown, etc.):**
- What's distinctively valued?
- How do writing/humanities students fare?
- Application emphasis differences?

**STEM-Focused (MIT, Caltech, CMU, Georgia Tech):**
- What unique expectations exist?
- How are non-STEM activities viewed?
- Technical demonstration expectations?

**Unique Curricular Models:**
- Open curriculum (Brown): how does this affect applicant evaluation?
- Core curriculum (Columbia, Chicago): what do they look for?
- Honors colleges within state schools (UVA, Michigan): differences?

**Large State Flagships (UCLA, Berkeley, Michigan, UVA):**
- How does evaluation differ from small privates?
- What's the role of intended major?
- In-state vs. out-of-state considerations?

### 5. Institutional Values That Differ

What genuinely differs in what schools prioritize?

**Service/Community Emphasis:**
- Which schools notably prioritize community engagement?
- Georgetown, Notre Dame, others?
- How should applications reflect this?

**Innovation/Entrepreneurship:**
- Which schools especially value this?
- Stanford, MIT culture differences?

**Intellectual Tradition:**
- Chicago's "Life of the Mind"
- Columbia's Core
- How do these affect essay and application approach?

**Pre-Professional vs. Exploratory:**
- Penn's Wharton, Cornell's schools vs. Brown's open curriculum
- How does institutional philosophy affect applications?

### 6. Supplemental Essay Approach by Category

Rather than 30 essay lists, what TYPES of essays matter?

**"Why Us" Essays:**
- Which schools require them? Word counts?
- What distinguishes a good "Why Us"?
- Research depth expected?

**Community/Diversity Essays:**
- Which schools emphasize these?
- What are they really asking?

**Intellectual Curiosity Essays:**
- "Letter to roommate" (Stanford)
- "Intellectual experience" (various)
- What's the archetype?

**Creative/Unconventional:**
- UChicago's quirky prompts
- "What matters to you" variations
- Approach differences?

**IMPORTANT: Focus on actionable categorizations. What should change in how a student approaches Harvard vs. MIT vs. Brown vs. UCLA? Only highlight differences that meaningfully affect strategy.**
```

### PROMPT C: Activity and Award Tier Classification Standards

```
I'm building an activity and award evaluation system for college advising. I need defensible, professional-counselor-level standards for classifying extracurricular activities and awards into tiers.

**Base this on how elite college admissions officers actually evaluate activities - not arbitrary rankings.**

### 1. Activity Tier Framework

Define four tiers with clear criteria:

**Tier 1: Rare/Exceptional Achievement (Top 1%)**

What specific achievements qualify as Tier 1?

In each major area, what's the threshold?
- **STEM research**: Publication? National competition placement? What level?
- **Math competitions**: USAMO? AIME qualification? What cut-off?
- **Science competitions**: ISEF finalist? Regeneron STS?
- **Music**: All-State vs. All-National? Competition wins?
- **Arts**: YoungArts winner? Gold Portfolio Scholastic?
- **Athletics**: D1 recruited? National rankings?
- **Debate**: TOC qualifier? National finalist?
- **Writing**: National awards? Publication quality?
- **Leadership**: What scale/impact qualifies?
- **Entrepreneurship**: Revenue? Users? What metrics?
- **Service**: National recognition? Presidential Scholar?

What makes something "rare" - is it number of recipients? Selection rate? Prestige?

**Tier 2: Distinguished Achievement (Top 5-10%)**

What's the threshold for "strong but not exceptional"?
- State-level recognition in competitions
- Regional leadership roles
- Selective program admissions (which programs?)
- Research experience without publication
- Significant organizational leadership
- High achievement in competitive activities

Provide specific examples and thresholds.

**Tier 3: Solid Achievement (Top 25%)**

What characterizes meaningful but common involvement?
- School-level leadership and recognition
- Multi-year commitment with progression
- Club/team captain roles
- Community impact at local level
- Skill development and dedication

Where's the line between Tier 3 and Tier 2?

**Tier 4: Participation**

What is "good but not distinctive"?
- Membership without leadership
- Short-term involvement
- Activities without progression or achievement
- Common extracurriculars without distinguishing features

How many Tier 4s is too many?

### 2. Activity Evaluation Criteria

What factors determine tier placement?

**Time and Commitment:**
- What hours/week matters? 5? 10? 20+?
- Years of involvement expectations
- Summer commitment significance
- How is intensity measured?

**Impact and Achievement:**
- How is "impact" verified?
- What metrics matter (people affected, funds raised, etc.)?
- How do officers distinguish real impact from inflated claims?

**Leadership and Initiative:**
- Position vs. demonstrated leadership
- Creating something new vs. excelling in existing roles
- Solo excellence vs. team leadership

**Recognition and Competition:**
- What recognition levels matter?
- How are competitions evaluated?
- Non-competitive achievement paths?

**Passion and Authenticity:**
- How do officers identify genuine interest vs. resume building?
- What patterns signal authenticity?
- Red flags for performative involvement?

### 3. Award Classification Standards

**Tier 1 Awards (Nationally Prestigious):**

List specific awards with:
- Name and sponsor
- Selection rate or number of recipients
- How admissions officers view it
- Examples: Presidential Scholar, Intel ISEF finalist, YoungArts winner...

**Tier 2 Awards (Regionally/Nationally Recognized):**
- State-level competition placements
- National Merit Semifinalist/Finalist
- Selective scholarship finalists
- Examples with context

**Tier 3 Awards (School/Local Recognition):**
- Department awards
- Local competition recognition
- School honors societies
- What's meaningful vs. filler?

**Red Flag "Awards":**
- Pay-to-play "honors"
- Misleading "national" recognitions
- How to identify vanity awards

### 4. Context Calibration

How should tier assessment change based on context?

**School Resources:**
- How is a rural student with no AP access evaluated?
- What about students from schools with no competitive teams?
- Limited extracurricular offerings?

**Socioeconomic Context:**
- Students who must work jobs
- Family responsibilities
- Limited access to expensive programs (summer camps, travel teams)

**Geographic Access:**
- Students far from competition venues
- Limited local opportunities
- Online vs. in-person achievement

### 5. Common App Optimization

How should students prioritize their 10 activities and 5 honors?

**Activity List Strategy:**
- Order of activities (most impressive first? Most meaningful first?)
- Description writing best practices
- Position/Leadership role phrasing
- Hours and weeks/year accuracy

**Honors Section Strategy:**
- Which 5 to select from a longer list?
- Recognition level designations
- Grade level assignments
- When "none" is better than weak entries

### 6. "Spike" Assessment

How should a "spike" be evaluated?

- What defines a genuine spike?
- Depth requirements for spike status
- How supporting activities should relate to spike
- When is a spike too narrow?
- Multiple interests vs. focused spike trade-off

**IMPORTANT: Provide specific, defensible classifications with reasoning. This should be usable as a reference for activity evaluation. Cite admissions officer guidance, counselor standards, or documented practices where available.**
```

## Implementation Plan

After running these 4 prompts, we will have:

1. **Universal Framework** (Prompt A) → Powers core evaluation logic
2. **School Categories** (Prompt B) → Powers strategic recommendations
3. **Activity/Award Tiers** (Prompt C) → Powers portfolio assessment
4. **Officer Insights** (Prompt D) → Powers authenticity of guidance

### Data We Still Need Per-School (Minimal Tailoring)

For each of the 30 schools, we only need:
- Testing policy (required/optional/blind)
- Demonstrated interest policy (tracked/not tracked)
- Early application options and any known advantage
- Supplemental essay count and word limits (changes yearly anyway)
- Notable institutional values keywords

This can be maintained in a simple lookup table updated annually, not deep profiles.

### What We DON'T Need Per-School

- Exact admit rates (changes yearly, publicly available)
- GPA/test score percentiles (students can look these up)
- Detailed program rankings
- Complex yield calculations

This approach is more maintainable, more honest about our data limitations, and actually better for students.
