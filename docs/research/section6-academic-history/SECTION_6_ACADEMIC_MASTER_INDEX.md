## Section Overview

Section 6 provides comprehensive research-backed frameworks for evaluating a student's academic history in college admissions. This includes course rigor assessment, grade interpretation, international curriculum conversions, and red flag detection.

### Key Capabilities

1. **Course Rigor Evaluation** - Assess course level hierarchy and AP difficulty
2. **Grade Interpretation** - Context-calibrated GPA analysis with inflation detection
3. **School Context Calibration** - Adjust for school type and resources
4. **International Curriculum Conversion** - IB, A-Levels, CBSE → US equivalents
5. **Special Populations** - Homeschool, dual enrollment evaluation
6. **Red Flag Detection** - Academic concerns and mitigation guidance

## Key Principles from Research

### 1. Context Is Everything
> "Colleges almost always evaluate students in comparison to the fellow students at their own high schools. Since no two high schools are alike, it is impossible to hold students to the same standards."
> — CollegeVine

A 3.8 at Phillips Exeter means something different than a 3.8 at an under-resourced rural school.

### 2. Rigor Maximization > Raw GPA
> "Admissions officers assess whether a student challenged themselves with AP, IB, or Honors courses, and whether they pursued rigor without their grades suffering."
> — IvyWise

Taking the most rigorous curriculum available matters more than the raw number of APs.

### 3. Junior Year Is Critical
> "Junior year is widely regarded as the most important year for GPA evaluation. These are the most recent grades colleges see when students apply."
> — Empowerly

### 4. Test Scores Validate Grades
> "Higher SAT/ACT scores are associated with higher college GPAs but higher high school GPAs are not."
> — Opportunity Insights (Harvard/Stanford research)

### 5. Senior Year Rigor Must Be Maintained
> "74.1% of colleges consider senior year grades important when making admissions decisions. 22% of colleges rescinded at least one admission per year."
> — NACAC / Education Week
