## Overview

International curricula present unique evaluation challenges. Unlike AP courses with standardized exams, international systems vary significantly in grading conventions, rigor levels, and recognition by US institutions.

### Key Principle

> "US universities use holistic admissions. High IB scores alone don't guarantee admission - essays, extracurriculars, and recommendations are equally important... Universities understand IB scoring - they don't need you to convert. Some don't convert at all and evaluate IB scores directly."
> — Crimson Education

## IB Diploma Program

### Score Structure

**Total Score:** 0-45 points
- 6 subjects × 7 points max = 42 points
- Extended Essay + TOK = 3 bonus points
- Maximum achievable: 45 points

### Overall Score Interpretation

| IB Score | GPA Equivalent | T20 Assessment | Global Percentile |
|----------|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| 43-45 | 3.95-4.0 | Exceptional | Top 0.5% |
| 40-42 | 3.85-3.95 | Very Strong | Top 2-3% |
| 38-39 | 3.7-3.85 | Strong | Top 5-8% |
| 35-37 | 3.5-3.7 | Competitive | Top 15-20% |
| 32-34 | 3.3-3.5 | Adequate | Top 30-40% |
| 28-31 | 3.0-3.3 | Below Competitive | 40-60% |
| <28 | <3.0 | Concerning | Below median |

### Subject-Level Score Conversion

| IB Score | Letter Grade | GPA Equivalent | Notes |
|----------|--------------|----------------|-------|
| 7 | A/A+ | 4.0 | Exceptional - top 10% of subject |
| 6 | A/A- | 4.0 | Strong - demonstrates mastery |
| 5 | B/B+ | 3.0-3.3 | Solid - adequate preparation |
| 4 | B-/C+ | 2.7 | Concerning for core subjects |
| 3 | C | 2.0 | Weak - below passing for college credit |
| 1-2 | D/F | 1.0-0.0 | Failing |

### Higher Level vs Standard Level

| Level | Hours | Weight | US Equivalent |
|-------|-------|--------|---------------|
| HL (Higher Level) | 240+ | 1.0 | AP level |
| SL (Standard Level) | 150 | 0.85 | Honors level |

### Predicted vs Final Scores

> "Predicted IB scores are crucial for university applications. Most applications are submitted in October-December of your final IB year, before you take exams in May. Universities make conditional offers based on predicted grades."

**Key considerations:**
- Typical variance: ±2 points from predicted
- US universities evaluate predicted scores for admission
- Conditional admission may be revoked if final scores significantly lower
- Some students underpredicted; strong final scores can help waitlist cases

### Elite School Expectations

| School Tier | Expected IB Score | Notes |
|-------------|------------------|-------|
| T5 (HYPSM) | 40+ | Need mix of 6s and 7s |
| T10 | 38+ | Healthy mix of 6s/7s, no <6 |
| T20 | 35+ | Competitive threshold |
| T30 | 32+ | Solid foundation |

> "With 3-7% admission rates, even perfect scores and applications don't guarantee admission. Most admitted students have: outstanding IB scores (40-42+), perfect GPA/school grades, SAT 1500+, strong extracurricular achievements."
> — NUM8ERS

## UK A-Levels

### Grade Conversion to US GPA

| A-Level Grade | US GPA | UK Percentage | US Letter |
|---------------|--------|---------------|-----------|
| A* | 4.0 | 90-100% | A+ |
| A | 4.0 | 80-89% | A |
| B | 3.3 | 70-79% | B+ |
| C | 2.7 | 60-69% | B- |
| D | 2.0 | 50-59% | C |
| E | 1.0 | 40-49% | D |

### Critical Context: UK vs US Percentages

> "A 70% in the UK is excellent and falls in the First-Class range, while in the US a 70% would be considered average (a C grade)."
> — TutorChase

**Key principle:** Never directly compare UK percentages to US scales. An 80% in UK A-Levels is exceptional, equivalent to an A in the US system.

### Elite School Expectations

| School Tier | Expected Grades | Notes |
|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| T5 (HYPSM) | A*A*A* or A*A*A | Three A-Levels minimum |
| T10 | A*AA or AAA | Strong showing |
| T20 | AAB | Competitive |
| T30 | ABB | Acceptable |

### Subject Requirements

Unlike US applications, A-Level subject choices are highly specialized:
- **Engineering:** Math + Physics required, Further Math valued
- **Pre-Med:** Biology + Chemistry required
- **Humanities:** Relevant humanities subjects
- **Economics:** Math strongly preferred

### AS-Levels (Advanced Subsidiary)

- Typically taken in Year 12 (age 16-17)
- Roughly equivalent to first half of A-Level
- Less weight than full A-Level
- Can demonstrate breadth beyond 3-4 A-Levels

## Indian Education Boards

### CBSE (Central Board of Secondary Education)

| Percentage | T20 Assessment | Notes |
|------------|----------------|-------|
| 95%+ | Strong | Top tier nationally |
| 90-94% | Competitive | Good standing |
| 85-89% | Adequate | Average for US applicants |
| 80-84% | Below Competitive | Challenging |
| <80% | Concerning | Significant disadvantage |

### ISC (Indian School Certificate)

Similar to CBSE but often considered slightly more rigorous:
- Generally higher grade expectations
- More recognized internationally
- Similar percentage thresholds

### Context Considerations

1. **Grade inflation concerns:** Recent years have seen significant score inflation
2. **External validation important:** SAT/AP scores help calibrate
3. **School reputation matters:** Top schools (DPS, Modern, Sanskriti) have more weight
4. **Competitive environment:** Context of intense competition understood

### Typical Strong Profile for T20

- 95%+ in Class 12
- Strong SAT (1500+) for validation
- Some AP exam scores if available
- JEE/NEET preparation shows rigor (even if not taking exam)

## Chinese Gaokao

### Overview

The Gaokao is China's national college entrance examination, taken by ~10 million students annually.

### Interpretation Challenges

- Raw scores vary by province (different tests)
- Provincial rankings more meaningful than raw scores
- Not directly comparable across provinces

### Evaluation Framework

| Provincial Rank | T20 Assessment | Notes |
|-----------------|----------------|-------|
| Top 1% | Highly competitive | Exceptional |
| Top 5% | Strong | Good foundation |
| Top 10% | Competitive | Solid |
| Top 20% | Adequate | Acceptable |

### Context for US Admissions

1. **Typically supplemented:** Most Chinese applicants also take SAT/TOEFL
2. **Gaokao not required:** US schools don't require Gaokao scores
3. **Provincial context matters:** Understand which province/competition level
4. **Competition intensity:** AOs understand extreme selectivity

## Holistic Evaluation Principles

### What US Universities Consider

> "American universities are experienced with international applicants and typically do not require a UK student to convert their grades to a 4.0 GPA. On applications, you can report your A-Level results and your university's degree classification as is."
> — Crimson Education

### Key Evaluation Factors

1. **Curriculum rigor:** Did student take hardest available courses?
2. **Performance in context:** How does student compare to peers?
3. **Trend:** Is performance improving or declining?
4. **Subject relevance:** Do courses align with intended major?
5. **External validation:** Standardized tests help calibrate

### What NOT to Do

> "The Fulbright Commission's EducationUSA advisers explicitly note that there isn't an exact conversion and recommend against trying to self-convert UK degree scores to GPA on applications."

**Avoid:**
- Self-converting to GPA (let universities do this)
- Comparing percentages directly across systems
- Assuming US standards apply to international grades

### What TO Do

- Report grades in original system
- Provide school profile/context
- Include standardized test scores (SAT/ACT, AP exams)
- Explain system if unusual
- Let counselor provide context in recommendation
