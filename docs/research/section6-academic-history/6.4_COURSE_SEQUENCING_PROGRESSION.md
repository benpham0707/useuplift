## Overview

Course sequencing reveals a student's academic planning, intellectual growth, and preparation for their intended field. Admissions officers analyze not just what courses were taken, but how they build upon each other and align with stated goals.

### Key Principle

> "If you take calculus prior to 12th grade and it is easily accessible, seek out opportunities for other math classes either online or at a local college. However, [Caltech] never expects you to enroll in other institutions to take more STEM courses, but they do want you to stay connected to math throughout high school."
> — Caltech Admissions

---

## The Sequencing Principle

### What Admissions Officers Evaluate

1. **Logical Progression:** Do courses build appropriately on prerequisites?
2. **Continuous Engagement:** Did student stay engaged with subject throughout high school?
3. **Major Alignment:** Do courses support intended field of study?
4. **Challenge Escalation:** Did rigor increase over time?
5. **Gap Avoidance:** Are there unexplained gaps in core sequences?

### The "Stay Connected" Philosophy

> "At Caltech, they like to see students push themselves to take the most advanced STEM courses that they can."

**Core insight:** Even after completing highest available course, find ways to continue the subject:
- Dual enrollment for advanced courses
- Online courses
- Independent study
- Research projects

## Mathematics Progression

### Standard Progression (4 Years)

| Year | Standard Track | Accelerated Track | Highly Accelerated |
|------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 9th | Algebra I | Geometry | Algebra II |
| 10th | Geometry | Algebra II | Pre-Calculus |
| 11th | Algebra II | Pre-Calculus | AP Calculus AB/BC |
| 12th | Pre-Calculus | AP Calculus AB/BC | Multivariable/Linear Algebra |

### STEM Major Expectations

| Major Field | Minimum Math | Competitive Math | Exceptional |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Engineering | Calculus AB | Calculus BC | BC + Multivariable |
| Computer Science | Calculus AB | Calculus BC | BC + Linear Algebra |
| Physics | Calculus BC | BC + Physics C | Differential Equations |
| Mathematics | Calculus BC | Multivariable | Research-level coursework |
| Economics | Calculus AB | Calculus BC | BC + Statistics |

### Math Red Flags

- Stopping at Algebra II when calculus is available
- Not taking calculus for STEM-intended major
- Downgrading from BC to AB without explanation
- Senior year gap in mathematics

### Math Strong Signals

- Completing Calculus BC by junior year
- Dual enrollment in Multivariable Calculus/Linear Algebra
- AIME/USAMO qualification
- Research involving mathematical methods

## Science Progression

### Standard Science Sequence

| Year | Typical | Competitive STEM | Notes |
|------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| 9th | Biology | Honors/AP Biology | Foundation |
| 10th | Chemistry | Honors/AP Chemistry | Core science |
| 11th | Physics | AP Physics 1 or C | Physical sciences |
| 12th | Elective/AP | Additional APs | Specialization |

### STEM-Specific Requirements

> "If you're applying to a very selective college, be aware that many will require or highly recommend that you complete four years of science in high school. They might also require your fourth year of science to be an AP science class."
> — PrepScholar

**Engineering/Physics Track:**
1. Biology (9th)
2. Chemistry (10th)
3. AP Physics C: Mechanics (11th)
4. AP Physics C: E&M + AP Chemistry (12th)

**Pre-Med Track:**
1. Biology (9th)
2. AP Chemistry (10th)
3. AP Biology (11th)
4. AP Physics + Additional science (12th)

### Science Red Flags

- Only 2-3 years of science
- Avoiding physics entirely (especially for engineering)
- No lab sciences beyond graduation requirement
- AP science without corresponding AP exam

### Science Strong Signals

- Both Physics C courses completed
- Research experience in science
- Science olympiad/competition achievements
- Independent scientific projects

## Humanities Progression

### English Sequence

| Year | Standard | Competitive Humanities |
|------|----------|----------------------|
| 9th | English 9 | Honors English |
| 10th | English 10 | Honors English |
| 11th | English 11 | AP English Language |
| 12th | English 12 | AP English Literature |

### History/Social Studies Sequence

| Year | Standard | Competitive |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 9th | World Geography/History | AP Human Geography |
| 10th | World History | AP World History |
| 11th | US History | AP US History |
| 12th | Government/Economics | AP Government + AP Econ |

### Humanities Red Flags

- No AP English courses
- Only one history AP
- Dropping foreign language before year 4
- Avoiding writing-intensive courses

### Humanities Strong Signals

- Both AP English courses (Language and Literature)
- Multiple AP histories
- 4-5 years of foreign language with AP
- Published writing (Scholastic, Concord Review)

## Foreign Language Progression

### Year-by-Year Expectations

| Years | Level | College Perception |
|-------|-------|-------------------|
| 2 | Minimum | Meets basic requirement |
| 3 | Adequate | Acceptable |
| 4 | Good | Demonstrates commitment |
| 5+ or AP | Excellent | Strong dedication |

### T20 Expectations

Most highly selective schools want to see:
- **Minimum:** 3 years of one language
- **Competitive:** 4 years with honors/AP
- **Exceptional:** 5+ years, AP exam, or native fluency demonstration

### Language Red Flags

- Stopping at year 2
- Switching languages without reaching proficiency
- Dropping language in senior year

### Language Strong Signals

- AP Language exam score of 4-5
- SAT Subject Test in language (where applicable)
- Study abroad experience
- Use of language in community service/activities

## Major-Aligned Sequencing

### Engineering / Computer Science

**Critical Sequence:**
```
9th:  Honors/Advanced Math + Biology
10th: Pre-Calculus or Calc AB + Chemistry
11th: Calculus BC + AP Physics C Mech + AP CS A
12th: Physics C E&M + Statistics + Advanced math if available
```

**Key Requirements:**
- Calculus BC (not just AB) preferred
- Physics C (not just Physics 1)
- AP Computer Science A for CS majors
- Continuous math through senior year

### Pre-Medicine / Life Sciences

**Critical Sequence:**
```
9th:  Honors Biology
10th: AP Chemistry or Honors Chemistry
11th: AP Biology + AP Chemistry (if not taken) + Calculus
12th: AP Physics + Statistics + Additional sciences
```

**Key Requirements:**
- All core sciences (Bio, Chem, Physics)
- Strong lab science foundation
- Math through at least Calculus AB
- Research experience valuable

### Humanities / Social Sciences

**Critical Sequence:**
```
9th:  Honors English + World History or Human Geo + Language Year 1-2
10th: Honors English + AP World History + Language Year 2-3
11th: AP English Language + AP US History + Language Year 3-4
12th: AP English Literature + AP Gov/Euro History + AP Language
```

**Key Requirements:**
- Both AP English courses
- Multiple AP histories
- 4+ years foreign language
- Strong writing portfolio

### Business / Economics

**Critical Sequence:**
```
9th:  Honors Math + Social Studies
10th: Pre-Calculus + AP Human Geography optional
11th: Calculus AB/BC + AP Microeconomics
12th: AP Statistics + AP Macroeconomics + Additional math
```

**Key Requirements:**
- Calculus (BC preferred)
- Both economics APs
- Statistics
- Quantitative strength demonstrated

## Red Flags in Course Sequencing

### Major Misalignment

| Claimed Major | Red Flag Pattern |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Engineering | Stopped at Algebra II or Calc AB |
| Computer Science | No CS courses, weak math |
| Pre-Med | Avoided Chemistry or Biology |
| English | No AP English courses |
| History | Only one history course |

### Rigor Avoidance Patterns

1. **Downgrading:** Moving from AP to honors without explanation
2. **Early Exit:** Stopping a sequence when higher levels available
3. **Cherry-Picking:** Only easy APs, avoiding hard ones in major area
4. **Senior Slide:** Dramatically reduced rigor in 12th grade

### Inconsistent Progression

- Strong in STEM, weak in humanities (acceptable)
- Strong in humanities, weak in STEM (acceptable for non-STEM)
- Erratic performance without explanation (concerning)
