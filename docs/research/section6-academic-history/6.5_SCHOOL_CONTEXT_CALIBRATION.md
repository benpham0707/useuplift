## Overview

A student's GPA cannot be evaluated in isolation—the school context fundamentally changes its meaning. This module provides the calibration framework for interpreting academic performance relative to school environment.

### The Core Insight

> "Colleges almost always evaluate students in comparison to the fellow students at their own high schools. Since no two high schools are alike, it is impossible to hold students to the same standards when they're coming from different circumstances."
> — CollegeVine

---

## The Context Principle

### Why Context Matters

| Same GPA | Different Contexts | Different Meaning |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 3.8 UW | Phillips Exeter | Top 20% (grade deflation) |
| 3.8 UW | Average suburban public | Top 35% (standard) |
| 3.8 UW | Under-resourced school | Top 15% (limited competition) |

### What Admissions Officers Consider

1. **School profile information** - Colleges receive profiles with grade distributions
2. **Peer comparison** - How does applicant compare to classmates?
3. **Available rigor** - Did student take the hardest courses offered?
4. **Historical data** - How have students from this school performed at our institution?
5. **Counselor context** - What does the recommendation letter reveal?

> "Colleges can easily notice school-specific grade inflation by looking at the school's yearly college profile. Many schools provide the colleges with a breakdown of GPAs or a grade distribution chart."
> — Top Tier Admissions

## Grade Deflation Schools

### The Grade Deflation Effect

> "At elite private schools, which, unlike public schools, can pick their classes and which, like elite colleges, have grown more selective over the past 20 years, it is not a surprise that their students are getting more A's... but many top prep schools maintain grade deflation policies."
> — Grade Inflation Analysis

### Known Grade-Deflated Institutions

| Type | Schools | Impact |
|------|---------|--------|
| Elite Prep | Exeter, Andover, Dalton | B+ is genuinely strong |
| STEM Magnet | TJ, Stuy, IMSA | STEM deflation common |
| Some Privates | Varies | Check school profile |

### How Deflation Affects Admissions

1. **AO Familiarity:** Officers from top schools know these schools
2. **Profile Data:** Grade distributions shown in school profile
3. **Peer Comparison:** Compared to classmates, not national norms
4. **Historical Success:** Track record of graduates at university

## Grade Inflation Patterns

### The National Trend

> "Before the pandemic, 47% of high school students graduated with an A-minus to an A-plus average—an increase of nearly 10 percentage points over a 20-year period. And some of the biggest increases are coming from 'resourced' schools, especially independent private schools."
> — Grade Inflation Research

### Inflation Indicators

| Signal | What It Means |
|--------|---------------|
| 70%+ of class has 3.5+ GPA | Significant inflation |
| Average GPA above 3.5 | Above national norms |
| Most grades are A's | Grade compression |
| Weak test scores with high GPAs | GPA doesn't reflect ability |

### How Colleges Respond

> "In recent years, many of the most selective colleges have returned to relying on standardized testing. Colleges like MIT, Georgetown, Brown, Cornell, Harvard, and Dartmouth, among others, have reinstated the requirement for SAT or ACT testing, arguing that testing is a more consistent predictor of college success that helps admissions offices evaluate an applicant in context."
> — Top Tier Admissions

**Standardized test return rationale:**
- Tests provide common comparison point
- GPA alone cannot distinguish in inflated environment
- AP exam scores validate course grades
- Test scores help calibrate school grading

## How Colleges Verify Context

### School Profile Information

What colleges receive:
1. Grade distribution (% with each GPA range)
2. Available courses (AP/IB/Honors offerings)
3. Class size and demographics
4. College-going rate
5. Historical applicant data

### Counselor Recommendation

The counselor provides:
- School context narrative
- Student's relative standing
- Course rigor explanation
- Any special circumstances

### Cross-Validation Methods

> "Schools cross-reference self-reported vs official records."

1. **Self-reported vs Official:** Discrepancies flagged
2. **Counselor confirmation:** Verifies context claims
3. **Historical data:** How past students performed
4. **Test scores:** Validate GPA meaning

## Context Adjustment Formulas

### Base Adjustment by School Type

| School Type | GPA Adjustment | Harvard Score Adjustment |
|-------------|---------------|-------------------------|
| Elite Prep | +0.0 (already calibrated) | -0.3 to -0.5 |
| Competitive Magnet | +0.0 | -0.2 to -0.3 |
| Well-Resourced | +0.0 | 0.0 |
| Average Public | -0.1 | +0.0 to +0.2 |
| Under-Resourced | +0.3 context bonus | -0.3 to -0.5 |
| Rural/Remote | +0.3 context bonus | -0.3 to -0.5 |

### Rigor Maximization Bonus

| Rigor Level | Adjustment |
|-------------|------------|
| 100% of available rigor | -0.2 to Harvard score |
| 90%+ of available rigor | -0.1 to Harvard score |
| 70-89% of available | 0.0 |
| 50-69% of available | +0.1 to Harvard score |
| <50% of available | +0.3 to Harvard score |

### First-Generation/Low-Income Adjustment

| Factor | Adjustment |
|--------|------------|
| First-generation college | -0.2 to Harvard score |
| Low-income background | -0.2 to Harvard score |
| Both | -0.3 to Harvard score |

## Special Populations

### First-Generation Students

> "First-generation students often face information asymmetry about course selection and college preparation."

**Evaluation considerations:**
- May not have known to take rigorous courses early
- Growth trajectory particularly valued
- Context of family support matters
- External validation through tests important

### Students with Work Responsibilities

| Hours/Week | Context Adjustment |
|------------|-------------------|
| 10-15 | Noted in context |
| 15-20 | -0.1 to Harvard score |
| 20+ | -0.2 to Harvard score |
| Primary family support | -0.3 to Harvard score |

### International Students

| System | Context Notes |
|--------|--------------|
| IB | Well-understood globally |
| A-Levels | Well-understood |
| Indian Boards | Percentile matters more than raw % |
| Chinese Gaokao | Provincial ranking key |
