## SECTION 5.2: RATING AND SCORING SYSTEMS

### The Multiplicity of Scales

Elite universities employ **proprietary methodologies** that differ dramatically:

| School | Scale | Directionality | Dimensions | Special Features |
|--------|-------|----------------|------------|------------------|
| **Harvard** | 1-6 | 1=highest | 6 (Academic, EC, Personal, Recs, Athletic, Interview) | +/- modifiers (e.g., "2+") |
| **Yale** | 1-4 | 1=highest | 2 (Academic, Personal) | Grid plotting with zigzag cutoff |
| **Stanford** | 1-6 | 1=highest | 3 (Academic, EC, Intellectual Vitality) | IV separates passion from achievement |
| **MIT** | 1-5 | **5=highest** (REVERSED!) | 3 (Academic, Activity, Interpersonal) | STEM-specific GPA weighting |
| **UChicago** | Mixed | Complex | 1-6 + A-E + W-Z | Deliberately opaque |
| **Duke** | 30-point | Cumulative | 6 × 5 points | Essays/tests dropped Feb 2024 |
| **UT Austin** | Grid | AI + PAI | Two-index system | Supreme Court precedent |

**CRITICAL WARNING**: MIT's reversed scale (5=best, 1=worst) is a trap for reverse-engineering attempts!

### Harvard's Six-Component 1-6 Scale

| Component | What It Measures | Rating 1 Definition |
|-----------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Academic** | Grades, test scores, rigor, rank | Top 1-2% nationally, 99th percentile SAT |
| **Extracurricular** | Quality and depth of activities | Extensive achievement in specialized domain |
| **Personal** | Character, leadership, interpersonal | Most predictive of admission |
| **Recommendations** | Teacher/counselor letter strength | Exceptional third-party validation |
| **Athletic** | Competition level (recruited athletes) | Division I potential |
| **Interview** | Alumni interview report | Outstanding connection |

**KEY INSIGHT**: Ratings are NOT equally weighted. Personal rating is MORE predictive than academic rating.

### Yale's Two-Dimensional Grid System

1. **Academic Rating (1-4)**: Grades, scores, rigor, "intense love of learning"
2. **Personal Rating (1-4)**: Specialized achievements, leadership, personal qualities
3. **Grid Plotting**: Applicants placed on 2D grid (Academic × Personal)
4. **Zigzag Line**: Drawn across grid to determine cutoff
5. **Above line = Admit, Below line = Deny**
6. **+/- modifiers** (e.g., "1+" or "2-") flag borderline cases

### Stanford's Intellectual Vitality Separation

Stanford's innovation: **explicitly separates Intellectual Vitality from academic achievement**

| Dimension | What It Measures |
|-----------|------------------|
| Academic (1-6) | Class rank, rigor, test scores, recommendations |
| Extracurricular (1-6) | Depth of achievement in specialized domains |
| **Intellectual Vitality (1-6)** | "Commitment, dedication, and genuine interest in expanding intellectual horizons" |

**CRITICAL QUOTE**: "Many students with Academic 1 ratings (perfect academics) achieve LOW Intellectual Vitality ratings (5-6) because they pursue grades to gain admission rather than from intrinsic passion for learning."

**The IV essay is "used to weed out countless 4.0 students who lack a true love of learning."**

### MIT's Reverse-Scale System

| Dimension | Scale | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Academic | 1-5 (5=best) | STEM-specific GPA weighted |
| Activity | 1-5 (5=best) | EC vs co-curricular balance |
| Interpersonal | 1-5 (5=best) | "Hardest category to get 4-5 in" |

**Ideal profile**: 5-5-5
**Most admitted**: 5-4-4
**Rare admits**: 4-4-4 (exceptional in one domain)

## SECTION 5.3: HOW RATINGS TRANSLATE TO DECISIONS

### The Harvard SFFA Data: Quantified Admission Probability

From SFFA v. Harvard litigation (2014-2019 actual admissions data):

| Overall Rating | Admission Probability | Interpretation |
|----------------|----------------------|----------------|
| **1** | 90-98% | "Exceptional — clear admit with very strong support" |
| **2** | 50-90% | "Strong credentials but not quite tops" |
| **3** | 20-40% | "Solid contenders" |
| **4** | **~2%** | "Neutral—respectable credentials" (effectively denial) |
| **5-6** | <1% | Functionally rejection |

### THE THRESHOLD EFFECT (CRITICAL)

> The difference between a "3" and a "4" represents a drop from 20-40% admission to 2% admission—a **catastrophic shift** based on a single rating level.

This suggests a **CUTOFF MODEL** where:
- Above threshold → Discussed, potentially admitted
- Below threshold → Proceeds almost automatically to rejection

### The Primacy of Personal Ratings

**KEY FINDING from SFFA statistical analysis:**

> "Personal ratings prove MORE PREDICTIVE of admission than academic ratings when controlling for other factors."

> "An applicant with top personal rating and moderate academic rating (1 personal, 3 academic) achieves HIGHER admission rates than the inverse profile."

**This contradicts the public narrative that academics are paramount.**

### Yale's Grid-Based Decision Mechanism

1. **Plot applicants**: Each gets Academic (1-4) and Personal (1-4) ratings
2. **Draw cutoff line**: Zigzag line position varies by pool strength and capacity
3. **Sort by line**: Above = presumed admit, Below = presumed deny
4. **Discussion zone**: Area around the line where committee deliberation matters

> "Students in the upper left corner [high AI, high PAI] are seen as the most prepared and likely to succeed."

**The line is NOT fixed year-to-year** - it shifts based on applicant pool strength.

### Committee Voting Patterns

**Yale voting threshold:**
- **3+ affirmative votes** → Guaranteed admission consideration
- **2+ negative votes** → Automatic denial

> "Any applicant with more than a total of two reject and/or wait-list votes was automatically denied"

**Deliberation impact:**
> "Research on medical school committees shows that deliberation changes voting in approximately **46% of applications with initial vote disagreement.**"

## SECTION 5.4: ACADEMIC INDEX AND HIDDEN HIERARCHY

### Academic Index: Origins and Truth

**The AI was designed for ATHLETIC RECRUITMENT REGULATION, not general admissions!**

**Original Formula (1950s Ivy League):**
- Class Rank Score (0-80)
- SAT I converted (0-80)
- SAT Subject Tests average (0-80)
- **Total: 60-240 scale**

**Updated Post-2021 Formula:**
- Class Rank/GPA (0-80)
- SAT EBRW (0-80)
- SAT Math (0-80)

**Key Benchmarks:**
- AI 240 = Theoretical maximum (valedictorian + perfect SAT)
- AI 176 = Historical Ivy minimum for recruited athletes (~1140 SAT, 3.0 GPA)

**CRITICAL MISCONCEPTION:**

Michele Hernandez's 1997 book "A is for Admission" popularized the Academic Index, "suggesting it was more central to general admissions than it actually is."

**TRUTH**: "Personal ratings, essays, recommendations substantially OUTWEIGH AI scores in differentiating among qualified applicants."

### The Hidden Weight Hierarchy (REVEALED)

| Tier | Component | Function | Reality |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Tier 1** | Academic | **THRESHOLD** | Once 1400+ SAT, top 10%, diminishing returns |
| **Tier 2** | Personal Rating | **DIFFERENTIATOR** | Most predictive among qualified candidates |
| **Tier 3** | EC + Intellectual Vitality | **HIGH** | "Spike" detection, genuine passion |
| **Tier 4** | Recommendations + Essays | **MEDIUM-HIGH** | Validation but gameable |
| **Tier 5** | Interviews | **LOWER** | Minimal independent contribution |

**KEY INSIGHT**: "A student with 4.0 GPA and 1580 SAT competes on roughly equal footing with a 3.9 GPA, 1520 SAT applicant at most elite schools."

### Personal Rating Character Traits (from SFFA Discovery)

Harvard explicitly evaluates:

| Trait | Definition |
|-------|------------|
| **Likability** | "Very attractive person to be with" in residential community |
| **Courage** | Risk-taking, resilience, willingness to challenge conventional thinking |
| **Kindness** | Empathy, leadership that elevates peers (not dominates) |
| **Being Widely Respected** | Peer recognition, mentor respect, community standing |
| **Positive Personality** | Demeanor, humor, authenticity |

**Harvard admissions director admission during SFFA**: The criteria are "not terribly helpful" - acknowledging the subjectivity.

### Red Flags That DAMAGE Personal Ratings

| Red Flag | Impact |
|----------|--------|
| Academic dishonesty | Near-certain rejection (non-negotiable) |
| Ghostwritten/AI essays | Entire application authenticity questioned |
| Inconsistent information | Credibility concern |
| Disciplinary history | Poor judgment signal |
| Problematic social media | Hate speech, illegal activity |
| Negative essay tone | Blame, complaints, entitlement |
| Unprofessional communication | Pushy emails, demanding treatment |
| Rec letter contradictions | Honesty/self-awareness concern |

## Special Cases: Institutional Priority Modifications

### STEM Major Applicants

> "Female applicants to STEM programs receive, on average, HIGHER dossier and interview ratings than male peers, yet still experience LOWER admission rates due to heavy weighting of major-specific test performance."

Holistic reviewers recognize female STEM applicants' qualitative strengths, but quantitative testing disadvantages remain outcome-determinative.

### Recruited Athletes

- Athletic recruitment operates through parallel process
- AI minimum (176) ensures baseline academic standards
- Committee accommodates athletic department preferences

### Geographic Diversity

- Rural/underrepresented area applicants receive contextual advantage
- Elite colleges cluster applications from affluent metros
- Affirmative action for underrepresented regions (informal)

### Institutional Needs

- Excess-application programs (humanities) → Higher bars
- Fewer-applicant programs (some engineering, languages) → Lower bars
