## Overview

Hackathons and competitive programming demonstrate technical skills, creativity, teamwork, and the ability to perform under pressure. These activities are particularly valued by universities with strong CS/engineering programs.

> "Universities want students who can operate well under pressure, who can meet tight deadlines when needed, and who seek out academic challenges for the fun of it. Hackathons demonstrate all of these traits and more."
> — Ivy Scholars

**Key Insight:** Competition rankings matter in competitive programming (USACO), but hackathons value innovation and execution. Both require tangible proof - working projects and advancement records.

## USA Computing Olympiad (USACO)

### What It Is

The premier competitive programming competition for pre-college students in the United States.

- **Format:** 4 online contests per year (December, January, February, March)
- **Languages:** C++, Java, Python
- **Duration:** 4 hours per contest
- **Topics:** Algorithms, data structures, problem-solving

### Division Structure

| Division | Level | Approximate Skill |
|----------|-------|-------------------|
| **Bronze** | Entry | Basic programming |
| **Silver** | Intermediate | Basic algorithms, data structures |
| **Gold** | Advanced | Advanced algorithms, graphs, DP |
| **Platinum** | Expert | Complex algorithms, optimization |
| **Camp** | Elite | Top ~24 students invited to summer camp |
| **IOI Team** | Top 4 | Represents USA internationally |

### Selectivity Context

> "There are ten times more students who pass the AP Computer Science A exam than pass USACO Bronze! Reaching USACO Gold would put you in the top couple thousand United States computer science students in your grade level."
> — Breakout Mentors

### Achievement Tier Classification

| Tier | Achievement | Selectivity | Admissions Impact |
|------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | IOI Team USA (Top 4) | 4 students nationally | Exceptional - actively recruited |
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | USACO Camp Invitee | ~24 students | Exceptional |
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Platinum Division | Top ~200-300 | Exceptional |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | Gold Division | Top ~1,000 | Very strong |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | Silver Division (late) | Top few thousand | Strong |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | Silver Division | Many students | Solid - shows dedication |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | Bronze promoted to Silver | Above average | Solid |
| **Tier 4 (Basic)** | Bronze Division only | Entry level | Shows interest |

### USACO Path to IOI

1. Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum (via contest promotion)
2. Top Platinum performers invited to **Summer Camp**
3. Camp participants compete for **IOI Team** selection
4. Team of 4 represents USA at International Olympiad in Informatics

### College Recognition

> "Securing gold or platinum awards in the USACO competition greatly enhances one's college application prospects. Top-tier U.S. universities, such as MIT, Harvard, and Carnegie Mellon, highly favor students who excel and win awards in USACO competitions."
> — Inspirit AI

**Career Impact:** USACO alumni frequently hired at Google, Meta, Microsoft, and top tech companies. Competitive programming experience provides significant advantage in technical interviews.

## Major League Hacking (MLH)

### What It Is

The official student hackathon league, powering 200+ hackathons annually with 150,000+ participants.

- **Season:** July through June annually
- **Events:** 200+ hackathons worldwide
- **Scope:** Beginner to veteran hackers

### MLH Season Rankings

Schools earn points based on student performance at MLH Member Events. Top schools compete for the MLH Season trophy.

**Historically Strong Schools:**
- Thomas Jefferson High School
- West Windsor-Plainsboro High School
- Bergen County Academies

### Achievement Tier Classification (MLH Hackathons)

| Tier | Achievement | Admissions Impact |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | MLH Season School Champion | Exceptional team achievement |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | 1st Place at Major MLH Hackathon | Strong |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | Multiple hackathon wins | Strong pattern |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | Category/Sponsor Prize Winner | Solid |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | Consistent hackathon participation (5+) | Shows commitment |
| **Tier 4 (Basic)** | Single hackathon participation | Shows interest |

## Congressional App Challenge

### What It Is

Official U.S. House of Representatives competition encouraging students to create apps addressing local/national issues.

- **Eligibility:** Middle and high school students
- **Scope:** Congressional district competitions
- **Recognition:** Winners displayed in U.S. Capitol

### Achievement Tier Classification

| Tier | Achievement | Admissions Impact |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | National Recognition / Congress Display | Strong - civic recognition |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | District Winner | Strong |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | District Finalist | Solid |
| **Tier 4 (Basic)** | Participant | Shows initiative |

### Benefits

- App displayed at U.S. Capitol
- Recognition from Congressional representative
- Civic-minded technology application
- Strong essay material for "impact" narratives

> "If you care about civic recognition, the Congressional App Challenge is gold."
> — Lumiere Education

## Programming Projects & Open Source

### What Counts as Strong

| Achievement | Tier | Evidence Required |
|-------------|------|-------------------|
| **Open source contribution to major project** | Tier 2 | Merged PRs to React, Linux, etc. |
| **GitHub project with 1,000+ stars** | Tier 2 | Verifiable GitHub profile |
| **App/Website with significant users** | Tier 2-3 | Usage metrics |
| **Personal projects with documentation** | Tier 3-4 | GitHub portfolio |
| **Tutorial/learning project** | Tier 4 | Shows learning |

### What Makes CS Projects Stand Out

1. **Real Users** - People actually use your creation
2. **Scale** - Thousands of users/downloads/stars
3. **Open Source Contribution** - Participating in larger community
4. **Technical Complexity** - Non-trivial engineering
5. **Impact** - Solves a real problem

### What Judges Look For

1. **Problem-Solution Fit** - Clear problem being solved
2. **Technical Implementation** - Working prototype
3. **Innovation** - Novel approach

5. **Polish** - Functional demo

## Quick Reference: Tier Summary

### Tier 1 (National/Elite) - Top 0.1-1%

- USACO Camp Invitee / Platinum Division
- IOI Team USA
- HackMIT/PennApps Grand Prize Winner

## Red Flags

### Competition-Related

- Claims USACO participation without specifying division
- Lists "hackathon participant" without any projects or outcomes
- Can't explain technical implementation of winning project
- Multiple "wins" with no verifiable evidence

### Project-Related

- Claims large user base without verifiable metrics
- GitHub profile with only forked repositories
- "CEO of tech startup" with no users or revenue
- Cannot demonstrate working project
