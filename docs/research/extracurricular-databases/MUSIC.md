**Purpose:** Comprehensive evaluation criteria for music achievements in college admissions, covering classical, jazz, contemporary, and composition pathways.

**System Usage:** Query when evaluating music-related activities, competition results, or conservatory/university music program applications.

## Overview: Music in College Admissions

### How Music Matters

| Context | Admissions Impact |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Conservatory applicant** | Audition is primary; music IS the application |
| **University music major** | Strong music + academics; audition matters |
| **Non-music major with exceptional talent** | Hook for liberal arts schools |
| **Strong musician, not pursuing major** | Excellent activity, shows dedication |
| **Casual musician** | Shows interest, limited distinction |

> "We're looking for students who will enrich our musical community—whether they major in music or not. The kid who will play in our orchestra while majoring in chemistry is valuable."
> — Admissions Officer, Liberal Arts College

### Pathways in Music

| Path | Profile |
|------|---------|
| **Conservatory** | 100% music focus; audition-based admission |
| **University music school** | Music major with broader education; balance of audition and academics |
| **University music minor** | Music as secondary focus; ensemble participation |
| **Liberal arts + music** | Music as extracurricular; enriches campus life |

## Music in Application Strategy

### For Non-Music Major Applicants

**When music helps most:**
- Genuine, long-term dedication (8+ years)
- Significant achievement level (Tier 1-2)
- Would actively contribute to campus music
- Connects to other interests or personal story

**How to position:**
- Lead activities list with music if primary commitment
- Reference specific achievements (All-State, competitions)
- Mention plans for campus involvement
- Show depth, not just participation

### For Music Major Applicants

**Conservatory applications:**
- Pre-screening recordings (excerpts, full works)
- Live audition (45-90 minutes)
- Often include theory/ear training assessment
- Some require piano proficiency

**University music school applications:**
- Audition requirements vary
- Academic credentials matter more than conservatory
- Often balance audition (60%) with academics (40%)

### Writing About Music

**In essays:**
- Focus on WHY music matters, not just WHAT you've done
- Connect to broader identity or values
- Show insight, not just description
- Avoid clichés ("Music is my life")

**In activities:**
- Be specific about repertoire level
- Quantify achievements (All-State 3 years, hours/week)
- Include leadership and teaching
- List significant performances separately

## Red Flags in Music Profiles

| Red Flag | Concern |
|----------|---------|
| **Can't name pieces or teachers** | Authenticity question |
| **Claims high level but no competitions/programs** | Unverified ability |
| **"Been playing for X years" without achievement** | Passive participation |
| **Quit before senior year** | Questions commitment |
| **Only school ensembles, no outside study** | Limited dedication |
| **Claims professional gigs but can't verify** | Misrepresentation |

### What Admissions Officers Can Verify

- Competition results (public records)
- Program participation (contact programs)
- Teacher credentials (professional networks)
- Recordings submitted (quality assessment)

## Music-Specific Application Components

### Pre-Screening Recordings

**What's typically required:**
- 10-15 minute selection
- Specific repertoire requirements per instrument
- Technical requirements (contrasting periods, unaccompanied Bach, etc.)
- Video with visible hands/embouchure

**Quality standards:**
- Professional recording preferred
- Clear audio, stable video
- No editing between takes
- Appropriate dress/setting

### Live Audition Tips

**Preparation:**
- Know requirements exactly (time limits, required pieces)
- Prepare beyond minimum (have backup pieces ready)
- Practice performing, not just playing
- Have accompanist ready (if applicable)

**Day of:**
- Warm up early and adequately
- Dress professionally but comfortably
- Be prepared to start with any piece
- Take breath before starting; don't rush
