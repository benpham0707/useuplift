## Overview

Entrepreneurship demonstrates initiative, creativity, problem-solving, and leadership - qualities highly valued at elite universities. However, **authenticity is critical** - admissions officers can distinguish between legitimate ventures and "resume padding."

> "The word entrepreneur is synonymous with a successful and motivated applicant. Admissions committees tend to see students who run a successful business in high school as more experienced."
> — Ivy Coach

**Key Insight:** Story and genuine impact matter more than revenue figures. A small business with authentic personal growth can be more compelling than a manufactured "startup."

## Self-Started Businesses

### How Colleges Evaluate Student Businesses

Admissions officers assess entrepreneurial ventures through several lenses:

### Evaluation Framework

| Factor | What They Look For |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Authenticity** | Is this a real business or resume padding? |
| **Commitment** | How much time/effort invested? |
| **Impact** | Customers served? Revenue? Social impact? |
| **Growth** | How did the business develop over time? |
| **Learning** | What did you gain from the experience? |
| **Story** | Is there a compelling narrative? |

### Achievement Tier Classification

| Tier | Achievement | Indicators |
|------|-------------|------------|
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Significant business with external validation | $50K+ revenue, outside funding, media coverage, significant user base |
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Acquired or scaled business | Sold company, raised angel/VC, 10,000+ users |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | Established profitable business | $5K-50K revenue, paying customers, 1+ years operating |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | Competition-winning business | Won Diamond Challenge, Conrad, etc. |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | Social enterprise with measurable impact | 500+ people served, quantified outcomes |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | Small business with real customers | Some revenue, actual customer base, 6+ months |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | Side project with traction | Website, users, but limited scale |
| **Tier 4 (Basic)** | "Business" without customers/revenue | Idea only, no execution |
| **Red Flag** | Fake or inflated claims | No website, no verifiable customers |

### Red Flags Admissions Officers Watch For

> "If your so-called 'business' is a passion project that you commit a few hours a week to and call it a day, don't make it the focus of your application. Admissions officers can tell whether your business is a legitimate enterprise."
> — Ivy Coach

1. **No website or digital presence** - Legitimate businesses have verifiable presence
2. **Vague descriptions** - Can't explain customers, revenue, or operations
3. **Inflated titles** - "CEO of a startup" with no employees or revenue
4. **No duration** - Started a month before applications
5. **No evolution** - Business hasn't grown or changed

### What Makes Student Entrepreneurship Stand Out

1. **Problem-Solution Fit** - Clear problem being solved
2. **Customer Validation** - Real people paying or using product
3. **Iteration** - Evidence of learning and pivoting
4. **Scale Attempts** - Tried to grow beyond initial version
5. **Personal Story** - Authentic connection to the problem
6. **Mentorship** - Sought guidance from business professionals

## Quick Reference: Tier Summary

### Tier 1 (National/Elite) - Top 1%

- DECA/FBLA National Champion (1st place)
- Diamond Challenge/Conrad Challenge Grand Prize Winner
- Business with $50K+ revenue or external funding
- Featured in major media for business success
- Wharton LBW attendee with notable achievement

### Tier 2 (Excellent) - Top 2-15%

- DECA ICDC Finalist / FBLA NLC Finalist
- State Champion in major event
- Diamond/Conrad Finalist
- Business with verified revenue and customers
- State/National officer of business organization
- Competition-winning business plan

### Tier 3 (Good) - Top 15-40%

- ICDC/NLC Qualifier
- State Finalist
- Regional competition winner
- Small business with some traction
- Chapter President with active involvement
- Summer business program participant

### Tier 4 (Basic) - Majority

- DECA/FBLA member without competition success
- "Business" without customers or revenue
- Competition participant without advancement

## Red Flags

### Competition-Related

- Lists DECA/FBLA membership without any competition results
- Claims national ranking but can't specify event or placement
- Multiple years of participation with no progression

### Business-Related

- "CEO" or "Founder" title with no verifiable business
- Claims revenue/impact but can't provide specifics
- Business started right before application deadline
- No website, social media, or digital presence
- Cannot explain business model clearly
