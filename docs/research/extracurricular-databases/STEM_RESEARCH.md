# Extracurricular Achievement Database: STEM RESEARCH & COMPETITIONS

**Category:** Academic Competition / Research / Science
**Last Updated:** January 2026
**Usage:** System can query this file specifically for STEM research and science competition evaluation

---

## Overview

STEM research achievements are among the most highly-valued extracurriculars at elite universities. However, **authenticity and quality matter enormously** - pay-to-play programs and superficial research can actually harm applications.

> "Evidence of substantial scholarship can elevate an applicant."
> — Harvard University filing in SFFA lawsuit

**Key Insight:** The hierarchy is clear - legitimate peer-reviewed research and prestigious competition awards carry far more weight than participation in paid research programs.

## Regeneron Science Talent Search (STS)

### What It Is

The most prestigious pre-college science research competition in the United States, often called the "Junior Nobel Prize."

- **Founded:** 1942 (as Westinghouse STS)
- **Sponsor History:** Westinghouse (1942-1998), Intel (1999-2016), Regeneron (2017-present)
- **Status:** Nation's oldest and most prestigious science competition

### Statistics

| Metric | Number |
|--------|--------|
| Annual Applicants | ~2,500 |
| Top 300 Scholars | 300 (~12% acceptance) |
| Top 40 Finalists | 40 (~1.6% acceptance) |
| Total Prizes | $3.1 million annually |

### Achievement Tier Classification

| Tier | Achievement | Selectivity | Admissions Impact |
|------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Top 10 Winner ($40K-$250K) | 10 of 2,500 (0.4%) | Exceptional - actively recruited by HYPSM |
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Top 40 Finalist | 40 of 2,500 (~1.6%) | Exceptional |
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Top 300 Scholar | 300 of 2,500 (~12%) | Very strong signal |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | Submitted qualifying project | Completed research | Shows initiative |

### Notable Alumni Impact

- 13 Nobel Prize winners
- 20 MacArthur Fellowship recipients

- 7 Breakthrough Prize winners
- 2 Fields Medalists

### Top Feeder Schools

## Regeneron ISEF (International Science and Engineering Fair)

### What It Is

The world's largest pre-college science competition, serving as the culmination of regional/national science fairs globally.

- **Participants:** ~1,800 finalists from ~75 countries
- **Feeder System:** ~7 million students participate in affiliated fairs globally
- **Prize Pool:** Over $8 million annually

| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Best of Category Winner | ~22 of 1,800 | Exceptional |
| **Tier 1 (Elite)** | Grand Award Winner (1st-4th place by category) | ~88 of 1,800 (~5%) | Exceptional |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | ISEF Finalist (competed at Worlds) | 1,800 of 7 million (~0.03%) | Very strong |
| **Tier 2 (Excellent)** | Special Award Winner | Varies | Strong |
| **Tier 3 (Good)** | State/Regional Fair Winner | Qualified for ISEF | Solid |
| **Tier 4 (Basic)** | Local science fair participant | Majority | Baseline |

### Special Recognition

- MIT's Lincoln Laboratory names asteroids after ISEF winners (Ceres Connection program since 2001)
- Dudley R. Herschbach SIYSS Award: Trip to Nobel Prize ceremonies in Stockholm

- **Participation:** ~250,000 students on 5,000+ teams in all 50 states

## Research Publications

### Publication Hierarchy

| Type | Description | Admissions Weight |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Peer-Reviewed Journal (High Impact)** | Nature, Science, Cell, etc. | Tier 1 - Exceptional (extremely rare for HS) |
| **Peer-Reviewed Journal (Indexed)** | PubMed-indexed, impact factor journals | Tier 1-2 - Very strong |
| **Conference Proceedings** | Presented at academic conferences | Tier 2 - Strong |
| **The Concord Review** | Most prestigious HS history journal | Tier 2 - "Like winning a national math contest" (Harvard Dean) |
| **Legitimate HS Journals** | Young Scientists Journal, etc. | Tier 3 - Solid |
| **Preprint Servers** | arXiv, bioRxiv (not peer-reviewed) | Tier 3-4 - Shows work, not validation |
| **Pay-to-Publish Journals** | No real peer review | **RED FLAG** - Can hurt application |

### The Concord Review (Humanities Research)

The most prestigious publication outlet for high school humanities research.

| Metric | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Acceptance Rate** | 5-8% (~45 of ~900 submissions) |
| **Founded** | 1987 |
| **Average Paper Length** | ~8,500 words |
| **Submission Fee** | $70-150 |
| **Papers Published From** | 46 countries |

**College Outcomes of Concord Review Authors:**
- 152 to Harvard
- 123 to Yale
- 86 to Stanford
- 76 to Princeton
- 35 to Brown
- 31 to Columbia

> "Having a paper accepted for publication by The Concord Review is viewed very favorably by university admissions committees, with the Dean of Admissions for Harvard College likening it to winning a national mathematics contest."
> — CollegeBase

### Publication Red Flags

**Warning signs that can HARM applications:**

1. **Pay-to-publish journals** - Admissions officers recognize these
2. **No peer review process** - Just submission fees
3. **Journal quality misrepresentation** - Claiming "peer-reviewed" when not
4. **Mismatch with profile** - Publication in field unrelated to interests
5. **Questionable authorship** - First author on complex research without lab experience

> "Admissions officers are now honing in on the authenticity of students' academic pursuits, particularly looking for indicators that a student's publication aligns with their overall academic profile, coursework, and genuine interest."
> — ProPublica

### Publication Best Practices

1. **Quality over publication** - Strong unpublished research > weak publication
2. **Mentor credentials matter** - Legitimate research advisor adds credibility
3. **Integration with narrative** - Publication should fit overall application story
4. **Honest representation** - Accurately describe your role and contributions
5. **Well-documented research** - Even unpublished, detailed research shows initiative

## Lab Research Experience

### Hierarchy of Research Experiences

| Type | Description | Admissions Weight |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Published Co-Author** | Named on peer-reviewed paper | Tier 1-2 |
| **Multi-Year Lab Member** | Sustained research (2+ years) | Tier 2 |
| **Selective Program Participant** | RSI, Clark Scholars, Garcia, etc. | Tier 1-2 |
| **University Lab Summer Intern** | Competitive/mentored position | Tier 2-3 |
| **Independent Research Project** | Self-directed with results | Tier 2-3 |
| **Paid Research Program** | Lumiere, Polygence, etc. | Tier 3-4 (authenticity concerns) |
| **School Science Fair** | Local/regional fair only | Tier 4 |

### What Makes Research Stand Out

1. **Duration** - Multi-year commitment > one summer
2. **Independence** - Own project direction vs. assigned tasks
3. **Results** - Publications, presentations, competitions
4. **Mentor quality** - University professor > graduate student > paid program
5. **Specificity** - Can explain methodology and findings in detail
6. **Connection** - Relates to intended major/career

## Red Flags

### Research Red Flags

- First author on complex paper without lab background
- Publication in pay-to-publish journal presented as peer-reviewed
- Cannot explain methodology or findings when asked
- Research topic completely disconnected from other interests
- Multiple "publications" in rapid succession from paid programs

### Competition Red Flags

- Lists "Olympiad participant" without specifying level reached
- Claims "national competition" for regional event
- High hours claimed with no advancement or awards
- Cannot discuss specific problems solved or techniques used
