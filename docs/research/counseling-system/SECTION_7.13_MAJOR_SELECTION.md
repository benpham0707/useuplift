# Section 7.13: Major Selection Strategy

**Purpose:** Guidance for selecting, declaring, and strategically positioning intended major for college admissions, including understanding impacted majors and school-specific considerations.

**System Usage:** Query to provide major selection advice based on student interests, achievements, and target schools.

**Last Updated:** January 2026

---

## Overview: How Major Choice Affects Admissions

> "Your intended major doesn't just affect what you study—at many schools, it significantly affects your chances of admission."
> — Former Cornell Admissions Officer

### The Three Realities

| Reality | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Major affects admissions** | At most schools, some majors are more competitive |
| **Authenticity matters** | Don't game the system; they'll know |
| **Flexibility exists** | Many schools allow changes after enrollment |

## Impacted Majors at Public Universities

### Definition

**Impacted** = More qualified applicants than spaces available

### Most Commonly Impacted Majors

| Major | Why Impacted |
|-------|--------------|
| Computer Science | Extremely high demand, limited capacity |
| Engineering (most types) | High demand, lab/equipment limits |
| Business | High demand |
| Nursing | Clinical placement limits |
| Pre-Med (Bio/Chem) | High demand |
| Communications | Popular, limited seats |

### Impacted Major Strategy

**If your intended major is impacted:**

1. **Be honest about interest** - Don't fake passion
2. **Show preparation** - Relevant coursework, activities
3. **Have backup plan** - Similar major you'd also enjoy
4. **Apply to multiple schools** - Don't rely on one impacted program
5. **Consider alternatives** - Related but less impacted majors

## Choosing Your Intended Major

### The Authenticity Framework

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| What do I genuinely enjoy studying? | Passion comes through in essays |
| What have I done related to this field? | Activities should support major choice |
| What do I read/watch/do voluntarily? | Reveals true interests |
| Can I talk about this for 30 minutes? | Interviews will test authenticity |
| Does my coursework support this? | Transcript should align |

### Major-Activity Alignment

| Intended Major | Supporting Activities |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Computer Science | USACO, hackathons, coding projects, CS research |
| Engineering | Robotics, physics olympiad, building projects |
| Biology/Pre-Med | Science research, hospital volunteering, USABO |
| Political Science | Model UN, debate, campaign work, civic engagement |
| English | Writing publications, literary magazine, Scholastic awards |
| Economics | DECA/FBLA, business activities, econ research |
| Art/Design | Portfolio, art competitions, visual projects |
| Music | Performances, competitions, ensembles |

### Red Flags: Major-Profile Mismatch

| Claimed Major | Red Flag |
|--------------|----------|
| "Computer Science" | No coding activities, no math strength |
| "Pre-Med Biology" | No science activities, no healthcare exposure |
| "English" | No writing samples, no literary involvement |
| "Art" | No portfolio, no art activities |
| "Engineering" | No math/science strength, no building projects |

## Strategic Major Considerations

### When Being "Undeclared" Helps

| Situation | Consider Undeclared |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Genuinely unsure | Authenticity matters |
| Interests span fields | Show breadth |
| Target school doesn't admit by major | No penalty |
| Would strengthen application | Some schools less competitive for undeclared |

### When Being "Undeclared" Hurts

| Situation | Declare a Major |
|-----------|-----------------|
| School admits by major/college | Cornell, CMU, etc. |
| Strong spike that aligns with major | Coherent narrative |
| Essay requires "why this major" | Need clear answer |
| Applying to specialized programs | Must show commitment |

### The "Less Competitive Major" Strategy

**Can you declare a less competitive major and switch?**

| School Policy | Reality |
|---------------|---------|
| Easy internal transfers | Strategy might work (risky) |
| Difficult internal transfers | Don't do it |
| No transfers allowed | Definitely don't do it |

**Important:** Schools know this strategy. If you declare economics but all your activities are CS, you'll be questioned or rejected.

**Better approach:** Be authentic and apply where you fit.

## The "Why This Major" Essay

### Common Prompts

- "Why do you want to study X?"
- "What experiences have led you to this field?"
- "How will you contribute to our X department?"
- "Describe a problem in X you'd like to solve."

### Strong "Why Major" Structure

**1. Origin Story (25%)**
- Specific moment that sparked interest
- NOT "I've always loved X"
- Personal, concrete, unique

**2. Exploration/Development (40%)**
- What you've done to explore this interest
- Classes, activities, research, reading
- Show depth of engagement

**3. Specific School Connection (20%)**
- Why THIS school for THIS major
- Specific professors, programs, courses
- Show you've researched

**4. Future Vision (15%)**
- Where you see this going
- Be specific but not limiting
- Show ambition and awareness

### Example: Strong vs. Weak

**Weak:**
> "I've always loved science. Biology is fascinating because it explains how life works. I want to study biology at Stanford because it has a great biology program and I could do research."

**Strong:**
> "The summer I turned fourteen, I dissected a frog and noticed the heart still beating. That shouldn't have been possible—the frog was dead. I spent the next week reading about cardiac automaticity, the heart's ability to beat without brain signals. This led me to cardiac electrophysiology, and last year, I shadowed Dr. Martinez at Stanford's Cardiovascular Institute, watching her team develop new arrhythmia treatments. Professor Lewis's research on ion channels would let me explore the questions that started with that frog's heart. I want to understand not just how life works, but why it persists."

## Changing Majors After Enrollment

### Schools with Easy Major Changes

| School | Major Change Policy |
|--------|-------------------|
| MIT | No major until sophomore year; easy switching |
| Stanford | Declare sophomore year; flexible |
| Most liberal arts colleges | Easy to change |
| Brown | Open curriculum; very flexible |

### Schools with Difficult Major Changes

| School | Major Change Difficulty |
|--------|------------------------|
| Cornell | Between colleges: very difficult |
| CMU | Between schools: competitive application |
| UC Berkeley | Into CS/Engineering: extremely difficult |
| UMich | Into Engineering: competitive |

### Internal Transfer Reality

| Target Major | Typical Internal Transfer Success |
|--------------|----------------------------------|
| Computer Science (at any school) | Low (high demand) |
| Engineering (most schools) | Moderate to Low |
| Business (competitive programs) | Low |
| Liberal Arts | Usually easy |
| Sciences | Usually moderate |

## Dual Majors and Interdisciplinary Programs

### When Dual Major Makes Sense

| Combination | Why It Works |
|-------------|--------------|
| CS + Linguistics | Computational linguistics |
| Econ + Math | Quantitative economics |
| Biology + Art | Scientific illustration |
| Engineering + Public Policy | Tech policy |

### Mentioning Dual Interests in Application

**Do:**
- Show how interests connect
- Demonstrate depth in primary area
- Explain the synthesis you're seeking

**Don't:**
- List multiple unconnected majors
- Seem unfocused
- Treat it as "backup"

## Major Choice by Career Path

### If You Know Your Career Goal

| Career Goal | Recommended Majors | Notes |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| Medicine | Biology, Chemistry, Biochemistry, any (just complete prereqs) | Major doesn't matter as much as GPA/MCAT |
| Law | Anything (Philosophy, Political Science, English common) | No required major |
| Investment Banking | Economics, Finance, Math | Prestige of school matters more than major |
| Software Engineering | CS, Engineering, Math, Physics | CS best but others work |
| Research Science | Field-specific (Biology, Chemistry, Physics) | PhD track |
| Management Consulting | Economics, Business, any analytical | School prestige matters |

### If You Don't Know Your Career

| Strategy | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Choose based on genuine interest | You'll do better in what you enjoy |
| Consider versatile majors | Economics, CS, Math offer options |
| Pick a school with flexibility | Can explore and change |
| Don't stress | Most people don't work in their major field |

## Pre-Professional Advice

### Pre-Med Reality

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "Must major in Biology" | Any major works if you complete prereqs |
| "Science majors have advantage" | Non-science majors often have higher MCAT scores |
| "Major matters for med school" | GPA and MCAT matter most |

**Best pre-med strategy:** Major in what you'll get good grades in while completing science prerequisites.

### Pre-Law Reality

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "Must major in Political Science" | Any major works |
| "Pre-Law programs help" | Not really; LSAT and GPA matter |
| "Certain majors look better" | Writing-intensive majors may help LSAT |

**Best pre-law strategy:** Major in something you're passionate about that develops writing and analytical skills.

### Pre-Business Reality

| Path | What Matters |
|------|--------------|
| Investment Banking | School prestige, networking, internships |
| Consulting | School prestige, problem-solving ability |
| General Business | Varied; experience matters |
