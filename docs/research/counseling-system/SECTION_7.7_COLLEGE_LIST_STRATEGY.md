# Section 7.7: College List Strategy & School-Specific Intelligence

**Purpose:** Guide for building a balanced college list and understanding school-specific requirements, preferences, and culture.

**System Usage:** Query by school tier or specific school to provide targeted advice.

**Last Updated:** January 2026

---

## Overview: Building the Right List

> "The best college list isn't about prestige—it's about finding schools where you'll thrive AND that will admit you. Too many students chase rankings and end up rejected everywhere."
> — Former Amherst Admissions Dean

### The Balanced List Framework

| Category | Definition | Recommended Count |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| **Reach** | Admit rate <15% OR below median stats | 2-4 schools |
| **Target** | Admit rate 15-40% AND at/above median stats | 4-6 schools |
| **Likely** | Admit rate >40% AND above median stats | 2-3 schools |

**Total:** 8-12 schools typically optimal

### Common Mistakes

1. **All reaches, no matches** - Ends up rejected or waitlisted everywhere
2. **No genuine interest in safeties** - Unhappy if that's where you go
3. **Chasing rankings only** - Ignoring fit and culture
4. **Applying to 20+ schools** - Diluted applications, essay burnout
5. **Ignoring financial realities** - Acceptance doesn't mean affordability

# BUILDING YOUR LIST

## Step 1: Academic Fit Assessment

### GPA/Test Score Matrix

| Your Stats (relative to school median) | Category |
|---------------------------------------|----------|
| Above 75th percentile | Likely |
| Between 25th-75th percentile | Target |
| Below 25th percentile | Reach |

### Other Academic Factors
- Course rigor compared to school expectations
- Intended major competitiveness
- Fit with school's academic style (Core vs. Open Curriculum)

## Step 2: Personal Fit Assessment

### Questions to Ask

1. **Size:** Do I want large (10,000+) or small (<5,000)?
2. **Location:** Urban, suburban, rural? Region preferences?
3. **Culture:** Collaborative or competitive? Greek life? Athletics?
4. **Weather:** Does climate matter to me?
5. **Distance from home:** How far is comfortable?
6. **Housing:** Guaranteed? Residential college system?

### Creating a Fit Profile

| Factor | My Preference | Schools That Match |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|
| Size | Small-medium | Duke, Dartmouth, Brown |
| Location | Urban | Columbia, Penn, NYU |
| Culture | Collaborative | MIT, Harvey Mudd |
| Research | Important | Any R1 university |

## Step 3: Financial Reality Check

### Net Price Calculator

**Every school has one.** Use it BEFORE finalizing your list.

### Financial Aid Policies

| Policy | Schools | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Need-blind for all | HYPSM, Amherst, MIT | Won't consider ability to pay |
| Need-blind domestic only | Most T30 | International students affected |
| Meet full need | Most T20 | Will meet calculated need |
| Merit-based aid | Some T30, most state schools | Based on stats, not need |

### Merit Aid vs. Need-Based

| School Type | Typical Aid |
|-------------|-------------|
| Ivy League | Need-based only |
| Top LACs | Often need-based only |
| State schools | Merit + need |
| Some T20-30 | Both available |

# EARLY APPLICATION STRATEGY

## Types of Early Applications

| Type | Binding? | # You Can Submit | Schools Offering |
|------|----------|------------------|------------------|
| **ED (Early Decision)** | Yes, binding | 1 | Most private |
| **ED2 (Early Decision 2)** | Yes, binding | 1 (after ED denied) | Some schools |
| **EA (Early Action)** | No | Multiple | Some schools |
| **REA/SCEA** | No, but restricting | 1 | Harvard, Yale, Stanford, Princeton |

## ED Strategy

### When to Apply ED

**Apply ED if:**
- School is clear #1 choice
- You're happy to commit if accepted
- Financial aid is either not needed OR school meets full need
- Your application is ready by November

**Don't apply ED if:**
- Need to compare financial aid packages
- Application isn't your best work yet
- Applying strategically without genuine preference
- Test scores still improving

### ED Boost by School

| School | Estimated ED Advantage |
|--------|----------------------|
| UPenn | Very significant (~50% of class from ED) |
| Duke | Significant |
| Northwestern | Significant |
| Cornell | Moderate |
| Columbia | Moderate |
| Brown | Moderate |

**Note:** At most schools, ED applicants do have higher admit rates, but this is partly because ED pools are self-selecting (strongest applicants apply ED).

## REA/SCEA Strategy

### Restrictions

| School | What's Allowed | What's NOT Allowed |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| Harvard (REA) | Public EA, international EA | Other private EA/ED |
| Yale (SCEA) | Public EA, international EA | Other private EA |
| Stanford (REA) | Public EA, international EA | Other private EA/ED |
| Princeton (SCEA) | Public EA, international EA | Other private EA |

### When to Use REA/SCEA

**Best for:**
- Top choice is one of these schools
- No other private school you'd commit to ED
- Strong applicant who might get in

**Consider skipping if:**
- You'd rather ED somewhere else
- Need to maximize early options

# FINAL LIST CHECKLIST

## Before Submitting

### Academic Verification
- [ ] All schools are academically realistic
- [ ] At least 2-3 schools are genuine targets/likelies
- [ ] Intended major is offered (and not impacted) at each school

### Personal Fit Verification
- [ ] Would genuinely attend any school on the list
- [ ] Have visited (in person or virtually) top choices
- [ ] Understand culture of each school
- [ ] Can articulate specific "why" for each school

### Financial Verification
- [ ] Run net price calculator for each school
- [ ] Understand aid policy (need-based vs. merit)
- [ ] Have discussed budget with family
- [ ] At least 2-3 affordable options

### Application Quality
- [ ] Supplements are school-specific, not generic
- [ ] "Why This School" essays are genuine
- [ ] Have thoroughly researched each school
