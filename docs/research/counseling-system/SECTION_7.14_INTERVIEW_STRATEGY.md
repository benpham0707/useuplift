# Section 7.14: Interview Preparation & Strategy

**Purpose:** Comprehensive guide to preparing for, performing in, and following up on college admissions interviews across all formats and school types.

**System Usage:** Query by school or interview type to provide targeted preparation advice.

**Last Updated:** January 2026

---

## Overview: The Role of Interviews

> "The interview rarely gets someone admitted. But it can keep someone out. A terrible interview raises questions about everything else in the application."
> — Former Yale Admissions Officer

### Interview Impact by School Type

| School Type | Interview Weight | Format |
|-------------|-----------------|--------|
| **HYPSM** | Informational/modest | Alumni |
| **Other Ivies** | Moderate | Alumni |
| **Top LACs** | Moderate to significant | Alumni or staff |
| **Georgetown, MIT** | Evaluative/significant | Alumni (Georgetown) or staff |
| **UCs, State schools** | Typically none | N/A |
| **Scholarship interviews** | Very significant | Committee/staff |

### What Interviews Can Do

| Can Help With | Can't Help With |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Demonstrating communication skills | Overcoming weak academics |
| Showing personality beyond essays | Making up for missing achievements |
| Explaining circumstances | Creating "wow factor" you don't have |
| Demonstrating genuine interest | Compensating for poor essays |
| Clarifying confusing application parts | Guaranteeing admission |

## Interview Formats

### Alumni Interviews

**What they are:** Conversations with graduates living in your area

| Aspect | Reality |
|--------|---------|
| **Training** | Usually minimal (1-2 hour session) |
| **Standard questions** | Often have suggested questions, but vary |
| **Where** | Coffee shop, library, video call |
| **Duration** | 30-60 minutes typically |
| **Report** | Brief written summary to admissions |
| **Weight** | Generally modest in final decision |

**Interviewer perspective:**
- Volunteers with full-time jobs
- Want to help the school, genuinely curious about you
- Not trying to "trick" you
- Remember they were once applicants too

### Staff/Admissions Officer Interviews

**What they are:** Conversations with professional admissions staff

| Aspect | Reality |
|--------|---------|
| **Training** | Extensive professional training |
| **Evaluation** | More structured and evaluative |
| **Where** | Campus, video call, admissions events |
| **Duration** | 20-45 minutes |
| **Report** | Detailed notes in your file |
| **Weight** | Can be significant |

**Schools using staff interviews:**
- MIT (optional but recommended)
- Georgetown (required for most)
- Some LACs (Middlebury, Bowdoin for visitors)
- Scholarship programs

### Group Interviews

**What they are:** Multiple applicants interviewed together

| Aspect | Reality |
|--------|---------|
| **Purpose** | See how you interact with others |
| **Key skills** | Collaboration, listening, building on others' ideas |
| **Dangers** | Dominating, interrupting, staying silent |
| **Where** | Some scholarships, some programs |

**Strategy:**
- Contribute without dominating (speak 25-30% of time in group of 4)
- Build on others' comments ("Building on what Sarah said...")
- Ask good questions to groupmates
- Show genuine interest in others

### Virtual Interviews

**What they are:** Video calls via Zoom, Skype, etc.

| Technical Setup | Importance |
|-----------------|------------|
| **Camera** | Eye level, stable, good quality |
| **Lighting** | Face well-lit (light source in front, not behind) |
| **Background** | Clean, neutral, professional |
| **Audio** | Clear, no echo, minimal background noise |
| **Internet** | Stable connection, wired if possible |
| **Backup plan** | Phone number in case of technical issues |

**Virtual-specific tips:**
- Look at camera (not screen) for "eye contact"
- Dress fully professional (not just top half)
- Close other applications, silence phone
- Have notes visible off-camera if needed
- Practice with the specific platform beforehand

## Preparation Framework

### The 10-Category Preparation Method

**Prepare 2-3 talking points for each:**

#### 1. About You
- Your story in 2 minutes (not resume recitation)
- What makes you unique
- Core values and personality

#### 2. Academic Interests
- Favorite subjects and why
- Intellectual questions that fascinate you
- How you learn best

#### 3. Extracurricular Depth
- Your main activity and what you've learned
- Leadership experiences
- Impact you've had

#### 4. Why This School
- 3 specific reasons (not rankings)
- Programs, professors, opportunities you'd pursue
- What you'd contribute

#### 5. Future Goals
- General direction (not rigid career plan)
- How this school fits your path
- What you want to explore

#### 6. Challenges/Growth
- Obstacle you've overcome
- Failure and what you learned
- How you've changed

#### 7. Community/Impact
- How you've helped others
- Communities you belong to
- How you'd contribute to college community

#### 8. Current Events/World
- Issues you care about
- Last interesting thing you learned
- Books, articles, media that influenced you

#### 9. Questions for Interviewer
- Thoughtful questions about their experience
- Questions about school culture/community
- Questions that show research (not found on website)

#### 10. Specifics About Your Application
- Be ready to discuss anything in your application
- Expand on activities, essays, or experiences
- Clarify anything that might be confusing

## Common Questions & Response Frameworks

### "Tell Me About Yourself" (2 minutes)

**Bad approach:** Resume recitation
**Good approach:** Story that reveals who you are

**Framework:**
```
[Hook/Interest] → [Background context] → [Key activities/passions] → [Future direction]
```

**Example:**
> "I'm someone who can't leave problems unsolved—which explains why I spent two years debugging my own drone rather than buying one that worked. I grew up in a small town in Ohio where my dad runs an auto repair shop, and I've been tinkering with things since I could hold a wrench. That led me to robotics in high school, where I'm now captain of our FRC team. But what really excites me isn't just building things—it's teaching others to build. I started a robotics program at the middle school, and watching 12-year-olds go from 'I can't do this' to programming their first robot is honestly the most rewarding thing I do. At [School], I want to combine engineering with education research to figure out how to make technical learning more accessible."

### "Why This School?"

**Bad approach:** Rankings, prestige, generic praise
**Good approach:** Specific fit with your goals and interests

**Framework:**
```
[Specific academic interest] + [Specific resource/program at school] + [How you'd engage]
```

**Example:**
> "I'm fascinated by the intersection of neuroscience and AI, specifically how we can model human learning in machines. [School]'s Cognitive Science department is one of the few that truly bridges these fields—Professor Chen's work on neural network interpretability is exactly what I want to explore. I'd love to contribute to the Computational Cognition Lab while also taking courses in the CS department. Beyond academics, the undergraduate research symposium and the active Science Society show that [School] creates space for students like me who want to be actively doing science, not just studying it."

### "What's Your Greatest Weakness?"

**Bad approach:** Humble brag ("I work too hard"), cliché, denial
**Good approach:** Genuine weakness with growth

**Framework:**
```
[Real weakness] → [Specific example] → [What you're doing about it]
```

**Example:**
> "I tend to take on too much and then struggle to prioritize. Last year, I committed to leading three different projects simultaneously, and my work on all of them suffered. I've since learned to be more realistic about my capacity—I actually turned down an opportunity this fall because I recognized I couldn't give it the attention it deserved. I'm still working on this, but I've gotten better at asking 'Should I do this?' before 'Can I do this?'"

### "Tell Me About a Challenge You've Overcome"

**Bad approach:** Trivial challenge, victim narrative, no growth shown
**Good approach:** Real challenge with agency and learning

**Framework (STAR-L):**
```
Situation → Task → Action → Result → Learning
```

**Example:**
> "My sophomore year, I was diagnosed with a chronic illness that meant I had to miss almost two months of school. [Situation] I was determined not to fall behind academically or give up my spot on the debate team. [Task] I worked with my teachers to create a modified schedule, and I practiced debate via video call with my partner every day from home. [Action] I came back and qualified for state in debate, and I actually improved my GPA that semester because the experience taught me to be incredibly efficient with my time. [Result] More importantly, I learned that I'm more resilient than I thought, and that asking for help isn't weakness—it's strategy. [Learning]"

### "What Do You Do for Fun?"

**Bad approach:** Activities that seem hollow, obviously college-focused, nothing
**Good approach:** Genuine interests that show personality

**What they're looking for:**
- Are you a real human with interests?
- Will you contribute to campus life?
- What will you be like as a roommate/classmate?

**Good answers include:**
- Genuine hobbies (cooking, hiking, video games—if you can discuss thoughtfully)
- Creative pursuits (music, art, writing for pleasure)
- Social interests (board games with friends, pickup basketball)
- Intellectual interests outside school (podcasts, documentaries, reading)

### "Do You Have Questions for Me?"

**Always have 3-5 prepared.** Never say no.

**Good questions:**

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Their experience** | "What's something you didn't expect about [School] when you were a student?" |
| **Culture** | "How would you describe the relationship between students and professors?" |
| **Specific program** | "I'm interested in [program]. What's your sense of how it's viewed on campus?" |
| **Community** | "What kind of students tend to thrive at [School]?" |
| **Recent changes** | "I read about [new initiative]. How has that impacted student life?" |

**Bad questions:**
- Anything on the website
- "What are my chances?"
- Nothing at all
- Questions that show you haven't researched

## Interview Red Flags (What to Avoid)

### Attitude Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It's a Problem |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Arrogance** | Nobody wants to admit someone who seems insufferable |
| **Entitlement** | "I deserve to go here" attitude |
| **Negativity** | Complaining about school, teachers, others |
| **Lack of curiosity** | No questions, no engagement |
| **Inauthenticity** | Obviously rehearsed, robotic answers |
| **Parent dependence** | Seems like parent wrote application |

### Content Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It's a Problem |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Can't discuss your activities** | Suggests you didn't really do them |
| **No "Why This School"** | Shows lack of research/genuine interest |
| **Contradicting your application** | Raises authenticity concerns |
| **Inappropriate topics** | Politics, controversial opinions unprompted |
| **Bad-mouthing other schools** | Shows poor judgment |
| **Only talking about rankings/prestige** | Suggests shallow values |

### Logistics Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It's a Problem |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Being late** | Disrespectful, poor planning |
| **Being unprepared** | Suggests you don't care |
| **Inappropriate dress** | Shows poor judgment |
| **Phone interruptions** | Disrespectful |
| **Parent in the interview** | Immediate concern |
| **Not following up** | Missed opportunity |

## Interview Day Logistics

### What to Wear

| Setting | Appropriate |
|---------|-------------|
| **Coffee shop/casual** | Business casual: nice pants, collared shirt, blouse |
| **Campus visit** | Business casual, comfortable walking shoes |
| **Formal interview** | Business formal: suit jacket, dress |
| **Video call** | Same as in-person (full outfit, not just top) |

**Universal rules:**
- Neat, clean, well-fitting
- No logos, graphics, or controversial messages
- When in doubt, slightly overdress
- Comfortable (you'll be nervous enough)

### What to Bring

| Item | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Resume/activity list** | Reference for yourself (don't hand to interviewer unless asked) |
| **Notes on questions** | Review before (not during) |
| **Questions for interviewer** | Written down so you don't forget |
| **Pen and small notebook** | For note-taking if appropriate |
| **Copy of your application essays** | Refresh your memory |
| **Interviewer contact info** | In case of delays |

### Timing

| When | What |
|------|------|
| **Day before** | Confirm time/location, prepare outfit, review notes |
| **2 hours before** | Light meal, review talking points |
| **30 minutes before** | Arrive at location (in-person) or test technology (virtual) |
| **15 minutes before** | In waiting area or logged in |
| **5 minutes before** | Calm, focused, ready |

## Post-Interview Protocol

### The Thank-You Note

**Timeline:** Within 24 hours

**Format:** Email (unless they specifically gave physical address)

**Length:** 3-5 sentences

**Content:**
```
Subject: Thank You - [School] Interview

Dear [Interviewer Name],

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today about [School]. I especially 
enjoyed our conversation about [specific topic discussed].

Our discussion reinforced my enthusiasm for [School], particularly [specific 
aspect you discussed]. I'm excited about the possibility of [specific opportunity 
or aspect of school].

Thank you again for sharing your experiences and insights.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

**Do:**
- Reference something specific from the conversation
- Keep it genuine and brief
- Proofread carefully
- Send promptly

**Don't:**
- Write a novel
- Be overly formal or stiff
- Ask about your chances
- Send generic template

### Self-Assessment After Interview

**Questions to reflect on:**
1. Did I communicate my key messages?
2. Were there questions I struggled with?
3. Did the conversation feel natural or forced?
4. What would I do differently?
5. Did I learn anything new about the school?

**If it went poorly:**
- Don't panic—interviews rarely make or break applications
- Consider sending additional context in thank-you note if appropriate
- Focus on what you can control (rest of application)
- Don't obsess or spiral

## Special Situations

### If You're Shy/Introverted

**Preparation is your friend:**
- Practice more than extroverts need to
- Have specific examples ready (reduces need to think on the spot)
- Remember: listening is valuable too
- Interviewers don't expect performance—they want conversation

**Reframe nervousness:**
- You're not performing; you're having a conversation
- They want you to succeed
- Pauses to think are okay
- Quality over quantity in responses

### If English Isn't Your First Language

**Strategies:**
- Practice with native speakers beforehand
- Ask for clarification if you don't understand
- Slow down rather than rushing
- Your accent is not a weakness
- Focus on content, not perfect grammar

**What interviewers understand:**
- You're communicating in a second language
- Accent ≠ intelligence
- They're evaluating ideas, not pronunciation

### If Asked About Weaknesses in Your Application

**If they ask about low grades:**
> "Junior year chemistry was a struggle for me—I got a B-. That semester, I was also dealing with [brief context if relevant]. Since then, I've [specific action you took], and my senior year science grades reflect that growth."

**If they notice a gap:**
> "You may notice I didn't have activities sophomore year. That's when my family moved, and I spent that time helping us get settled and supporting my younger siblings. By junior year, I was able to re-engage and [specific activities]."

**General principle:** Acknowledge, explain briefly (no excuses), show growth or current success.

### If Asked About Controversial Topics

**General rule:** Avoid unless directly relevant to your story.

**If you must engage:**
- State your view thoughtfully
- Acknowledge other perspectives
- Don't be preachy or dismissive
- Quickly move to related, safer ground

**Example:**
> "I'm passionate about environmental policy because of [personal reason]. I understand people have different views on how to address climate change, and I think the most interesting discussions happen when we can understand why people disagree. At [School], I'd love to engage with the environmental policy program where I could learn more about the economic considerations I haven't fully explored yet."

## Practice Methods

### Solo Practice

1. **Record yourself** answering common questions
2. **Time yourself** (most answers should be 60-90 seconds)
3. **Review recordings** for filler words, rambling, nervous habits
4. **Practice speaking clearly** and at moderate pace

### Partner Practice

1. **Mock interviews** with parents, counselors, teachers
2. **Ask for honest feedback** on content and delivery
3. **Practice with strangers** (alumni, counselor contacts) for realistic nerves
4. **Do full mock interviews** (30-45 minutes) at least twice

### What to Practice

| Focus Area | How to Practice |
|------------|-----------------|
| **Answering concisely** | Time yourself; aim for 60-90 seconds |
| **Specific examples** | Have 2-3 ready for each topic |
| **Thinking aloud** | Practice verbalizing your thought process |
| **Asking questions** | Practice your questions sounding natural |
| **Recovery from mistakes** | Practice recovering when you mess up |

## Interview Checklist

### 1 Week Before
- [ ] Confirm date, time, location, format
- [ ] Research interviewer (LinkedIn, briefly)
- [ ] Review your application (essays, activities)
- [ ] Prepare talking points for each category
- [ ] Prepare 5 questions for interviewer
- [ ] Plan outfit
- [ ] Schedule mock interview

### Day Before
- [ ] Confirm logistics
- [ ] Review talking points (don't over-memorize)
- [ ] Prepare outfit
- [ ] Get good sleep
- [ ] Plan transportation/test technology

### Day Of
- [ ] Eat a good meal
- [ ] Review notes briefly
- [ ] Arrive/log in early
- [ ] Turn off phone
- [ ] Bring necessary materials
- [ ] Take a deep breath

### After
- [ ] Send thank-you within 24 hours
- [ ] Note what went well/poorly for future interviews
- [ ] Don't obsess about the outcome
