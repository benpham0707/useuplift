## Overview: Understanding Financial Aid

> "The sticker price is not what most families pay. But understanding the system is crucial to getting the aid you deserve."
> — FinAid.org

### The Two Types of Aid

| Type | Based On | Who Offers |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Need-Based** | Family financial situation | Most schools, federal/state gov |
| **Merit-Based** | Academic/other achievements | Some schools, external organizations |

### Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cost of Attendance (COA)** | Total cost including tuition, room, board, books, personal expenses |
| **Expected Family Contribution (EFC)** | What the government/school thinks you can pay |
| **Financial Need** | COA - EFC = Need |
| **Net Price** | COA - All Aid = What you actually pay |
| **Gift Aid** | Grants and scholarships (free money) |
| **Self-Help** | Loans and work-study (you pay back or earn) |

## Financial Aid Policies by School Type

### Elite Schools: Need-Blind, Full Need

| School | Policy | Loan-Free? | Income Threshold |
|--------|--------|------------|------------------|
| Harvard | Need-blind, meets full need | Yes | <$85K = free |
| Yale | Need-blind, meets full need | Yes | <$75K = free |
| Princeton | Need-blind, meets full need | Yes | <$65K = free |
| MIT | Need-blind, meets full need | Yes | Based on formula |
| Stanford | Need-blind, meets full need | Yes | <$100K = free |
| Amherst | Need-blind, meets full need | Yes | Varies |

**"Need-blind" means:** Admission decision is made without considering ability to pay.

**"Meets full need" means:** They'll cover the gap between COA and your EFC.

**Important:** "Full need" is based on THEIR calculation of your need, which may differ from yours.

### Selective Schools: Various Policies

| Policy | Examples | What It Means |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Need-blind, meets need | Most Ivies, Stanford, MIT | Best scenario |
| Need-aware, meets need | Some LACs | May consider ability for borderline admits |
| Need-blind, gaps in aid | Many publics | May offer aid but not cover everything |
| Merit + Need | Many private | Combines achievement-based and need-based |

### International Students

| School Type | International Aid |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Need-blind for all | Harvard, Yale, MIT, Princeton, Amherst (very few) |
| Need-aware for int'l | Most selective schools |
| Very limited int'l aid | Most public universities |

**Reality for international students:** Competition for aid is fierce; full-pay students have advantage at most schools.

## The FAFSA

### What It Is

**FAFSA** = Free Application for Federal Student Aid

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Opens** | October 1 (for following school year) |
| **Required for** | Federal grants, loans, work-study |
| **Used by** | All US schools |
| **Cost** | Free |
| **Determines** | Your Student Aid Index (SAI) |

### FAFSA Timeline

| When | Action |
|------|--------|
| October 1 | FAFSA opens |
| October-November | Complete FAFSA (earlier = better) |
| December-January | State deadlines (varies) |
| February-March | Many school priority deadlines |
| February onward | Award letters begin arriving |

### What FAFSA Considers

| Included | Not Included |
|----------|--------------|
| Parent income | Home equity (primary residence) |
| Parent assets | Retirement accounts (401k, IRA) |
| Student income | Small business/farm (under 100 employees) |
| Student assets | Life insurance value |
| Savings | |
| Investments | |

### FAFSA Asset Protection Allowances

| Parent Age | Asset Protection (approx) |
|------------|--------------------------|
| Under 40 | ~$0 |
| 45 | ~$5,000 |
| 50 | ~$10,000 |
| 55 | ~$15,000 |
| 60 | ~$20,000 |

**Meaning:** Assets below this aren't counted against you.

## The CSS Profile

### What It Is

**CSS Profile** = College Scholarship Service Profile (College Board)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Opens** | October 1 |
| **Required by** | ~400 private colleges, mostly selective |
| **Cost** | ~$25 first school, $16 each additional (fee waivers available) |
| **More comprehensive than** | FAFSA |
| **Includes** | Home equity, non-custodial parent info |

### CSS Profile Timeline

| When | Action |
|------|--------|
| October 1 | CSS Profile opens |
| November-January | Most school deadlines |
| 2 weeks before school deadline | Ideally submit by |

### What CSS Profile Considers (Beyond FAFSA)

| Additional Factors | Impact |
|-------------------|--------|
| Home equity | May increase expected contribution |
| Non-custodial parent income | Required at most schools |
| Sibling private school tuition | May decrease expected contribution |
| Medical expenses | May decrease expected contribution |
| Business/farm assets | More detailed analysis |

### FAFSA vs. CSS Profile

| Factor | FAFSA | CSS Profile |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| Home equity | Not counted | Counted (usually capped) |
| Non-custodial parent | Optional | Usually required |
| Stepparent income | Counted | Counted |
| Small business | Not counted | May be counted |
| Number of family members in college | Counted heavily | Counted (varies by school) |
| Cost | Free | ~$25+ |

## Understanding Your Award Letter

### Components of an Award Letter

| Component | Type | Details |
|-----------|------|---------|
| **Grants** | Gift aid | Free money (institutional, federal, state) |
| **Scholarships** | Gift aid | Free money (merit, outside) |
| **Work-Study** | Self-help | Earn money through campus job |
| **Subsidized Loans** | Self-help | Government pays interest while enrolled |
| **Unsubsidized Loans** | Self-help | You pay all interest |
| **Parent PLUS Loan** | Self-help | Parent borrows (usually at higher rate) |

### Calculating True Cost

```
Cost of Attendance (COA)
- Grants
- Scholarships
- (Work-study: usually not subtracted from "cost")
= Net Price

Net Price
- Federal loans (your responsibility)
- Parent loans (parent responsibility)
= Out-of-pocket needed
```

### Comparing Award Letters

**Schools present aid differently.** Normalize by calculating:

1. **Total gift aid** (grants + scholarships)
2. **Net price** (COA - gift aid)
3. **Total loans offered**
4. **Expected out-of-pocket + loans per year**
5. **Four-year total cost**

### Example Comparison

| | School A | School B |
|---|----------|----------|
| **COA** | $80,000 | $55,000 |
| **Grants** | $45,000 | $20,000 |
| **Net Price** | $35,000 | $35,000 |
| **Loans Offered** | $5,500 | $5,500 |
| **Work-Study** | $3,000 | $2,000 |
| **Out of Pocket** | $26,500 | $27,500 |

**Note:** Same net price, but different COA means different value.

|--------|--------|
| All Ivy League | Need-based only |
| MIT | Need-based only |

| Strategy | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Apply to schools where you're above median | They want to attract you |

| Warning Sign | Concern |

| School you've never heard of offering huge scholarship | Quality concern |
| Scholarship that covers "everything" at low-ranked school | Check conditions |
| Renewable only with very high GPA | May lose it |
| Too good to be true | Probably is |

## Financial Aid Negotiation

### When Negotiation Works

| Situation | Negotiation Success Likelihood |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Comparable school offered more | Good |
| Circumstances changed (job loss, etc.) | Good |
| Original information was incomplete | Good |
| Just asking for more without reason | Poor |
| School already meets full need | Usually won't adjust |

### Negotiation Framework

**Step 1: Gather Leverage**
- Better offers from comparable schools
- Changed circumstances
- Specific documented reasons

**Step 2: Professional Appeal**
```
Subject: Financial Aid Appeal - [Student Name] [ID#]

Dear [Financial Aid Office],

Thank you for [Student]'s admission to [School] and the generous
financial aid package. We are excited about the opportunity.

We are writing to request reconsideration of our aid package due to
[specific reason].

[Explain circumstances: other offers, changed situation, etc.]

[Include specific documentation/evidence]

We remain committed to [School] as our first choice and hope to
find a way to make enrollment possible.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
[Parent/Family Name]
```

**Step 3: Be Specific**
- State the gap amount
- Explain what would make enrollment possible
- Provide evidence (other award letters, documentation)

### What Schools Will and Won't Adjust

| May Adjust | Usually Won't Adjust |
|------------|---------------------|
| Discretionary grants | Federal aid (determined by formula) |
| Institutional scholarships | Need calculation methodology |
| Special circumstances | Merit aid (at schools that offer it) |
| Matching competitor offers | |

### Negotiation Etiquette

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Be polite and professional | Be demanding or entitled |
| Provide specific documentation | Just ask for "more" |
| Explain genuine need | Exaggerate circumstances |
| Be grateful for existing offer | Threaten to go elsewhere |
| Follow up appropriately | Harass the office |

## Outside Scholarships

### Finding Outside Scholarships

| Source | Examples |
|--------|----------|
| **Scholarship search engines** | Fastweb, Scholarships.com, College Board |
| **Local organizations** | Rotary, Chamber of Commerce, community foundations |
| **Employer** | Parent employer education benefits |
| **Professional associations** | Field-specific scholarships |
| **Heritage/identity** | Cultural organizations |
| **School counselor** | Local scholarship lists |

### Outside Scholarship Impact

**Important:** Outside scholarships may reduce your institutional aid.

| School Policy | What Happens |
|---------------|--------------|
| Reduces loans first | Best case - keeps grants |
| Reduces all aid proportionally | Okay - some benefit |
| Reduces grants | Worst case - no net benefit |

**Strategy:** Ask financial aid office about their policy BEFORE applying for outside scholarships.

### Scholarship Scams to Avoid

| Red Flag | Concern |
|----------|---------|
| Application fee required | Legitimate scholarships are free to apply |
| Guaranteed win | No one can guarantee |
| You have to buy something | It's a sales pitch |
| You won something you didn't apply for | Scam |
| High-pressure tactics | Legitimate orgs don't do this |

## Loans: Understanding Your Options

### Federal Student Loans

| Loan Type | Interest Rate (2025-26) | Limit (Dependent) |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Direct Subsidized | ~5-6% | Based on need; max varies by year |
| Direct Unsubsidized | ~5-6% | $5,500-7,500 depending on year |
| Parent PLUS | ~7-8% | Cost of attendance minus other aid |

### Subsidized vs. Unsubsidized

| Feature | Subsidized | Unsubsidized |
|---------|------------|--------------|
| Based on need | Yes | No |
| Interest while enrolled | Government pays | You pay (or it accrues) |
| Better deal | Yes | Less favorable |

### Loan Limits by Year

| Year | Subsidized + Unsubsidized Total |
|------|--------------------------------|
| Freshman | $5,500 |
| Sophomore | $6,500 |
| Junior | $7,500 |
| Senior | $7,500 |
| **Total Undergraduate** | **$31,000** |

### Should You Take Loans?

| Situation | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| Subsidized loans available | Usually take them (good terms) |
| Small unsubsidized (<$5K/year) | Often reasonable for good school |
| Large unsubsidized (>$10K/year) | Reconsider school choice |
| Parent PLUS loans | Depends on family finances |
| Private loans | Last resort only |

### Debt-to-Salary Guidelines

| Guideline | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Total debt < first year salary** | Manageable |
| **Monthly payment < 10% gross monthly income** | Comfortable |
| **Total debt > 2x starting salary** | May cause hardship |

**Example:**
- Expected starting salary: $60,000/year
- Recommended max debt: $60,000
- ~$600/month payment (10-year term)
- ~10% of gross monthly income
