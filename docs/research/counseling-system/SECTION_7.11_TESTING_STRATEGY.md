# Section 7.11: Testing Strategy

**Purpose:** Comprehensive guidance for standardized testing decisions including SAT vs. ACT, test-optional policies, AP strategy, and score optimization.

**System Usage:** Query to provide personalized testing recommendations based on student profile and target schools.

**Last Updated:** January 2026

## Overview: The Testing Landscape (2026)

### Current State of Test-Optional

| Policy Type | Schools | Trend |
|-------------|---------|-------|
| Test-Required | Georgetown, MIT, Caltech, some others | Returning post-COVID |
| Test-Optional | Most T30 schools | Stabilized |
| Test-Blind | UC System, California schools | Permanent policy |
| Test-Flexible | Some schools accept alternatives | Declining |

**Key Insight:** Test-optional does NOT mean tests don't matter. At most selective schools, students who submit strong scores have higher admit rates.

> "We see applications both with and without scores. But when scores are submitted and they're strong, they do help. When they're not submitted, we wonder why."
> — Former Duke Admissions Officer

## SAT vs. ACT: Choosing Your Test

### Fundamental Differences

| Factor | SAT | ACT |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **Total Questions** | 154 | 215 |
| **Time** | 2 hrs 14 min (no essay) | 2 hrs 55 min (no essay) |
| **Sections** | 2 (Reading & Writing, Math) | 4 (English, Math, Reading, Science) |
| **Math Calculator** | Yes (all sections) | Yes (for entire test) |
| **Science Section** | No | Yes (data/graph interpretation) |
| **Pacing** | More time per question | Faster pace required |
| **Score Range** | 400-1600 | 1-36 composite |
| **Guessing Penalty** | No | No |

### SAT is Better For Students Who:

| Characteristic | Why SAT |
|----------------|---------|
| Strong vocabulary | Reading passages reward this |
| Prefer more time per question | Less time pressure |
| Strong in math concepts | Math is weighted equally to verbal |
| Dislike pure time pressure | Pacing more manageable |
| Struggle with pure science | No dedicated science section |

### ACT is Better For Students Who:

| Characteristic | Why ACT |
|----------------|---------|
| Fast processors | Can handle more questions/time |
| Strong science/data readers | Science section plays to strength |
| Consistent across subjects | Composite rewards balance |
| Good stamina | Longer test rewards endurance |
| Strong in grammar | English section is more straightforward |

### The Diagnostic Approach

**Step 1:** Take a full practice test of EACH (under timed conditions)

**Step 2:** Compare concordance

| SAT Score | ACT Equivalent |
|-----------|----------------|
| 1600 | 36 |
| 1570 | 36 |
| 1540 | 35 |
| 1510 | 34 |
| 1480 | 33 |
| 1450 | 32 |
| 1420 | 31 |
| 1390 | 30 |
| 1360 | 29 |
| 1330 | 28 |

**Step 3:** Choose the test where you score HIGHER on the concordance table

## Testing Timeline

### Recommended Schedule

| Grade | Fall | Winter | Spring | Summer |
|-------|------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Freshman** | — | — | — | — |
| **Sophomore** | PSAT 10 | — | SAT/ACT diagnostic | Begin prep if early |
| **Junior** | PSAT/NMSQT | SAT/ACT #1 | SAT/ACT #2 | SAT/ACT #3 if needed |
| **Senior** | Final SAT/ACT if needed | — | — | — |

### PSAT/NMSQT Strategy (Junior Year)

**What it is:** Practice test + National Merit qualifier

**National Merit Cutoffs (Approximate):**

| State | Selection Index Cutoff (approx) |
|-------|--------------------------------|
| Alabama | 212 |
| California | 223 |
| Connecticut | 222 |
| Massachusetts | 223 |
| New Jersey | 223 |
| New York | 222 |
| Texas | 221 |

**Note:** Selection Index = (Reading/Writing Score × 2) + Math Score

**If aiming for National Merit:**
- Junior year PSAT is the ONLY one that counts
- Score needed depends on state
- Semifinalist → Finalist requires essay and school endorsement

### When to Take SAT/ACT

**First Attempt:** December or January of Junior Year
- Allows time for improvement
- Results back before spring testing

**Second Attempt:** March-May of Junior Year
- Targeted improvement based on first scores
- Most students see score increases

**Third Attempt (if needed):** June or August
- Final opportunity before senior year
- Only if meaningful improvement expected

**Senior Year Testing:** October or November
- ONLY if needed for EA/ED deadlines
- Should be rare if junior year was well-planned

## Test Preparation Strategy

### Preparation Approaches

| Approach | Cost | Time Required | Effectiveness |
|----------|------|---------------|---------------|
| Self-study (books + free resources) | $20-100 | High (100+ hours) | High if disciplined |
| Online courses | $100-500 | Moderate | Moderate to High |
| Group class | $500-1500 | Moderate | Moderate |
| Private tutoring | $1000-10000+ | Depends on hours | High |

### Free/Low-Cost Resources

| Resource | What It Offers |
|----------|----------------|
| Khan Academy (SAT) | Free official practice |
| College Board Practice Tests | 10+ official SATs |
| ACT.org | 6+ official practice tests |
| Erica Meltzer (books) | Grammar/reading strategy |
| College Panda (books) | Math strategy |
| UWorld | Practice questions (free trial) |

### Preparation Timeline

**3-Month Intensive Plan:**

| Week | Focus |
|------|-------|
| 1-2 | Full diagnostic; identify weaknesses |
| 3-4 | Core content review (biggest gap areas) |
| 5-6 | Practice problems + strategy |
| 7-8 | Timed section practice |
| 9-10 | Full practice tests (1 per week) |
| 11-12 | Review mistakes; final practice test; rest before test |

**Target:** 1-2 hours per day, 5-6 days per week

### Expected Score Improvements

| Starting Score | Typical Improvement (with good prep) |
|----------------|--------------------------------------|
| SAT 1000-1100 | 100-200 points |
| SAT 1100-1200 | 80-150 points |
| SAT 1200-1300 | 60-100 points |
| SAT 1300-1400 | 40-80 points |
| SAT 1400-1500 | 30-60 points |
| SAT 1500+ | 20-50 points |

**Reality Check:** Improvement gets harder at higher scores. Moving from 1300→1400 is easier than 1500→1600.

## Test-Optional Strategy

### When to Go Test-Optional

**Consider test-optional if:**

| Situation | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|
| Scores significantly below 25th percentile | Hurts more than helps |
| Limited improvement despite significant prep | Diminishing returns |
| Test anxiety significantly impacts performance | Doesn't reflect ability |
| Other application components are exceptional | Scores would detract |
| Schools are genuinely test-optional (verify) | Policy supports this |

### When to SUBMIT Scores

**Submit if:**

| Situation | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|
| At or above school's 25th percentile | Competitive |
| Scores support your academic narrative | Reinforces strength |
| School is test-flexible but values scores | Shows willingness |
| You have strong subscores | Even if composite is lower |

### The Test-Optional Reality

**Data from recent admissions cycles:**

| Applicant Type | Typical Admit Rate |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Applied with strong scores | Higher |
| Applied test-optional (but strong otherwise) | Slightly lower |
| Applied test-optional (to hide weak scores) | Much lower |

**Key Insight:** Test-optional helps students who genuinely can't test well but excel elsewhere. It doesn't help students hiding weak academics.

## Superscoring Strategy

### What is Superscoring?

Taking your highest section scores from multiple test dates to create a new composite.

**Example:**
| Date | Math | Reading/Writing | Total |
|------|------|-----------------|-------|
| March | 720 | 680 | 1400 |
| June | 690 | 740 | 1430 |
| **Superscore** | **720** | **740** | **1460** |

### Schools That Superscore

| Policy | Schools |
|--------|---------|
| **SAT Superscore** | Most schools including all Ivies |
| **ACT Superscore** | Fewer schools; verify each |
| **Don't Superscore** | Georgetown, UC System (test-blind anyway) |

### Superscoring Strategy

**If one section is significantly weaker:**
1. Focus prep entirely on weak section
2. Retake knowing you only need to improve one area
3. The strong section "carries over"

**Risk:** Some schools see all scores even if they superscore. Major improvement over short time can raise questions.

## AP Exam Strategy

### Purpose of AP Exams

1. **College credit:** Save money, skip intro courses
2. **Placement:** Start at higher level
3. **Rigor signal:** Shows you challenged yourself
4. **Score validation:** Demonstrates mastery

### How Many APs?

| School Target | Recommended APs (Total by Graduation) |
|---------------|--------------------------------------|
| HYPSM | 8-12+ (most available) |
| Top 20 | 6-10 |
| Top 50 | 4-8 |
| Top 100 | 2-5 |

**Quality over quantity:** 8 APs with scores of 5 > 12 APs with mixed scores

### AP Score Reporting Strategy

| Score | Report? | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| 5 | Always | Demonstrates mastery |
| 4 | Usually | Strong performance |
| 3 | Maybe | Depends on subject and school |
| 2 | No | Suggests struggle |
| 1 | No | Best not mentioned |

**For Elite Schools:** Report 4s and 5s only unless school requires all.

### AP Exam Timeline

| When | What |
|------|------|
| September | Enroll in AP courses |
| February | Register for AP exams |
| May | Take AP exams (first two weeks) |
| July | Scores released |
| After enrollment | Send official score report to college |

### AP vs. IB vs. Dual Enrollment

| Program | Pros | Cons |
|---------|------|------|
| **AP** | Widely recognized, standardized, flexible | Can be content-heavy |
| **IB** | Holistic, internationally recognized, rigorous | Less flexible, full program required |
| **Dual Enrollment** | College credit, college experience | Less standardized recognition |

**For elite US colleges:** AP is generally most recognized, but IB is equally valued if available.

## Score Reporting Logistics

### Score Choice

**What it is:** You choose which scores to send

**Policies:**
| Policy | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Score Choice | Send any scores you want |
| All Scores Required | Must send everything |
| Recommends All | Won't penalize for weaker scores |

### How to Send Scores

| Method | Cost | Timeline |
|--------|------|----------|
| College Board (SAT) | $14/report | 1-2 weeks electronic |
| ACT | $16/report | 2-3 weeks |
| Free sends | 4 free with registration | Sent after test date |

### Self-Reporting vs. Official

**Common App:** Self-report during application

**Official Report:** Sent only after enrollment (at most schools)

**Verification:** Schools trust self-reporting but verify upon enrollment

## Special Testing Situations

### Testing Accommodations

**Who qualifies:**
- Documented learning disabilities
- Physical disabilities
- Medical conditions
- Must have documentation

**Common accommodations:**
- Extended time (1.5x or 2x)
- Separate room
- Breaks
- Computer use

**How to get:**
1. Document disability with school
2. Have accommodations in place at school first
3. Apply through College Board/ACT
4. Allow 7+ weeks for approval

### International Testing

| Consideration | Details |
|---------------|---------|
| Test availability | SAT/ACT available worldwide, fewer dates |
| Score reporting | Same process, may take longer |
| English proficiency | TOEFL/IELTS may be required additionally |

### Test Date Conflicts

**If you have a conflict (sports, religious, etc.):**
1. Register for alternate date
2. Contact testing agency
3. Document conflict

## Testing Decision Flowchart

```
START: Do your target schools require testing?
  │
  ├─ YES (MIT, Georgetown, etc.) → Prepare and take SAT/ACT
  │
  └─ NO (Test-optional) → Take diagnostic test
                            │
                            ├─ Score at/above 25th percentile? → Submit scores
                            │
                            └─ Score below 25th percentile?
                                │
                                ├─ Time to improve significantly? → Prep and retake
                                │
                                └─ No / Won't improve enough → Go test-optional
                                                               (Focus on other strengths)
```

## Testing Myths Debunked

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "You should take both SAT and ACT" | Take ONE and do it well |
| "Take it as many times as possible" | 2-3 attempts is optimal |
| "Superscoring means take it 5+ times" | Diminishing returns after 3 |
| "Test-optional means tests don't matter" | Strong scores still help |
| "ACT Science requires science knowledge" | It's data/reading interpretation |
| "Expensive prep is always better" | Discipline matters more than cost |
| "March is the best test date" | Any date with good prep is fine |
