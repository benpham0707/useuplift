# Section 7.9: Recommendation Letter Strategy

**Purpose:** Comprehensive guide for selecting recommenders, preparing them to write strong letters, and managing the recommendation process.

**System Usage:** Query to help students optimize their recommendation strategy for maximum impact.

**Last Updated:** January 2026

---

## Overview: Why Recommendations Matter

> "Letters of recommendation are one of the few places where we get an outside perspective on a student. A truly glowing recommendation from a teacher who knows a student well can be decisive."
> — Former MIT Admissions Officer

### What Recommendations Provide

| Component | Information Gained |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Academic | How you learn, think, participate |
| Character | Who you are as a person |
| Context | What obstacles you've overcome |
| Comparison | How you stack up against peers |
| Potential | What you might become |

### The Reality Check

**Strong recommendations:** Can push a borderline admit to accepted
**Weak recommendations:** Can push a borderline admit to rejected
**Neutral recommendations:** Provide no boost (a missed opportunity)

## Who to Ask: Teacher Recommendations

### The Selection Framework

**Priority Order:**

1. **Teacher who knows you BEST** (most important)
2. **Teacher in core academic subject** (important)
3. **Teacher from junior year** (ideal timing)
4. **Teacher whose class challenged you** (shows intellectual engagement)

### Subject Considerations

| Intended Major | Recommended Subjects | Why |
|---------------|---------------------|-----|
| STEM | Math, Science | Shows quantitative ability |
| Humanities | English, History, Languages | Shows writing/analysis |
| Social Sciences | History, Economics, Psychology | Shows analytical thinking |
| Undeclared | One STEM, one Humanities | Shows range |
| Business | Math, Economics, English | Shows quant + communication |

**Key Insight:** Even STEM applicants should consider one humanities teacher who can speak to writing ability and discussion participation.

### Teacher Selection Matrix

| Factor | Weight | What to Look For |
|--------|--------|------------------|
| **Relationship depth** | 40% | Do they KNOW you beyond grades? |
| **Subject relevance** | 20% | Does it align with your interests? |
| **Grade in class** | 15% | Ideally A or strong B |
| **Recency** | 15% | Junior year ideal, sophomore OK |
| **Writing ability** | 10% | Can they write well? |

### Red Flags in Teacher Selection

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Teacher you barely spoke to | Can only write generic letter |
| Teacher from freshman year only | Too long ago |
| Teacher where you got a C | Unless great story of growth |
| Teacher you had conflict with | Risk of negative letter |
| Famous teacher who doesn't know you | "I had 200 students" = weak letter |

## Who to Ask: Counselor Recommendation

### What Counselors Provide

The counselor letter offers:
- School context (rigor, opportunities)
- Explanation of transcript anomalies
- Broader view of your involvement
- Institutional perspective

### Building Counselor Relationship

| Grade | Action |
|-------|--------|
| Freshman | Introduce yourself at least once |
| Sophomore | Check in twice per year minimum |
| Junior | Monthly contact ideal |
| Senior | Clear communication throughout process |

### What to Provide Your Counselor

| Material | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Updated resume | Reminder of activities |
| Brief personal statement | Who you are beyond resume |
| List of colleges | They'll customize by school |
| Context for any issues | Grades, disciplinary, circumstances |
| Brag sheet | Your accomplishments in detail |
| Thank you note | Builds goodwill |

## Who to Ask: Additional/Supplemental Recommendations

### When to Use Additional Recommendations

**GOOD reasons:**
- Different perspective (coach, employer, mentor)
- Explains significant activity in depth
- School specifically requests it
- Adds substantial new information

**BAD reasons:**
- "More is better" thinking
- Parent thinks it will help
- Recommender is famous but doesn't know you
- Repeating what teachers already said

### Who Makes a Good Additional Recommender

| Recommender | Strengths | Cautions |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Research mentor** | Speaks to intellectual work | Only if substantive relationship |
| **Employer** | Work ethic, responsibility | Only if meaningful job |
| **Coach** | Character, teamwork, dedication | Only if coach truly knows you |
| **Art/Music teacher** | Creative ability, passion | If arts are central to application |
| **Community supervisor** | Service, leadership | Only if long-term relationship |

### Who to AVOID as Additional Recommender

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| **Family friends** | Biased, schools know it |
| **Politicians you don't know** | Transparent name-dropping |
| **Alumni who met you once** | Obvious favor |
| **Anyone famous who can't speak to you personally** | Backfires |
| **Parents** | Never |

## When to Ask

### Timeline

| When | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Spring of junior year** | Identify potential recommenders |
| **April-May of junior year** | Ask teachers in person |
| **Before summer** | Confirm commitment, provide materials |
| **August-September** | Follow up, provide updates |
| **October** | Ensure letters submitted for EA/ED |
| **November-December** | Ensure letters submitted for RD |
| **After decisions** | Thank notes regardless of outcome |

### How to Ask

**In-person request (required):**
```
"Mr./Ms. [Name], I've really valued your class because [specific reason].
I'm applying to college and was wondering if you'd be willing to write
me a strong letter of recommendation? I want to make sure you have
time and feel comfortable writing positively about me."
```

**Key elements:**
- In person, not email
- Specific reason you're asking THEM
- Ask if they can write a "strong" letter (gives them out if needed)
- Respect their time

### What to Do If They Decline

Teachers who decline are doing you a favor—they're acknowledging they can't write a strong letter.

**Response:**
```
"Thank you for being honest with me. I really appreciate it.
Could you suggest another teacher who might know me better?"
```

## Preparing Your Recommenders

### The "Brag Sheet" or Questionnaire

**Provide to each recommender:**

1. **Resume/Activities List**
   - Comprehensive, updated
   - Highlights particularly important items

2. **Personal Information**
   - Why you're applying to specific schools
   - What you hope to study and why
   - Future goals
   - Personal qualities you want highlighted

3. **Class-Specific Memories**
   - Specific projects, papers, discussions
   - Moments of growth or insight
   - Challenges you faced in the class
   - What you learned beyond content

4. **Anecdotes You Want Included**
   - Specific stories that reveal character
   - Moments they might remember
   - Examples of your engagement

### Sample Brag Sheet Questions

| Question | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| What are you most proud of from this class? | Highlights specific achievement |
| Describe a time you struggled and overcame | Shows resilience |
| What do you want to study in college and why? | Connects class to future |
| What three adjectives describe you as a student? | Focuses their writing |
| What should I know about your circumstances? | Provides context |
| What specific moments from class should I remember? | Provides content |

### The Email After They Agree

```
Subject: Recommendation Letter Materials - [Your Name]

Dear Mr./Ms. [Name],

Thank you so much for agreeing to write my recommendation letter.
I've attached materials to help you:

1. Resume with all activities
2. Brag sheet with background information
3. List of schools with deadlines
4. Specific memories from your class that I hope you'll mention

The earliest deadline is [DATE] for [SCHOOL]. I'll add you to my
Common App portal shortly—you'll receive an email from them.

Please let me know if you need anything else. I really appreciate
your time and support.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
```

## What Makes a Strong Recommendation

### Elements of Excellent Letters

| Element | What It Looks Like |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Specific anecdotes** | "I remember when [student] argued that..." |
| **Comparison to peers** | "In 15 years of teaching, one of the best..." |
| **Intellectual characterization** | "Their approach to problems is..." |
| **Personal qualities** | "Beyond academics, [student] is..." |
| **Growth narrative** | "At the start of the year... by the end..." |
| **Genuine enthusiasm** | Tone conveys real admiration |

### The "Top 5%" Standard

> Most teachers say: "This student is in the top 5% I've taught."
> What admissions wants: "This student is in the top 5% I've taught BECAUSE..." followed by specific evidence.

### What Hurts: Weak Letter Signals

| Red Flag | What It Suggests |
|----------|------------------|
| Focuses only on grades | Teacher doesn't know student |
| Very short (half page or less) | Teacher didn't invest time |
| Generic praise | Could apply to anyone |
| Damning with faint praise | "Adequate work ethic" |
| Contradicts other application parts | Inconsistency = concern |
| No specific examples | Lack of relationship |
| Template language | Mass-produced letter |

## Managing the Process

### Tracking System

| School | Teacher 1 | Teacher 2 | Counselor | Additional | Deadline |
|--------|-----------|-----------|-----------|------------|----------|
| MIT | Requested ✓ | Requested ✓ | Requested ✓ | — | 11/1 |
| Stanford | Submitted ✓ | Submitted ✓ | Submitted ✓ | — | 11/1 |
| Duke | Pending | Pending | Pending | Coach | 1/4 |

### Following Up (Tactfully)

**Two weeks before deadline:**
```
"Hi Mr./Ms. [Name], I wanted to check in about my recommendation letter.
The deadline for [School] is [Date]. Please let me know if you need any
additional information from me. Thank you!"
```

**If not submitted close to deadline:**
```
"I noticed that my recommendation for [School] hasn't been submitted
yet—the deadline is [Date]. Is there anything I can do to help? I want
to make sure everything is in order. Thank you!"
```

### What If Something Goes Wrong

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Teacher forgot | Gentle reminder, offer to resend materials |
| Deadline passed | Contact admissions, most accommodate briefly |
| Teacher can't do it anymore | Ask backup teacher immediately |
| Suspect weak letter | Too late to change; focus on other components |

## Special Situations

### Home-Schooled Students

**Challenge:** No traditional teachers

**Solutions:**
- Outside instructors (co-op, tutors, community college)
- Mentors from activities
- Employers
- Parent letter (some schools accept/expect this)

### International Students

**Considerations:**
- Non-English speaking recommenders: translation requirements
- Different educational systems: explain context
- Teacher titles may differ: specify relationship clearly

### Transfer Students

**Who to ask:**
- College professors (ideal)
- Academic advisor
- Graduate student instructors (if substantive relationship)

### Non-Traditional Recommenders

**When appropriate:**
- Long-term mentor relationship
- Significant project collaboration
- Can speak to qualities teachers can't

## After the Process

### Thank You Notes (Required)

**Timing:** Within one week of decisions, regardless of outcome

**Format:**
```
Dear Mr./Ms. [Name],

I wanted to thank you for writing my recommendation letter. I was
[accepted to/waitlisted at/not admitted to] [School]. Regardless of
the outcome, I'm grateful for your support throughout this process.

Your class taught me [specific thing], and I'll carry that with me
to [wherever you're going]. Thank you for investing in me.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
```

### Updating Recommenders

**If you get great news:** Share it! Teachers love to know their effort paid off.

**If results are mixed:** Still thank them. Their letter wasn't the problem.

## Recommendation Strategy by School Type

### Elite Schools (HYPSM)

- Need EXCEPTIONAL letters (not just good)
- "Best in my career" language helps significantly
- Specific intellectual characterization matters
- Both teachers should be strong

### Selective Schools (T20-30)

- Strong letters expected
- One exceptional + one solid is OK
- Context letters (counselor) very important
- Additional rec from mentor can help

### Target Schools

- Solid letters are sufficient
- Focus on match and enthusiasm
- Counselor context helpful for yield protection
