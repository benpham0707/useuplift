## Overview

Community service evaluation has shifted dramatically. International voluntourism is now a red flag, while sustained local commitment is highly valued.

### Key Takeaways

- **International trips = Red flag:** Expensive mission trips "reek of privilege"
- **70% prefer single cause:** Officers prefer sustained commitment to ONE cause
- **Local > International:** "Act locally, think globally"
- **Doing With not For:** Partnership over charity; genuine understanding
- **53% tiebreaker:** Service is critical differentiator when academics equal

> "Expensive mission trips to exotic locales WORK AGAINST applicants at elite institutions, not for them... such activities reek of privilege."
> — Ivy Coach

## Revolutionary Findings

### 1. International Service Trips = Red Flag

> "Expensive mission trips to exotic locales WORK AGAINST applicants at elite institutions, not for them...such activities reek of privilege."
> — Ivy Coach

- $5,000-$10,000+ trips reflect family WEALTH, not student initiative
- "Colleges will turn you down if you write about how the mission trip changed your life"
- EXCEPTION: Self-funded, self-organized international work (e.g., Eagle Scout project)

### 2. 70% Prefer Sustained Single Cause

- DoSomething survey of 264 admissions leaders
- Majority recommend focusing on **ONE service commitment** over high school years
- Directly contradicts "well-rounded" misconception

### 3. Local > International

- MIT Dean Schmill: emphasizes "mindset of service" at LOCAL level
- Strategic recommendation: "Act locally, think globally"
- Weekly literacy center work > one-week international orphanage

## Harvard's Turning the Tide Framework

### The Three Core Dimensions

| Dimension | Description | Key Question |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Authenticity of Choice** | Volunteering because they genuinely care, not for applications | Why did student choose THIS cause? |
| **Consistency of Engagement** | Sustained, not episodic involvement | Multi-year commitment to same cause? |
| **Capacity for Reflection** | Structured opportunity for reflection | Can student articulate learning? |

**KEY INSIGHT:** This framework directly contradicts the "activity count maximization" strategy most students pursue.

## Doing With vs Doing For

### Harvard Framework

> Service should focus on **"doing with" rather than "doing for"** — genuine partnership and understanding of community needs rather than top-down charity.

| "Doing For" (Weak) | "Doing With" (Strong) |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| Fly in, help, leave | Relationship building over time |
| Savior complex | Mutual benefit and learning |
| Distance from community | Genuine understanding of needs |
| International trips | Local sustained commitment |
| Pre-packaged "experience" | Partnership with community |

## Service Connected to Personal Experience

### Why Personal Connection Matters

Service connected to student's identity demonstrates deeper motivation:

- Student from immigrant family → immigrant mutual aid organization
- Student whose sibling has autism → disability advocacy nonprofit
- Student whose community faced housing crisis → housing justice work

These choices reveal:
- **Insider knowledge** of the issue
- **Sustained motivation** grounded in lived experience
- **Authentic connection** that will persist

### vs. Detached Volunteerism

Selecting service from a menu of "impressive-sounding" options lacks reflection, motivation, and character indicators.

### Strength-of-Character Indicators

- Demonstrating humility about positionality
- Willingness to learn from community partners
- Articulating how work challenged previous assumptions

## Duration and Depth Expectations

### Meaningful Duration: Minimum 2-3 Years

**DoSomething Survey:**
- 70% prefer sustained commitment over multiple years
- Majority recommend single service commitment

**Stanford Admissions:**
> "Depth, not breadth, of experience is most meaningful to the admissions committee."

### Depth Dimension — After Initial Involvement

| Indicator | Strong Signal | Weak Signal |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| **Leadership** | Takes on leadership roles | Just shows up |
| **Initiative** | Develops new programs | Follows assigned tasks |
| **Mentorship** | Mentors newer volunteers | Solo participant |
| **Problem-Solving** | Addresses community needs | Generic "help" |
| **Impact** | Documents specific outcomes | Vague "making a difference" |

### Example of Deep Engagement

Student at homeless services organization for 3 years who can:
- Articulate specific policies limiting housing access
- Name particular residents' stories
- Explain how volunteer role connects to systemic change

...demonstrates far deeper engagement than student without understanding of root causes.

## Detecting Service for Apps vs Genuine Commitment

### Primary Red Flag: INCONSISTENCY

| Inconsistency Type | Example | Signal |
|--------------------|---------|--------|
| **Essay-Activity Mismatch** | Emphasizes environmental justice but no environmental ECs | Strategic positioning |
| **Recommendation Gap** | Claims deep service but no teacher mentions it | Exaggeration |
| **Social Media Contradiction** | Service narrative but partying content | Inauthenticity |
| **Timeline Implausibility** | Founded nonprofit + varsity + 4.0 + multiple jobs | Fabrication risk |

### Verification Mechanisms

Officers may:
1. **Call school counselors** to verify time/commitment to stated activities
2. **Contact organization leaders** — even unnamed ones
3. **Review social media** for consistency with stated interests
4. **Check organizational websites and public records** for major claims

**Jim Jump (Admissions Professional):**
> Colleges seek "authenticity rather than heroism — evidence of what students actually did, not what they wish they had done."

## Service as Character Tiebreaker

### IESD Survey Finding

- **53% of officers at private four-year colleges** rate community service as important decision factor
- At institutions where 95%+ have strong academics, character becomes paramount

### The Four-Dimension Character Framework (Through Service)

| Dimension | What It Assesses |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Integrity** | Does service narrative align across essays, activities, recommendations? |
| **Initiative** | Did student create/improve service, or just show up? |
| **Growth** | Can student articulate what they learned? |
| **Collective Orientation** | Genuine concern for others or primarily self-interest? |

## School-Specific Information

### Harvard

- "Turning the Tide" initiative emphasizes authenticity, consistency, reflection
- "Doing with not for" philosophy
- Values family contributions equally with traditional service

### MIT

- Dean Schmill emphasizes "mindset of service" at local level
- Essay prompt: "How have you improved the lives of others in your community?"
- Values impact over prestige

### Stanford

- "Depth, not breadth" philosophy
- Values sustained commitment over multiple high-profile experiences
- Context matters: service evaluated relative to opportunity

### Yale

- Values authentic engagement
- Seeks curation over accumulation
- Service should connect to broader interests
