# Prompt A2: Portfolio Evaluation Standards

**Phase 1: Admissions Perspective**
**Focus:** How elite colleges evaluate activities, recommendations, character, and conduct holistic review

Copy everything below the line into Perplexity Pro with Deep Research mode.

---

I'm building a college advising system and need to understand how the top 30 US universities (Ivy League, Stanford, MIT, top privates, top publics) evaluate student portfolios. Focus on extracurricular activities, recommendations, character assessment, red flags, and the holistic review process.

**Cite specific sources: admissions officers by name, published guides, research studies, Common Data Sets, institutional documentation. Use high quality, credible sources only. Include direct quotes where available.**

---

## SECTION 1: Extracurricular Activity Evaluation

How do admissions officers at elite schools evaluate extracurricular activities?

### 1.1 Quality vs. Quantity Framework
- Is there an ideal number of activities? What have admissions officers specifically said about this?
- What's considered "too few" activities? At what point does a list seem thin?
- What's considered "too many" or "padding"? How do officers describe resume-stuffing?
- How do officers distinguish genuine involvement from activities done just for college apps?
- What specific signals indicate DEPTH vs. superficial participation?
- What specific signals indicate BREADTH that adds value vs. scattered unfocused involvement?

### 1.2 Time Commitment Standards
- How many hours per week signals "serious commitment" to admissions officers?
- What's the threshold for "casual participation" that doesn't impress?
- How is consistency (years of involvement) weighted vs. intensity (hours per week)?
- How do officers view summer-only activities vs. year-round commitment?
- What do they say about activities started in junior or senior year?

### 1.3 Leadership Evaluation
- What types of leadership actually impress admissions officers beyond holding a title?
- How do officers evaluate "founded an organization" - what makes this credible vs. suspicious?
- How do they evaluate "led an existing organization" - what distinguishes good leadership here?
- What evidence of leadership IMPACT do they look for? How is impact demonstrated?
- When does individual excellence (awards, achievements) matter more than leadership roles?
- How do they view "president of 5 clubs" vs. "deeply committed to 2 activities"?
- What do officers say about leadership in small organizations vs. large ones?

### 1.4 The "Spike" Concept
- How universally do elite schools value a focused "spike" vs. well-rounded profile?
- What specific quotes exist from admissions officers about spikes vs. well-rounded?
- What makes a spike COMPELLING vs. just narrow or one-dimensional?
- Can applicants have multiple spikes, or does that defeat the purpose?
- How do admissions officers describe the ideal balance of depth and breadth?
- Has the spike concept changed in recent admissions cycles?

### 1.5 Impact and Achievement Assessment
- How do officers assess "impact" claims in activity descriptions?
- What's the difference in how they view:
  - School-level impact
  - Local community impact
  - State/regional impact
  - National/international impact
- How are activity claims verified or fact-checked by admissions offices?
- What makes impact claims CREDIBLE vs. obviously inflated?
- How do they evaluate quantitative claims ("raised $50,000", "served 500 students", "grew membership 300%")?
- What's the role of the counselor in verifying activity claims?

### 1.6 Context and Circumstances
- How do officers evaluate activities for students who must work paying jobs?
- How are family responsibilities (caregiving, family business, siblings) viewed as activities?
- How do they calibrate expectations for students from under-resourced schools/communities?
- What do they say about paid work vs. unpaid extracurriculars?
- How do geographic limitations (rural, no access to programs) factor in?
- How do they evaluate students who had limited time due to documented circumstances?

### 1.7 Activity Categories and Preferences
- Do certain activity TYPES carry more weight (research, athletics, arts, service, etc.)?
- How do officers view "typical" activities (NHS, student government, sports team member)?
- What makes an activity stand out as UNUSUAL or particularly interesting?
- How do they evaluate passion projects or self-directed initiatives?
- What's the view on online activities, virtual organizations, or remote involvement?

## SECTION 2: Recommendations - What Officers Actually Learn

### 2.1 Teacher Recommendations
- What do elite schools want to learn from teacher recommendations that they can't learn elsewhere?
- What specific phrases or descriptors carry weight with admissions officers?
- How important is the recommender's prestige/credentials vs. the content of their letter?
- What do officers say about recommendations that are "nice but generic"?
- How do they view superlatives ("best student in 20 years", "top 1%") - helpful or discounted?
- What specific ANECDOTES or EVIDENCE makes recommendations powerful?
- How do they interpret lukewarm, neutral, or "damning with faint praise" recommendations?
- What are the warning signs that a recommendation is actually negative?

### 2.2 Counselor Recommendations
- What context do elite schools want from school counselors?
- How should school context (under-resourced, limited course offerings, etc.) be communicated?
- What do officers want counselors to say about the student specifically?
- How do they view counselor recommendations from large public schools where counselors have 400+ students?
- What role does the School Profile play alongside the counselor recommendation?

### 2.3 Additional/Supplemental Recommendations
- When do additional recommendations HELP vs. HURT an application?
- What types of additional recommenders are most valuable?
- How do officers view recommendations from:
  - Research mentors/supervisors
  - Employers
  - Coaches
  - Arts instructors
  - Religious/community leaders
  - Famous or powerful people who don't know the student well
- What's the guidance on peer recommendations (for schools that accept them like Dartmouth)?
- How many additional recommendations is too many?

## SECTION 3: Character and Personal Qualities Assessment

How do admissions officers evaluate character and personal qualities?

### 3.1 Intellectual Curiosity
- How is intellectual curiosity demonstrated in applications?
- What EVIDENCE do officers look for beyond "says they love learning"?
- How do they distinguish genuine curiosity from performed/fake interest?
- What activities or behaviors signal authentic intellectual engagement?
- How do they evaluate intellectual risk-taking vs. playing it safe?

### 3.2 Resilience and Grit
- What evidence of resilience do admissions officers find convincing?
- How do they evaluate adversity claims - what COUNTS vs. what seems exaggerated?
- What's the difference between adversity that's meaningful vs. privileged complaints?
- How do they view failure and recovery vs. consistent success?
- What do officers say about students who haven't faced adversity?

### 3.3 Integrity and Ethics
- How do officers assess integrity through applications?
- What signals honesty and authenticity vs. exaggeration and posturing?
- How do they evaluate consistency across application components?
- What integrity concerns do they look for?

### 3.4 Community Contribution and Service
- What counts as meaningful community contribution?
- How do they evaluate local service vs. international service trips?
- What distinguishes "service for college apps" from genuine commitment?
- How do they view service that connects to personal experience vs. detached volunteering?
- What duration and depth of service is meaningful?

### 3.5 Leadership Potential (Beyond Titles)
- Beyond titles, what signals leadership potential?
- How do they evaluate leadership in non-traditional contexts?
- What do they say about introverted leaders vs. extroverted leaders?
- How do they assess ability to influence and inspire others?

### 3.6 Self-Awareness and Maturity
- How does maturity appear in strong applications?
- What level of self-awareness impresses officers?
- How do they evaluate emotional intelligence?
- What signals that a student is ready for college independence?

### 3.7 "Fit" and Campus Contribution
- How do officers assess whether a student will contribute positively to campus?
- What do they mean when they talk about "building a class"?
- How do they evaluate potential for positive peer influence?
- What "types" of students are they trying to balance in a class?

## SECTION 4: Red Flags - What Hurts Applications

What universally hurts applications at elite schools?

### 4.1 Academic Red Flags
- Beyond low grades, what academic patterns concern officers?
- How do they view grade inconsistency across subjects?
- What course selection patterns raise concerns (avoiding hard classes, dropping rigor)?
- How are academic integrity violations viewed?
- What about unexplained gaps or anomalies in transcripts?

### 4.2 Activity Red Flags
- What activity patterns suggest inauthenticity or resume padding?
- How do they view sudden activity explosion in junior/senior year?
- What inconsistencies between activities and stated interests concern them?
- What signals that activities were done purely for college admissions?
- How do they view "founding" organizations that seem to exist only on paper?

### 4.3 Character/Integrity Red Flags
- How do disciplinary records affect evaluation? What level matters?
- What social media presence issues have officers mentioned?
- How do they handle inconsistencies within applications?
- What do they say about students who seem "too perfect" or inauthentic?
- How do they view excessive self-promotion or arrogance?

### 4.4 Application Component Inconsistencies
- What inconsistencies between application components raise flags?
- How do they verify information across essays, activities, and recommendations?
- What happens when recommendations contradict the student's self-presentation?
- How do they identify exaggeration or misrepresentation?

### 4.5 Demonstrated Interest Red Flags
- At schools that track interest, what behaviors hurt applicants?
- How is "no demonstrated interest" viewed at schools that care?
- What negative signals come from campus visits, interviews, or communications?

## SECTION 5: The Holistic Review Process

How do elite schools actually conduct holistic review?

### 5.1 The Reading Process
- How are applications typically read? (First reader → committee? Multiple independent readers?)
- How much TIME do readers spend on each application? What do studies show?
- What do they look at FIRST? What's the typical order of reviewing materials?
- How do numerical ratings or scores work in the evaluation process?
- What's the role of regional admissions officers vs. senior staff?

### 5.2 Rating and Scoring Systems
- What rating scales do elite schools use (1-6? 1-9? Letter grades?)?
- What dimensions are rated separately (academic, extracurricular, personal)?
- How do ratings translate into admit/deny decisions?
- What role does the "academic index" or similar calculations play?

### 5.3 Committee Decision Making
- How are close/borderline decisions made?
- What tips a "maybe" to "yes" or "no"?
- How does committee discussion actually work?
- Who has the final say on admission decisions?
- How do they handle disagreements between readers?

### 5.4 Institutional Priorities
- How do officers balance individual merit vs. class composition?
- What does "shaping a class" actually mean in practice?
- How do they handle trade-offs between qualified candidates?
- What institutional needs affect individual decisions (geographic diversity, major distribution, etc.)?
- How do legacy, development cases, and athletic recruits factor into holistic review?

### 5.5 The "It Factor" and Advocacy
- What do officers describe as qualities that make someone stand out?
- How do they talk about memorable applications?
- What makes an admissions officer advocate strongly for a candidate?
- What's the difference between a "good" application and one that excites readers?

### 5.6 Yield and Waitlist Considerations
- How does predicted yield affect admissions decisions?
- How do they decide who goes on the waitlist vs. reject?
- What factors move someone from waitlist to admit?
