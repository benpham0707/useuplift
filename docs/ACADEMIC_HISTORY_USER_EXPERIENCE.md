## Decision Framework: Should I Take This AP?

Before choosing any AP course, run through these questions:

### 1. What grade do I realistically expect to get in this AP course?
- A or A-: **Go for it**
- B+ or B: **Proceed with caution** — only if it's relevant to your major or a clear strength
- Below B: **Probably not worth the risk**

### 2. What grade would I get in the honors or regular version?
- If the difference is 0.5+ grade points: **Consider the lower level**
- If the difference is <0.3 grade points: **Take the AP** — your GPA can handle it

### 3. Is this a subject where I'm strong or struggling?
- **Strong:** Take the AP
- **Neutral:** Consider based on workload
- **Struggling:** Protect your GPA here

### 4. How many other challenging courses am I taking?
- Already at 4-5 APs? Adding another has diminishing returns and increasing risk.

### 5. Is this subject relevant to my intended major or interests?
- **Yes:** Lean toward the AP even if it's harder
- **No:** Only take the AP if you can maintain an A-

### Your Specific Guidance

Given your track record, if you expect at least a B+ in the AP version AND it's a strength subject, go for it. Your grades typically hold up under challenge.

## Course Correction Guidance

### Warning Signs to Watch For

- Grade dropping below B in any course for more than 2 weeks
- Feeling consistently overwhelmed or anxious about workload
- Regularly pulling all-nighters to keep up
- Falling behind in multiple classes simultaneously
- Physical symptoms: insomnia, loss of appetite, frequent illness
- Losing interest in extracurriculars you used to enjoy

### What to Do When Warning Signs Appear

| Signal | Response | Timeline |
|--------|----------|----------|
| One grade dropping | Talk to the teacher immediately. Get tutoring. Dedicate extra study time. | Address within 1 week |
| Multiple grades dropping | Meet with your counselor. Consider dropping one level in your weakest non-essential course. | Address within 2 weeks |
| Consistent overwhelm | Schedule audit needed. Something has to give. | Address immediately |
| Physical/mental symptoms | Your health is more important than any grade. Talk to parents and counselor. | Address today |

### Proactive Monitoring

Check your grades weekly, not at the end of the semester. Set a recurring reminder every Friday to review where you stand. Small problems addressed early don't become big problems later.

### The Normalization Message

Adjusting your schedule mid-year is not failure — it's wisdom. Many successful students have dropped from AP to honors when they recognized the fit wasn't right. Admissions officers would rather see strong grades in appropriate courses than a transcript that shows you drowning.

Making smart adjustments is a sign of maturity and self-awareness.
